I think it is very clear to all but the most partisan and politically near-sighted that, in terms of Campaign Management, Barack Obama has been as outstanding as John McCain has been a total failure.

And if the Presidential Campaign Trail is as good a test and demonstration of a candidate's ability to manage the pressures and challenges of being President as anything, then it is obviously apparent that Obama is as supremely qualified as McCain is hopelessly unqualified for the job.

What this Campaign and tomorrow's Election are really about though, is the dawning realisation on the part of the American Electorate, of just how disastrous and damaging the Bush Administration has been for the United States; and how a McCain Administration would be nothing more than an horrific extension of the same course.

This much at least has been acknowledged by the McCain Campaign, which has had to focus at least as much on running [b]*AGAINST THE BUSH ADMINISTRATION*[/b], as against the Obama Candidacy.

However, this has meant that Senator Obama hasn't really had to represent or promise any real change for U.S. Government policies and actions; but has merely campaigned on a change of political personality, without proposing any new direction for the U.S.

But Obama daren't have done otherwise. Any suggestion that an Obama Administration would follow any radically alternative Domestic or Foreign Policy, would have been relentlessly hammered by the McCain Campaign.

So maybe there is still hope...

I [b]*DO*[/b] think that if Obama is successful tomorrow, that success will represent a massive desire on the part of Americans to change the direction that their nation has been travelling in for the generation since Reagan.

I think that a more important outcome than an Obama victory tomorrow, will be determined by how well the Republican Party does in its battle for Senators and Representatives. I think that if the GOP is really hammered in Congressional races, then that might prove to be a mortal blow for the U.S. Republican Party. And I think that would be a good thing because it would herald a shake-up of the whole U.S. Political System.

The GOP isn't so much a single political party these days, as it is a number of different factions, clinging together for mutual security. I think the Neo-Conservative faction will take the full force of the blame for any major meltdown, and this could (and should) herald the splintering of the Republican Party, into more honestly ideological groups.

These ex-Republican groups would then attract ideological compatriots from the DNC, and hey-presto! The U.S. would finally have a multi-party Democracy.

And [b]*THAT*[/b], would be a [b]*VERY GOOD THING*[/b].
