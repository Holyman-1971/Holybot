Here is an interesting and readable article of "Purpose Driven Organisations":

[url= https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/purpose-driven-firms]"What Is a Purpose-Drive Firm?", Kate Gibson, Harvard Business School Online[/url]

Implementing a Purpose-Driven ethos in the cultural fabric of organisations is something I have some experience of.

The ultimate objective is to define a Purpose that everyone within the organisation, its customers and suppliers, can fully support.

Making money has been the more or less ubiquitous "purpose" defined and agreed by a majority of organisations. This is understandable, given the ideological and economic climates these organisations were operating in.

However, in recent years, thanks in no small part to the Economic Apocalypse that was triggered in 2008, simply "making money" is no longer a "purpose" that a majority of human beings can get behind. Not solely "making money", at least.

As a unifying "Purpose" for a commercial organisation, "making money" doesn't work. A majority of human beings just do not find "making money" a fulfilling way to spend their time.

And unfulfilled employees and suppliers are unreliable. They have even been known to become rebellious. Which is a headache you can do without when you are attempting to control a large organisation (hence Senior Executives' anathema towards Unions).

However, if your organisation has a clearly defined and stated Purpose, then those who support that Purpose will be attracted to it. They will have a clear understanding of what you are attempting to do with your organisation and can make a truly informed decision about cooperating with you. Everyone in your Purpose-driven organisation will function with a high degree of autonomy and personal responsibility, because their individual decisions will fully align with your organisation's Purpose.

For 3 years, I worked with the second-largest bank in the Netherlands, Rabobank. Rabobank's implication and participation in the [url= https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libor_scandal]2012 LIBOR Scandal[/url], caused it to receive a one billion Euro fine from Europe's Central Bank regulators. This generated a significant scandal in the Netherlands, and Rabobank's shareholders demanded a clear and resolute change to the workplace culture within their bank. It was made very clear that any subsequent scandal would result in the shareholders collectively selling their shares to the Dutch Government, effectively nationalising the bank.

Rabobank's response was to become a Purpose-Driven Organisation. Where for the previous 3 centuries of its existence in one form or another, Rabobank existed to make money for its shareholders and clients, from 2015, the bank's stated Purpose has been to [url= https://www.rabobank.com/about-us/in-short/mission]"Grow a Better World Together"[/url] [i](link to: Rabobank's website)[/i].

Rabobank provides banking services to 65% of the World's producers of food for human beings. It has decided to use all of the data it gathers through its day-to-day business operations, to figure out how to feed every human being on the Planet, sustainably, forever.

One of the key properties of the definition of a Purpose-Driven Organisation's Purpose, is that it can (and arguably should) seem very, very challenging. It needs to be ambitious, and an ambition that can be clearly and Truthfully branded.

So what if the Purpose is never achieved?

That doesn't matter. In fact, it will be a pain-in-the-arse when it is achieved, because a new, equally-effective Purpose will need to be defined.

The purpose of the Purpose is to align Everyone's activities and energies. Let Everyone get on with doing what their subject matter expertise means they know how to do better than anyone else. They know where their Organisation's True North is. As long as their output remains aligned with the Organisation's Purpose, they can function autonomously.

Take personal responsibility for their own failures and successes; and not waste their or anyone else's Time by bickering and bitching about others.

I can report that the 3 years I spent working with Rabobank where the most productive and positive of my entire career. I really enjoyed working with Everyone their, because we were all driven by and towards the same Purpose.

I hit my 3-year term limit as a contractor, and sadly had to decline the offer of a permanent role, due to my personal circumstances. But my 3 years working for a Purpose-Driven Organisation were both profound and highly-fulfilling.

I do feel that if Humanity can become a Purpose-Driven Organisation, initially serving the Purpose of saving the Planet Earth's Environment, it can pull itself out of its current nose-dive, and show itself what it is capable of achieving, when it cooperates.
