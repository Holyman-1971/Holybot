I have a very old and very good friend who is a WO1 in the British Army Intelligence Corps, and we regularly discuss the merits of the Iraq and Afghanistan "Wars".

He acknowledges that the general feeling in the British Army is that Tony Blair "broke the covenant" between the government and the military, when he hitched the UK to Bush's illegal and counter-productive GWOT bandwagon.

But he still feels that the efforts and sacrifices that the individual service personnel have made in Afghanistan, still has validity on a humanitarian basis.

From these discussions, I came up with a proposal that I'll probably include in my campaign manifesto for my run at becoming Emperor of the Universe.

It is simply this:

When an individual joins their nation's military force, they usually do so with the (somewhat naïve) belief that if they are called upon to actually fight, it will be in defence of their own nation, their friends and family.

Fact of the matter is that by and large, the G7 nations - particularly the U.S. and U.K. - are never seriously going to be threatened by an external, comparable military force. So the chances of a soldier, sailor, marine or pilot ever having to actually fight in defence of their own nation are pretty limited.

Is it then appropriate for a government to order its military personnel to engage in conflict thousands of miles away from their home nation, in "defence" of purely political (and usually economic) objectives?

Morally, the answer must clearly be no, it is not appropriate or acceptable.

So, my answer to this particular conundrum is this:

When an individual joins the Armed Services, they are required to abrogate certain of their civilian rights, and conform to a Uniform Code of Military Justice. This is something they do voluntarily, as a matter of personal choice.

So I think that it is also appropriate that when someone joins a nation's military, that they be given the choice to elect to fight [b]*SOLELY*[/b] in their Nation's defence; or, signal a willingness to fight in non-National Defensive conflicts as well.

The United States has no legal mandate to police the World.

The United Nations (the creation of the U.S., btw) [b]*DOES*[/b] have a mandate to enforce international law; and it can (when permitted by competing nationalist polities) organise and institute an armed, United Nations Peacekeeping Force to act legally, as an international Police Force.

So, when someone joins their nation's military, they should have the facility to indicate whether they opt to serve [b]*ONLY*[/b] in their nation's defence; or whether they would be [b]*ALSO*[/b] willing to wear the blue helmet of a United Nations military police force.

[b]*ONLY*[/b] the United Nations has a clear and legal mandate to be enforcing international law on this Planet of ours.

Clearly, as presently constituted, the United Nations is a flawed bureaucracy, dominated as it is by the diplomatic squabbling between the five permanent members of the Security Council. But just because the structure of the U.N. is flawed, does not mean that the [b]*IDEAL*[/b] of the United Nations is not still sound. Indeed, I would venture that the idea of the U.N. is more vital and necessary now, than it was at the end of World War Two.

Individuals who join their Nation's military to defend their country, their friends and their family should [b]*NOT*[/b] be subject to the whims and connivances of the civilian politicians who would use them - [b]*SACRIFICE THEM*[/b] - to pursue political and diplomatic objectives that have nothing to do with the defence of the Nation to which the military and politicians both belong.

Only an idiot could not see the sense and Truth in this.
