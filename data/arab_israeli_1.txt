Aaaaaaaanyway... The most important thing to understand is that the Arab-Israeli Conflict can not be treated as a Religious-Historical Conflict, by the Rest of the World.

Whilst we can and should respect the multifarious religions that underscore the Jewish, Islamic, Christian, Druze and Zoroastrian cultures in the region, is is impossible for civilised democracy to be anything other than secular.

And remember, there's an important distinction between secularism and atheism. No-one is saying that all these different religions, sects and cults can't practice whatever religion or spirituality they want to; it's just that they can not be allowed to use their religious beliefs to justify killing others who believe differently.

Doesn't matter what religion they are, we in the dominant, democratic and secular World have a responsibility to World Peace and Harmony to mediate and mitigate the Conflict.

The "Problem" in the Arab-Israeli Conflict is not that Jewish people were given a huge chunk of Palestinian Land. Though to suggest that all the Palestinians who evidently lived there before the creation of the State of Israel have no legitimate claim to the land they and their families had inhabited through more than 1300 years of first Arabian Caliphate and then Turkish Ottoman Rule, is as disingeneous as it is wilfully callous.

The campaign to create a modern state of Israel in the British Palestinian Mandate arguably kicked off with the Balfour Declaration of 1917. Though it took a further 40 years, Zionist terrorism, the collapse of the British Empire, the Holocaust, the United Nations, and instigation from the United States to finally create a wholly new and sovereign nation-state.

Arguably then, it can be said that the modern State of Israel was created in former Ottoman Turkish territory, rather than independent Palestinian land. Territory that became "ownerless" with the defeat and collapse of the Ottoman Empire at the end of World War One.

And such is the burden of all downtrodden people who live under the yoke of imperialism, foreign or domestic, that they are forever pawns in the Great Game of Empire.

No, the chief "Problem" in the Arab-Israeli Conflict is that the United Nations of Earth have never followed through on the committment it made, when it first created a new sovereign territory on Earth.

In the first instance, up to 1967, the United Nations - and specifically the United States - failed to provide the new Jewish State with the protection it undoubtedly required from the envious, jealous and ideologically opposed Arab nations that surrounded Israel.

As President Nasser of Egypt ramped up his Pan-Arab Nationalism rhetoric in the 1950's and 1960's, the UN, and particularly the dominant power, the US, preferred to leave well alone.

Indeed, when Israel, along with France and the UK declared war on Nasser in 1956, ostensibly for his nationalising the Suez Canal, it was the United States that forced Israel, France and the UK to stand down.

As June 4th, 1967 approached, as far as the Rest of the World was concerned, Israel was becoming a failed experiment in Nation-creation, which was about to be terminated by the impending Arab coup de grace.

But then, shock of shocks, in the Six-Day War, Israel managed to completely and utterly destroy the offensive capacity of ALL it's Arab neighbours, and expand its territory at the same time.

And here we see the second failing of the International Community.

Having asserted itself violently, having shown that it was a dominant, permanent force to be reckoned with, Israel became subject to UN Resolution 242.

 The Security Council,
Expressing its continuing concern with the grave situation in the Middle East,
Emphasizing the inadmissibility of the acquisition of territory by war and the need to work for a just and lasting peace in which every State in the area can live in security,
Emphasizing further that all Member States in their acceptance of the Charter of the United Nations have undertaken a commitment to act in accordance with Article 2 of the Charter,

Withdrawal of Israeli armed forces from territories occupied in the recent conflict;

For achieving a just settlement of the refugee problem;
For guaranteeing the territorial inviolability and political independence of every State in the area, through measures including the establishment of demilitarized zones;

And where the International Community failed in 1967, and where it continues to fail today, is in the enforcement of that Resolution. Not only does it maintain the Conflict to this day, but it also de-legitimises subsequent UN resolutions calling for similar measures in similar conflicts.
