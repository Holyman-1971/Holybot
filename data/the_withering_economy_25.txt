I suppose the first thing to understand is that there is no classically agreed definition of Capitalism. Economists and Sociologists tend not to use the term, because it is so ambiguous and ill-defined.

My immediate reaction to reading what you wrote above was to think, well, I can't think of any other Economic system that [i]has[/i] been practiced by man. Capitalism is virtually a synonym of Economics.

Capitalism as an Economic [b]AND[/b] Social System is arguably different to say Feudalism, where money wasn't too much of an issue, because if you wanted something you couldn't afford to buy, you just hit someone over the head until they gave it to you. The only real issue for a monarch was raising an army with which to hit another monarch over the head with. But with Feudalism, you generally required that the tenants on your land would serve in your army whenever you desired them to do so (usually in the spring season, after the winter and before the harvest).

Capital is certainly an established synonym for money, so one could almost say that Capitalism is the same as Money-ism (not monetarism, that's something else). If you're talking about a system that involves money, then you're talking about a system that involves capital. Is that not then Capitalism?

I think we could probably agree that the colloquial and generally accepted definition of Capitalism, is an Economic System where production factors (the "Means of Production") are privately owned, labour, goods and capital are traded in markets, and profits distributed to owners and investors.

This allows us to differentiate Capitalism from Feudalism, where everything, including labour, belongs to the monarch; and Communism, where the means of production and any profit belongs to the State.

Arguably though, those are just differentiations of ownership and control, but without any fundamental systemic differences with the way that capital (money) exists and flows.

We can't really call a communitarian barter arrangement an economic system, because if no-one is measuring or differentiating rates of exchange, there is no material Economy. And if there [i]are[/i] measures of exchange  - one barn built equals four bushels of wheat for example - then there [i]is[/i] an Economic flow, it is just not measured in a currency that is recognised outside of the community.

Wikipedia's "History of Capitalism" - http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_capitalism - tells me that the first "thoroughly capitalist country" was the Netherlands in the 17th Century.

[quote][i]In early modern Europe, it featured the wealthiest trading city (Amsterdam) and the first full-time stock exchange. The inventiveness of the traders led to insurance and retirement funds, as well as such less benign phenomena as the boom-bust cycle, the world's first asset-inflation bubble (the "Tulip Mania" of 1636 - 1637), and according to Murray Sayle, the World's first bear raider, Isaac le Maire, who forced prices down by dumping stock and then buying it back at a discount.[/i][/quote]

If we take that as a good as any definition of what we understand to be Capitalism, then we see that Capitalism is characterised by inherent (and apparently immediate) instability, and a vulnerability to manipulation.

I don't know that characterises Capitalism as stable and sustainable.

I mentioned Communism in one of my previous posts, and derided it because I feel it negates an essential component of the Human Social Dynamic: the role of the Individual. So I won't go back over that, except to say that I think Communism is just a different iteration of Capitalism, which uses the same basic systems and structures, but with an emphasis on massively (and destructively) centralised command and control.

So apart from Capitalism - and Communism, if you want to split hairs - I'm not really aware of any alternatives to Capitalism that have been tried.

But I don't think that is too much of a problem.

I'm taking the view of a Systems Designer when I look at the flaws of Capitalism in its current form, and what I see is not anything fundamentally wrong with the basic principle of Capitalism: I just think it needs upgrading.

So instead of trying to build from scratch a completely and evidently different system to Capitalism, I think what we should be looking at, as a Society, is trying to design, scope, implement and develop..:

Capitalism 2.0

I think Capitalism 1.0 is flawed with a lot of bugs; it's at least 400 years old as piece of Socio-Economic "Software"; and I think technology, communications and the intellectual emancipation of Society now requires that it be upgraded.
