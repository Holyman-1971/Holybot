Clearly, Primary/Extractive Industry remains to this day, the most physically and financially risky of all civilian occupations. But I'm not a Marxist, so I'm not going to blame the Owners, and obviously I'm not going to blame the Workers. I'm not even going to blame the commodity speculators, who are more responsible for driving up the price of oil these days than any oil company or national government.

No, at the level of my Soul, I am a Systems Architect, so I blame: The System.

I *DO* think that oil is the anthropological equivalent of heroin. I *DO* think Humanity needs to ween itself off its century-old addiction to the Black Stuff. And I *DO* think that Human Society's addiction to oil is so serious that it is impractical and dangerous for the World's finite reserves of oil to be subject to either private or national ownership and control.

It needs to be held in common trust by Global Society as a whole. I think far more attention needs to be paid to reducing consumption of the stuff, rather than continuing the obsession with how much of it can be produced. And I think that like Water, it is inappropriate and impractical for the control and supply of oil to continue to be subject to profit-making activities.

The Whole Human System of oil extraction and refining should be [b]*INTER*[/b]-nationalised.

And actually, I would feel that the most appropriate organisations to be tasked with such a vital Human Programme, are those organisations we have come to know and loathe as "Big Oil": the ones with all the knowledge and expertise.

But having once removed the profit element from the supply and distribution of oil, Humanity needs to stop demonising the producers of the stuff, and focus instead on reducing consumption.

It is easy to rage against the Oil Machine... Much, much easier than it is to reduce the amount of oil we each consume ourselves.

And that's the Real Problem.
