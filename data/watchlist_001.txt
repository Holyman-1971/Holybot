I finally got around to watching [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt17279496/]"Civil War"[/url].

Umm... Found it a bit meh, to be honest.

Almost nothing (deliberately, as I understand) about why Texas and California have joined forces to secede from the Union. Several clashes between the opposing military forces, and several "encounters" during what is basically just a Road Movie through a United States that is going through a civil war.

The two casting draws for me were Wagner Moura (played Pablo Escobar in "Narcos") and Nick Offerman (played Ron Swanson in "Parks & Recreation"). But Offerman gets very little screen time (< 5 minutes) and isn't allowed to even hint at his Duke Silver [i]alter ego[/i]. And whilst Wagner Moura plays a more central role, with much more screentime than Offerman, given his performance in "Narcos", his acting abilities are criminally under-used in this film.

I stuck with the film all the way through, and it has some good action sequences and a few jump-scares, but as the credits rolled, I was very much thinking that I was glad it was over.

A lot of effort went into this movie, and the participation of Wagner Moura and Nick Offerman requires that I give it 3/5. But not really the movie I was hoping for.

Another film I've watched recently that was definitely shit (and to be fair, this time around I was expecting it to be shit), is [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt5672290/]"The First Omen"[/url].

I'm a fan of the original "Omen" trilogy. The first move scared the bejesus out of me when I first watched it at age 12. I enjoyed the two sequels as well. First memory of Sam Neill is him playing the Antichrist in [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0082377/](Omen III) "The Final Conflict"[/url].

But this new film, "The First Omen", is just a shit cash-in on the "Omen" brand. It is just about the order of satanic nuns that provided natal care and midwifery to the mother of the original devil-brat in the very first film.

If you're an "Omen"-Completist, then you've probably already watched it (but then, you have probably also already watched the 1991 TV Movie, [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0102585/]"Omen IV: The Awakening"[/url]).

Otherwise: don't bother. It isn't even particularly scary.

Oh, but it does have [url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0408591/]Ralp Ineson[/url] doing a weirdly-unnecessary impression of [url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0873743/]Patrick Troughton's[/url] character from the first film.

Patrick Troughton played the priest in the first film that gets impaled by a lighting-rod falling from the spire of a church. He was also the second actor to play "Doctor Who" after [url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0367156/]William Hartnell[/url] retired from the role.

Ralph Ineson played Chris "Finchy" Finch in the original UK version of Ricky Gervais' and Stephen Merchant's [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0290978/ ]"The Office"[/url]. A very recognizable British character actor ("Peaky Blinders", "Sherlock", "Chernobyl" &c.), he's had a slowly-developing career in movies over the last couple of decades, and he (at the time of going to press) will be playing [url=https://marvel.fandom.com/wiki/Galactus]"Galactus"[/url] in the next volume of MCU films (I'm keen to see Galactus done right, and it will be 95% CGI, so hopefully my memory of Ineson's Patrick Troughton impression won't get in the way...).

But sadly "The First Omen" only gets a 1/5 from me. Sorry.

Okay, turning the corner now:

I was not expecting the film [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt17076046/]"Weird: The Al Yankovic Story"[/url] to be all that good, but actually, it is alright. Funny. Dynamic. Interesting. Worth a watch.

[url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0705356/]Daniel Radcliffe[/url] continues to demonstrate that he is so much more than just Harry Potter. He is the reason I chose to watch a film about someone (Weird Al) who I was aware of in the 1980's.

It is an eclectic film.

It plays around with various events in the 1980's that we all have a vague memory of, for example, Weird Al's lengthy and torrid affair with Madonna Ciccone, as they both rose to fame.

Some great gags in the film, mostly verbal-, some sight-. Kind of film you put on at 04:00, when everyone is back at your gaff and still buzzing.

4/5

I also finally got around to watching [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt15239678/]"Dune: Part Two"[/url].

It's alright, isn't it?

Can totally see why I should have seen it in a proper cinema. But even watching it on my modest home rig, I got what everyone has been going on about.

I've read the source book and have seen the original 1984 David Lynch version a couple of times, and I also managed to get through "...Part One" of Denis Villeneuve's new interpretation. But I don't think I've ever fully understood all of the Lore that runs through Frank Herbert's epic creation. This "...Part Two" film has helped a lot with that. I still don't fully understand it all, but I've got more of a handle on it now.

I know I'm late to the "...Part Two" party, but do watch it if you haven't already.

I'd give it a 5/5 if I'd seen it at a cinema, but in the comfort of my own home, there were a few too many slow moments of exposition, and the whole Bene Gesserit backstory is a bit distracting (though I suspect that it will become the front-and-centre story in "...Part Three").

So just a 4/5 on this occasion I'm afraid (might revise later, after Villeneuve has completed his Dune delivery).

I've rewatched some other classic movies recently, too many to list here, but the one I'm really pleased I revisited is [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0088286/]"Top Secret"[/url].

Starring [url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000174/]Val Kilmer[/url], 2 years before he hit the big time in [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0092099/]"Top Gun"[/url], it is the film written and directed by Jerry Zucker, Jim Abrahams and David Zucker after their success with [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0080339/]"Airplane!"[/url] and its sequel, and before they embarked on their [url=https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0095705/]"Naked Gun"[/url] franchise with [url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000558/]Leslie Nielsen[/url].

"Top Secret" has a more or less continuous back-drop of sight-gags that are always at least as funny as the action and dialogue in the foreground. An outstanding turn from [url=https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0001725/]Omar Sharif[/url] that is far more than a cameo, and has him playing hilariously with his public status in the World at the time.
