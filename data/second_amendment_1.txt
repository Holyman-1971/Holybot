The big problem that the United States has with firearms is that the Genie is very much out of the bottle; and it's going to take more than a redefinition or Amendment of the Constitution to get it back in.

That said, I think American Society as a whole needs to make a symbolic admission that it does have a major problem with firearms. Any attempt to "Put the Genie Back in the Bottle" has to start with such an acknowledgement.

The rate of firearms homicide rates in the U.S., compared to other developed nations, is truly shocking, and does nothing to commend the American "Way of Life".

To an outsider, the most pernicious aspect of America's love affair with personal weapons, is just that: America's [b]*LOVE AFFAIR*[/b] with personal weapons.

Whilst other developed nations like Canada, Finland and Switzerland have comparable gun ownership rates; nowhere outside America do guns enjoy such a status as an extension of the gun-owner's personality.

Pacifist as I am, I really would see major, probably insurmountable problems, with a sudden and blanket ban on private firearms ownership, across the nation.

But I [b]*DO*[/b] think that the American People need to collectively acknowledge that their love affair with weapons, is causing far more problems than it solves. Some kind of symbolic gesture is required for American Society to demonstrate (to itself) that there [b]*IS*[/b] a big, big problem with the quantities of firearms in the country; and particularly the fetishistic attitude that so many Americans have towards personal weapons.

From that symbolic point, the practical and doubtless very lengthy process of cleansing American Society of lethal weaponry could begin.
