Yes, just to clarify:

Most oil-carrying super-tankers - of the sort that crowd the Straits of Hormuz - aren't actually equipped with AEGIS technology...

American warships in the Persian Gulf *might* be safe from the anti-ship missiles - though they're not going to be sleeping very soundly at night - and the US Navy might even be able to deploy enough minesweepers to keep the "Global Chokepoint" that are the Straits of Hormuz relatively clear (though I understand they still haven't finished clearing up all the sea-mines laid by Iran during the Iran-Iraq War yet).

But the key point is... International Oil Prices.

Fact is, the price of crude oil jumped at the weekend as a direct result of the Iranian War Games. Even just one super-tanker being taken out would dramatically increase the insurance premium for the tankers. And of course, despite record profits for the oil companies, they aren't going to bear that cost increase themselves: they'll pass it straight on to the consumers at the pump.

No-one doubts then, that the United States military has the power and facilities to overcome any conventional military force, eventually. Another fact for your contemplation is that where U.S. military expenditure this year will be above $500 billion, Iran's military budget is only around $4.5 billion... Oh, also, Iran's "Air Defences" consist almost entirely of aged Mig fighters, which Saddam Hussein moved to Iran in 1991, for protection from the first U.S. air assault on Iraq.

So what we're really talking about is a marginally more conventional, but still guerilla-like defence from Iran, in the face of an American assault. It'll be just like the "Insurgency" in Iraq, only instead of IED's, it'll be super-fast anti-ship torpedoes, and radar-evading missiles (actually, missiles move that fast that it doesn't really matter if they are detected by radar...).

It is unlikely that given the recent Palestinian and Israeli elections, there is the will or capability for Israel to launch anything other than a nuclear strike against Iran. And of course, were Israel to do that, it would rather undermine the, uh, moral imperative that they and their unequivocal supporters in Washington D.C. claim...

And of course, if you're after natural resources under the ground, the last thing you want to do is go and irradiate all that land for the next 10,000 years...

No, I'm rather enjoying the Condi Rice/Jack Straw roadshow at the moment. And reading between the PR lines, and deciphering the diplomatic language, I really do think that there is no heart or stomach in D.C., London or Tel Aviv for another military adventure in the Middle East.

Best guess: the subject will remain on the same metaphorical table as the possibility of using force to bend the Iranian Mullah's to the will of the West. But as the American mid-terms approach; as Blair is finally turfed out of his Downing Street foxhole; as the Israelis try to patch together a governing coalition from the fragmentation of their recent elections; and as Rice & Straw over-reach their selves in trying to oust the democratically chosen Iraqi Head of State (and give the lie to the whole "Freedom and Democracy" branding exercise)...

Well, I suspect some well crafted diplomatic solution that presents the Chinese and/or Russians as arbiters of Peace in the Middle East will emerge.

And we can all go back to watching television and playing games. At least for the next 6-8 years, which is the period reported by all American Intelligence Agencies as being the earliest that Iran could produce one, functioning nuclear weapon.

And Americans can go back to sleep. :)
