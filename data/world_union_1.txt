Oh, I think it is only a matter of Time Sid.

Look at History, and look at the current state of World Affairs, and it becomes obvious that a functioning, unified Global Society is inevitable. It isn't a question of [b]*IF*[/b] Humanity will ever unite as a single political entity; just a question of when.

Track History first.

The History of Humanity is a History of increasing co-operation and collaboration over wider and wider geographic areas, and across increasingly diverse (and increasingly homogenised) ethnic lines.

I'll pick Britain (the, uh, [b]*UNITED*[/b] Kingdom of...) as just one example of how a Society expands its definition of itself; but I could just as easily use Italy, Germany, Spain, China, Russia or any number of other "Nations".

In theBeginning... Like much of the Rest of the World, the British Isles was populated by disparate tribes, aware of each other, and often at odds with each other. But the cultural identity of each tribe was based on the geographic territory they occupied and tried to control.

This was the case in Stone-age and early Iron-age Britain, right up to the time of the (first successful) Roman Invasion (under Claudius), in the 1st Century C.E. Indeed, it was arguably the disparate nature of British tribes is what enabled the Romans to conquer England. (I won't digress... :))

Following the departure of the Romans, Britain was divided (though more unified, compared to pre-Roman Britain), into many distinct supra-tribal areas.

[img=http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/15/Britain_500_CE.png]

And by only a few centuries later, those areas had coagulated into three or four larger and more distinct areas: Wessex, Mercia, the Danelaw and Bernicia (at this stage, Wales, Scotland and Ireland were as distinct from the rest of the British Isles as Mexico, Canada and Cuba are currently distinct from the U.S. (possibly more so).

And less than a century after Alfred the Great signed the Treaty with the Danish Guthrum, the Vikings had more or less lost their hegemony over North and East England to the Saxons.

Now, zip forward to the end of the 13th Century, and Edward I is doing his damndest to carve out Britain as we know it today, by conquering Powys (Wales) and Scotland. But it wasn't until the 16th and 17th Centuries that the Tudors and Stuarts fully united Britain (without Ireland) as a (nominally) single nation. Ireland gets (more or less added) in the 17th and 18th Centuries...

And finally, in 1800, the British Act of Union finally merges the two kingdoms of Ireland and Great Britain, to create the United Kingdom (apparently parts of Ireland had second thoughts, a century or so later...).

At each stage of the expanded definition of Nation/Country, there were Celts/Jutes/Saxons/Danes/Normans/Welsh/Scottish/English/Irish etc., who opposed the expansions, and cited how such a unification could never work, and would probably never happen. Too much blood had been shed in previous enmities, to ever allow reconciliation between the peoples who would eventually form the United Kingdom.

But it happened anyway.

And also at each stage, those opposed to expansion vigorously defended the existing definition of who they were, even though, psychologically, they were the same type of person who a previous expansion earlier, would have been attacking the prospect of that expansion, even as  they were now defending it.

It was a similar story in France (who remembers Austrasia?), Germany, Italy and just about every other region of the World.

And so it goes.

In the second half of the 20th Century, against the backdrop of the flawed United Nations experiment, we began to see the emergence of Continental identities. The European Union, the Arab League, the African Union, the Mercosur, and the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation.

And in addition to those continental groups, we also witnessed the rise of the G-7/G-8, as a group of internationally co-operating nations.

And that brings us to the phase we are in now.

I don't sit here dreaming of a "Utopia" that is a United World; but I do see the current, continual and accelerating emergence of a [i]de facto[/i] World Nation.

Our "Leaders" already comport themselves as "Inter-national" statesmen, more concerned with "Foreign" Policy that domestic issues. Economics is Global, as are all forms of trade. There is an international consensus on just about everything, and if we can tear our focus away from our obsession with the "leading" G-8 powers, we can notice that the Non-Aligned Movement is quietly and peacefully uniting already more than half the World's nations into a group that is far more functional and less imbalanced than a United Nations that is hobbled by veto-wielders.

And, of course, there is The Internet...

The only obstacle - or rather, retarding influence - is the anachronism that is Nationalism.

But as a Collective Human Consciousness, we are fast approaching the realisation that like all anachronistic concepts, Nationalism is causing more problems than it is solving. And as we see in Georgia, Iraq and Tibet, is a concept largely ignored anyway, by the "Leading" national entities of our World.

Nationalism these days is of relevance only to geo-politicians who use national boundaries to demarcate their power-bases; and to dysfunctional humans (mostly adult males), who lack the psychological facility to self-identify with their own personalities.

So there are those who say that Global Social and Political Union will never happen, just as there were those in Wessex in the 7th Century who could not accept the possibility of a Greater Britain.

And there are those who say that Global Union is a Human Imperative, and one that must be achieved in order to combat Environmental and Existential threats to Humanity.

And then, there are people like me, who can see that Global Human Union is already underway, is completely inevitable, and will happen whether others like the idea or not.
