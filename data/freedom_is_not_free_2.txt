I think that there are two definitions of Freedom worth considering:

Freedom to Do

Freedom to Be

[b]1. Freedom to Do[/b]

When we agree to participate (or continue to participate) in any form of society, we enter into a compact with that society where we exchange the opportunity to enjoy the benefits of that society, with the acceptance of certain restrictions on exactly what we can do.

The overwhelming majority of us don't kill or rape others because to do so would not conform with who we believe ourselves to be as individuals. We don't not kill or rape because there are laws against it: we don't kill or rape because intrinsically, we are not killers or rapists.

When we declare and enact laws forbidding (laws forbid, they don't prevent) killing and raping, we do so and support doing so because it is a declaration of who we believe ourselves to be, individually and collectively.

However, after the obvious prohibitions against killing and raping, everything else begins to get a little more subjective...

Some of us may consider that certain forms of theft - usually from institutions, such as government or hotels - are acceptable in certain instances. Some of us may consider that driving above the legal speed limit, or driving whilst intoxicated isn't a mortal sin, provided breach of those laws isn't too excessive or routine.

But fundamentally, in order to reassure ourselves that others are forbidden from doing what they might want to do to us, we are happy to accept certain restrictions on some of the things that we might, if so pressed, want to do to others.

And since we accept or maintain acceptance of (most of) the conditions of our compact with Society, more or less freely, we do not really consider that the Laws of our Society that forbid us from [b]*DOING*[/b] certain things, are necessarily restrictions on our personal Freedom.

Like I said, we [b]*ARE*[/b] Free to [b]*DO*[/b] as we please, just as long as we understand that we will face punitive consequences if we are caught doing anything that our Society forbids.

Of course, where we are forbidden from [b]*LEAVING*[/b] a Society, where we do not agree with some or all of the restrictions placed on what we are permitted to do without associated negative consequence for ourselves, then it is fair to say that we are not Free: we are prisoners.

[b]2. Freedom to Be[/b]

Now this is the Freedom that I am most concerned with.

Being is not the same as Doing. Who I Am, is not the same as: what I do.

Who I Am is, and in my opinion [b]*SHOULD*[/b] be, all about the personal choices I make about what I want to believe; with my behaviour secondary and subordinate to those beliefs.

I do not believe myself to [b]*NOT*[/b] be a killer or a rapist, because I do not do either of those things: I do not kill or rape [b]*BECAUSE*[/b] I do not believe myself to be a killer or a rapist.

Oh, I know that there are those people who do kill and rape who do not believe their selves to be killers and rapists, because they dehumanise their victims and don't consider they are actually killing or raping an actual human being.

But those people are, in my and I think most people's opinion, mentally dysfunctional and obviously delusional. But they are also at the extreme end of the psychological spectrum, and I do not observe or believe myself to be psychologically unbalanced.

At a more rational level:

I am not a Christian because I resisted the Christian indoctrination of my Society (not my parents' indoctrination: they also resisted the same indoctrination and are an atheist (my Dad) and an agnostic (my Mum)).

I am also not a Nationalist or "Patriot", for the same reasons.

Neither am I motivated by material gain and the accumulation of wealth; I neither desire nor retreat from the social approval of my peers; I don't listen to certain types of music because that's what my friends listen/listened to; I didn't choose my sexuality based on an acceptance or rejection of the values prevalent in my Society.

And I don't believe in the same political ideology as my father; nor feel the same way about education or health and well-being as my mother.

I am, in short, and I hope in confidence: My Own Man.

I know that where I believe the same as another, it is a result of coincidence, not compulsion or coercion.

And that is what Freedom to Be, means to me.

And it's an internal state of mind, rather than an external activity.

I would be the same person, no matter which Society I lived in. I might not be able to speak as Freely about my ideals and values if I lived in some other society. And there's a whole bunch of stuff I would want to [b]*DO*[/b] to express my Self, but wouldn't be able to, in quite a few other societies. But that wouldn't change Who I Am, underneath the necessary and contextual conformance to whichever Society I chose to belong to (I wouldn't voluntarily imprison myself in a Society that forbade me from leaving; and would seek to escape such a Society at the earliest possible opportunity).

I think this is what has made Nelson Mandela such an icon of our Time.

He [b]*WAS*[/b] physically imprisoned, for a very long time, but he never compromised Who He Is, or What He Believes In.

By contrast, there are many people in Western Society who continue to behave the way they do, in order to conform with the external ideology they have been indoctrinated to.

I am not saying that Religion or Conservatism is inherently wrong. But I am saying that individuals should be able to [b]*CHOOSE*[/b] their religious or political beliefs, as they see fit. Not be compelled into religious or political conformity because that is what their parents' and/or Society requires of them.

Indeed, I believe that certain religions and political ideologies would be [b]*STRENGTHENED*[/b], if more of their adherents had voluntarily and Freely chosen to support them; that they are weakened by the fact that much of their support comes from those who have been taught/ordered to Fear any alternative.

Free Speech isn't, to me, about Freedom of the Press or Media; it is about the Freedom to say whatever one thinks, whenever one thinks it, without Fear of negative, hostile and/or violent reprisal.

I'm not talking about the Freedom to walk up to any other member of Society and say or shouting insulting and offensive personal remarks. I'm talking more about, for example, being able to speak the idea that maybe the 9/11 attacks on America were motivated by some of America's own policies, without being shouted down.

You can always tell a person who doesn't really understand what Free Speech really means, not because of [b]*WHAT*[/b] they might say in response to your voicing an opinion they disagree with, but because of [b]*HOW*[/b] they respond.

If they get angry or enraged because you have voiced an idea that is not personally attacking them, but which jars with their World View, then they are showing that they do not understand the True meaning of Free Speech.

Freedom to Be the individual you choose to be is the [b]*MOST IMPORTANT*[/b] aspect of Freedom.

As Nelson Mandela (and too many others) has demonstrated: even the most unjust and prolonged imprisonment is endurable, provided you do not forget Who You Are.
