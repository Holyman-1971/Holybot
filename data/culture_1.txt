But at the risk of being too semantic Border, and to address the broader topic this thread has turned to, I think what you're talking about above are cultural attitudes.

This thread took its current turn, addressing the issue of cultural [b]*DISTINCTIVENESS*[/b]. My contention is that contemporary American culture is just an extension of European culture, and nothing like as idiosyncratic as many Americans would like to believe.

To that end, we've got to identify characteristics that distinguish American culture from European culture. Attitudes like fiscal responsibility are more to do with social cultures that scan horizontally across many (if not all) national and ethnic cultures - same really with "Celebrity Worship" - so neither can be wholly claimed by one national or ethnic culture over another.

However, as of this moment in writing, I do have several clear ideas in my mind of several unique contributions to the overall contemporary Human Culture. And two of those are "Hollywood" and "Rock 'N' Roll".

Though driven by and dependent upon technological innovation, I think U.S. Culture can certainly claim the modern movie and popular music industries as wholly American inventions. I guess one mark of a successful cultural contribution, is its emulation by other cultures; and it's difficult to think of a culture so widely emulated, as the Entertainment Industry centred around Hollywood, and popular music.

The worship and idolisation of wealth and success, is by no means unique to the United States; but credit for the nexus between wealth and success, and figures in the Entertainment Industry - the creation of Movie Idols and Rock Gods/Divas - is difficult to take away from America.
