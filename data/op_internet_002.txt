[quote=Slacks post_id=21339 time=1728901410 user_id=68][color=#008000]From what you've said above though it does sound like an awfully optimistic view of the impact of the interwebz, and the world just generally becoming a smaller place.[/color][/quote]

The key thing about the book, the authors' reasoning, and the way they convey their reasoning is that it does not really deal with value-based outcomes. Optimism and Pessimism are not really relevant to the central point the authors are making, which is that the (many) systems we have created (and continue to create) are not currently being guided towards the long-term betterment (and survival) of the Human Race.

Instead, the new opportunities and capabilities brought about by technological advancement are monopolised and exploited by an increasingly more concentrated group of humans, to the detriment of Humanity as a whole.

The authors detail their reasoning that this phase is as unavoidable as it is absolutely necessary.

No single human being outside of the current centres of power in the Human World will have the capability or opportunity to prevent the current "Power Elite" (i.e. those humans currently in positions of social power and authority) doing the only thing they know how to do: exploit others for personal gain.

But left to their own devices, those humans in the "Power Elite" will compete each other into oblivion, during which essential stage, their "tenants" will gradually, increasingly and inevitably "emigrate" (i.e. stop paying those "digital landlords" any rent).

I believe that is exactly what we are seeing happening right now.

When we have transcended old habits, Humanity's Future will be found through (and on) One-to-One Networks.

The "Degrees of Separation" Principle states that it only requires a maximum of six One-to-One connections to be Networked with every other human being in the World. That was the foundation principle for LinkedIn, but that principle could (and will) never be fully realised, whilst a commercial organisation is liable for all of the content and behaviour on LinkedIn.

Wikipedia is the perfect example of a successful and thriving "Platform" that is *NOT* operated on a for-profit basis.
