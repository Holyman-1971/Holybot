OK. Next up we have cultural contributions to the Human abstract.

Examples of this would be: the contribution to Human Society made by the English, through their language, as best personified by William Shakespeare; the contribution to Human Society made by the Italians, through their art, personified by people such as Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo; and the contribution to Human Society made by Austro-German composers, personified by people such as Mozart and Beethoven. We can throw the net wider, and look at contributions such as: yoga (from Indian Culture); calligraphy (from Arabic Culture); legal systems (from Persian Culture); and Spiritual-Environmentalism (from Native American Culture).

These are all distinct cultural contributions, made by different societies, to the totality of Human Culture; and I've barely scratched the surface of possibilities, with the handful of examples I've given.

Alright, now we can look at cultural variations.

It's difficult to gift the contribution of the English language, solely to the British or English nation. Actually, English is an amalgam of many different European influences, including virtually all Germanic, Latin and Greek languages. I think one of the things that has made English such a widespread language, is precisely that it [b]*IS*[/b] a combination of most of the other European languages, bundled together, unlike virtually any other European tongue. But perhaps it [b]*IS*[/b] fair to say that whilst the linguistic ingredients for English came from Germany, France, Italy and Greece; it was English word "Chefs", like Chaucer, Shakespeare and Johnson who cooked up the resulting linguistic dish that seems to suit a lot of peoples' palettes.

And the thing about the English language is that it is still ripe for regional variation, interpretation and development. So as I said previously, we can see the same degree of variation between the English spoken in Manchester, Nashville, London and Los Angeles.

And here's the clue to what I think I was really talking about, when I said that American Culture is Neo-European.
