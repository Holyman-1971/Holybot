����Well Shotgun...

At first glance, your thread and posts appear to be utter lacking in humility and grace, but actually, the more I read on, the more I reconsidered that initial impression.

Unlike most *actual* "Whores", you do appear to be putting thought and consideration into how you are behaving with respect to the Game and the Community. And I think for that alone, your comments and expression are worthy of respect.

In reference to the specific incident you've screenied, I think you have every right to feel aggrieved, because as you pointed out in the second shot: "You people have a tank too." (Not to mention AT-class soldiers and the ability to work as a team to overcome just one single player dominating the round.)

On the broader themes in your thread.

This Game does currently have many balancing issues, to the point where I describe my participation in it as being part of an unofficial beta. Not least of these issues are the use of the vehicles, particularly the tank and Black-Hawks. The interaction between vehicles and infantry should, I anticpate, be balanced out as the Game evolves - assuming EA wish to keep selling game units, and potentially investigate charging fees for on-line participation.

But I feel that the question you posed in your first post cuts straight to the heart of the matter:

"Should the players that have the ability to see through the game and use every tool to their best advantage be throttled down to a lower level of skill by force? "

There's the rub Shotgun.

If I answer that question personally, it will be with an emphatic YES! Here's why:

BF2, like everything else, *is* just a Game. It's just a collection of binary digits that when pushed through a BPU/GPU silicon semiconductor, produces a visual representation of an artificial reality.

In other words: it ain't Real. It's pretend. It's a simulation. It's only a Game...

In many ways, it can also be called Art. The artistic skills of the Game-engine developers are not as evident as say the skills of the map and texture designers, but they do represent the creative expression of an imagined concept. The purpose of any artist working in any medium is to facilitate the willing suspension of disbelief. For example, Monet's impressionistic use of "dabs of light" are not meant to create a photographic representation of what a field of flowers actually looks like, but rather to convey to the viewer what a field of flowers *feels* like; to momentarily transport the viewer to the sensation of actually *being* in a field of flowers, rather than merely looking at a visual representation of them.

The same idea can be applied to any and all computer games. The intent of the developer is not to physically teleport you into a Real Life combat situation, for example, with red-hot metal whizzing around seeking to penetrate your soft, fleshy and non-respawning body. The actual intent is to go as far as possible to create a medium that will allow the game player to forget for a moment that they are sitting in front of an unnaturally lit, hot and stuffy gaming rig; to transport them to the heart of a battleground, fantasy world of wizards and dragons, or a simulated version of daily life.

This is a co-operative process that relies as much on the game player's ability to use their imagination as it does the developer's ability to create an artificial reality that reflects the true constraints of Real Life, without the inconvenient presence of the responsibilities that come with being a part of society. In other words, you *can* go out and play Battlefield 2 in Real Life, anywhere you can lay your hands on the hardware, but you'll have to deal with the fact that neither you nor the people you kill have a respawn facility... (Well, unless you're a Buddhist; but I understand the reincarnation process takes longer than 15 seconds, and most people tend to disconnect while waiting...)

Perhaps the greatest "spoiler" to the suspension of disbelief is being reminded by others that one is not actually immersed in a facsimilie of Real Life, but is rather just playing a game that can be seen through, and every "tool" can be used to their best advantage; where "tool" equates to the limitations of software code and the facility to create a fully immersive gaming environment, along the lines of Star Trek's "Holodeck".

Consider if BF2 *was* a fully artificially immersive Game, and you really only did get a single life - just one Life, that when lost, put you outside of the Game forever. How recklessly would you play with that life?

But you know Shotgun, that's just me, just how I like to play Games, and the BF2 Game. That's why I answer your question with an emphatic *personal* YES!

I do however understand that other players may not feel the same need, or gain the same level of enjoyment from escaping from their every day existence. To other players, squeezing out every possible advantage that the mechanics of the Game allows in order to advance their statistics is just as important to them. And I'm not making any judgement about that alternative choice, just defining it as a different, but equally valid motivation.

I think then that the answer to the issue - until such times as Total Immersion Video Games become a reality - is simply to find an accommodation.

If I find myself on a gaming server where the majority of the players are intent on bumping their statistics up, and at that moment, I would rather escape from reality ('cos sometimes *I* like to bump up my stats as well...), then all I need to do is find another server.

On the other hand, when I'm not concerned about my stats, I'll try to find a server where the majority *also* favour suspending their disbelief and don't choose to utilise the differential between Real Life and in-Game mechanics to achieve a statistical advantage over their fellow game players. On those occasions, if a single or small number of players join the server with stat-inflation as their motivation, I would hope that they would recognise they are on the wrong kind of server and leave to find a server that fits their game-play style without any antagonism.

In summary then Shotgun, your question is a closed one, with only "Yes" or "No" possible as answers. I imagine like most people, the answer I choose is going to depend on the mood I'm in. It will usually be yes, but if I'm chasing a new weapon to unlock, or a missing badge or medal, it's going to be no.

The question I return to you then is:

Would you stay on a server where your style of Game play was not welcome?

And kudos to you for a sincere "Confessions of a Stat-Whore"..! (If you'll pardon the phrase...) beer

Peace,

Holyman
