No.

The "Free Market" isn't a functioning institutional entity (I know you know this, but I need to state it to help my prose flow :)); it's just an abstract set of conceptual conditions. It doesn't have its own enforcement facilities, beyond its fundamental systemic conditions that dictate who can participate and on what terms.

The situation I am talking about goes on all the time, and has been going on for a long time.

Individuals and corporations regularly go bankrupt. When they do, a "receiver" or "administrator" is appointed to collect monies owed to that individual or corporation, liquidate any remaining assets they may have, and then distribute the proceeds amongst that individual or corporation's creditors. Any shortfall is written off by the individual or corporation's creditors as an irretrievable debt.

Furthermore, it is quite common practice for creditors to "write-down" debts owed - reduce the outstanding debt - in the common sense understanding that it is better to have some of a debt repaid, rather than none of it.

This situation often occurs when a person who has run-up massive credit card bills phones their credit card company and says they can't afford to pay what they owe. The credit card company will, if it accepts that it is unlikely to be able to recover all the outstanding debt, reach an accommodation with the debtor, and write-down some of the debt so that it stands a chance of recovering the remainder.

The situation that the Wall Street Banks currently face is that their debt obligations far exceed any monies owed or assets owned. Furthermore, their borrowings are primarily from each other, and routed through complex and arcane debt derivative instruments that even the CEO's of the banks have been forced to acknowledged that they don't understand.

What really kicked off this current catastrophic stage of the Global Economic Meltdown, was the collapse of Lehman Brothers at the end of last year. When it collapsed, it was clear that all its creditor institutions weren't going to see much or any of the monies owed by Lehman's. This in turn created shockwaves throughout the banking sector, since other banks were relying on Lehman's paying their debts, to allow Lehman's creditor banks to pay their debts.

It is the pyramidical nature and inter-connectedness of current Banking debt - of which some $60 trillion is accounted for by mystical derivatives called Credit Default Swaps - that has created the conditions for a domino effect that would, more or less simultaneously, wipe out all the major banks if they were forced to face up to their debts.

It is such a total collapse amongst all the major banks that would liberate Main Street from their debt obligations.
