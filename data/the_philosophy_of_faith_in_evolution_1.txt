You know what I think is always the missing component of these, uh, "Debates" about Science versus Religion?

A healthy dose of Philosophy.

I mean, c'mon, if we're mixing Science with Religion, I don't see why Philosophy shouldn't get a look in as well. Particularly as, more often than not, Philosophy is the means by which the irreconcilable can be reconciled - conceptually at least.

Truth is, I do occasionally get glimpses of where JFitch is coming from, with his arguments. Not the religious dogma, obviously - that's not an area I've ever particularly explored from the inside - but the conceptual basis of his iconoclasm.

In their own way, the "Scientists" or pro-Science posters are as close-minded as the "Creationists", or JFitch, uh, poster... Albeit on a much broader conceptual field.

See, whilst JF refuses to consider any evidence or theoretical mechanism or maxim that isn't validated by the Bible; the "Scientists" - or shall we call them the "Rationalists" - also refuse to consider any notions or concepts that can't be validated by empirical observation of evidence.

The key distinction, of course, is that there is a whole [b]*PLANET*[/b] full of evidence that can be empirically observed; whereas JFItch is restricted to the "evidence" of a single book - or Book.

But whereas JFitch is quite candid about his refusal to move outside the dogmatic constraints of a single piece of liturgy; the Rationalists are less honest about their disdain for anything that comes from outside of their [b]*OWN*[/b], admittedly much broader, but nevertheless still self-constrained conceptual framework.

I would add another distinction here however, which is that I believe the Rationalists, if presented with empirical proof of a Supernatural influence, [b]*WOULD*[/b] be quite willing to incorporate - indeed: [i]Naturalise[/i] - that additional force/factor into their conceptual framework. Whereas it is quite clear that JFitch is unwilling to accommodate, on Faith, many of the fundamental precepts of Science that we all exist in observable accordance with. Unless, I suppose, Bible 2.0 is published, with an unequivocally Divine Seal of Validation, which contains an instruction to the Faithful to acknowledge and accept all the Wonders of Human Endeavours, as facilitated (if not directly delivered) by God.

What JFitch alludes to, and for his own part, confesses to, is a conceptual basis for his [b]*OWN*[/b] Existence.

He Believes, Therefore He Is.

Furthermore, within the conceptual framework he has for (his) Existence, he understands [b]*HIMSELF*[/b] to be both of Supernatural Origin, and Supernatural Being.

Supernatural Origin, in that he was created by God; and Supernatural Being, in that he was created by God [b]*ABOVE*[/b] all animals (and presumably plants and fungi...).

By contrast, the Rationalists are far more humble about their own origins, and station in Life.

They see themselves as nothing more than particles within a vast, chaotic cosmos of Energy; at best, no more than cogs in a deterministic and fluid model.

And therein lies the limitations of the Rationalists' Conceptual Framework: they embark on an empirically scientific exploration of the [b]*REST*[/b] of Existence, [b]*BEFORE*[/b] they have established the nature (and evidence for) their [b]*OWN*[/b] Existence.

Just as the Religionists like JFitch take as axiomatic, the nature of their Existence as defined by the Bible; the Rationalists take as the basis for their own Existence, equally received wisdom.

Without Question.

Rationalists [b]*THINK*[/b] they Exist; Therefore They Must... Right..?

But if a Rationalist cannot verify or validate their own Existence; how can they hope, from such a precarious conceptual position, to verify or validate the Existence of anything else?

So here we come to the Philosophical root of the Problem:

How can a Rationalist prove, by their own standards, that they themselves Exist?

Oh, of course, they [b]*THINK*[/b] they Exist... But then, they're bound to, Right?

Just as the Biblically inclined defend the validity and veracity of what is written in the Bible, by fundamentally pronouncing that the [b]*BIBLE*[/b] says that the Bible is the True Word of God... So too do the Rationalists maintain, equally Fundamentally, that they must Exist, because they [b]*THINK*[/b] they exist.

Well they would say that, wouldn't they. :)

The Philosophical, uh, "Truth" of the matter (though good Philosophy should produce far more questions than answers), is that the same kind of circular reasoning is used to justify the Rationalists' point of view, as that which produces the Religionists' unshakeable Faith.

And just as the Fundamentalist Religionist is absolutely terrified at the prospect of considering or contemplating anything that falls outside of the rigid, canonical constraints of their own Ideology; so too are the Rationalists (usually) terrified of questioning whether they themselves Exist, or if, in Fact, they are just in a Waking Dream.

If we don't, in Fact, Exist in any Real Sense... If, in fact, the very notion of "Reality" is just something we've dreamed up to try and provide us with a degree of comfort in a Dream we don't seem to be able to wake up from... Then everything else that follows, Everything we Experience within that Dream, is necessarily Created within the constraints and confinement of our own, highly subjective Dreamscape.

And is therefore subject to whatever rules of Perception and Experience, we care to Imagine, at any given Moment.

Just Think About It.
