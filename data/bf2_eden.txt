***[[[[�������Have you thought about the historical context within which the Genesis story was first written?

There is a tendency, even an assumption, to view the Genesis story as one written contemporarily to the reader. But we know that the story was first transcribed, and committed to text more than three thousand years ago (c. 1,500BC). It is most probable that this transcription was based on the oral tradition of the nomadic Semitic tribes generically referred to as Hebrew. And indeed, we can see many precursors to the events and people described in Genesis in the written record of even earlier civilisations, all the way back to the first written records of any human civilisation, through Akkadian to Sumerian (c. 5,000 B.C.).

The Sumerian �Epic of Gilgamesh� is generally regarded to be the first story ever written down, and is the mythical/fictional story of one of the first kings of Sumer. The story was actually used as a sort of primer for scribes, and was frequently copied out by novice clerks and scribes as part of their education. The not quite complete story that survives to this day is collated from several individual and incomplete fragments that have been found in different locations in Mesopotamia at different times.

There are circumstantial similarities between the story of Gilgamesh and the Hebrew creation story that are notable, if not conclusive, including the creation of Enkidu, the wild man of the forest, by the Sumerian God. Enkidu�s downfall occurs early on in the story as the result of his falling to the temptation of a woman (Shamhat, the Temple prostitute). Later on in the Epic of Gilgamesh there are events that are almost identical to the story of Noah and the Flood, as well as other aspects that mirror the book of Ecclesiastes very closely.

Just as we see in Christianity much symbolism and even narrative threads that are observable in Pagan precursors, when we look at the etymology of the Old Testament, we can see that it too is an eclectic compendium of many of the stories contemporary to its time, all stemming from what one must infer was the generic Neolithic oral tradition in the region.

But let�s be clear, to identify more down-to-Earth sources for stories in Genesis (and indeed, the whole Pentateuch � the first five books of the Old Testament), does not have to negate their Divine origins. Indeed, one could easily infer that the importance and durability of these particular stories may well be a reflection of the importance that Neolithic cultures placed upon them. And that importance may very well be based on the understanding of those stories� origins.

So moving out of the history and into the theosophical, we conclude that these stories � The Story � were indeed communicated to Humanity by its Creator. But as we do so, we ought to pay careful attention to the degree of social, cultural and technical development of Humanity at the time these stories were first transcribed.

Looking at the Eden Story, we might find it easier to consider that the language used is allegorical, rather than literal. It certainly makes more sense to do so and requires far less rational contortionism. In the same way that parents stereotypically relate the mechanics of sexual reproduction to young children referring to metaphorical �Birds and Bees�, doesn�t it make more sense to believe that God wrapped up the anthropological complexities of the development of Humanity into a story about a single man and a single woman giving rise to the entirety of the Human Race?

The alternative, literal interpretation requires us to do all sorts of mental somersaults to over-ride the whole question of incest and what we understand today to be the biological and genetic problems with incestuous relationships. We are forced to fall back on the idea that Adam & Eve were genetically flawless, and indeed, they would need to be to have lived the 800 years they did after the birth of Seth, and during which they had �many sons and daughters� (Genesis 5:4). But when we start talking about beings that lived for 800 years, interbreeding flawlessly, are we really still talking about human beings? Or have we crossed cultures again perhaps to talk about the Egyptian Sons of Horus or even the Greek Titans? If the seed couple were genetically flawless, and all humanity descended (were �begat by�..!) that original, flawless couple, where then did the genetic flaws that are now present and observable in humanity come from? A later addition by God?

No, it is *far* easier (and, uh, more sensible) to consider the Eden story as an allegory in �baby language�. Not a lie, not untruthful, but just a simplistic presentation of information to complex to be understood by a culture and society only just beginning to get to grips with the basics of animal husbandry.

There is another interpretation of the Eden story that does make it more contemporarily relevant, and that is that the story describes our own individual lives. The �expulsion� from Eden relates to our exit from Paradise/Heaven to begin a temporal experience here on Earth. Before each one of us begins our human life, we as spirits choose to partake of the �Fruit of the Tree of the Knowledge of Life�, and begin an Earthly experience separated from God and Paradise.

The Eden story then serves both as the answer to the question �Where did *we* come from?� and also, �Where did *I* come from?� And one would anticipate that a Divinely sourced answer would be eloquent and elegant enough to answer both questions with such a simple response.

There is a desire on the part of those who wish to interpret the Eden story, and indeed the rest of the Bible literally, to see the answers it contains as literal, conclusive and final; even as those answers are contained in the translations of translations of a transcribed oral tradition that stretches back through humanity�s anthropological and cultural genesis.

I personally think that just like the �answers� we give to children, the Truths in the Bible are intended to give the kind of understanding that invites further exploration of a more sophisticated Truth.

That is, of course, unless we choose to remain as intellectual nomads in the Desert of Understanding.
