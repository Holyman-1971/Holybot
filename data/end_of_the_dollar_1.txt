Prior to World War Two, most nations backed their economies with gold - the [link=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_standard]Gold Standard[/link].

Sure that most of you know how that worked, so I'll let Wikipedia^^^ do the explaining if anyone needs further.

Main thing to know is that a combination of The Great Depression and World War Two fucked the Gold Standard.

So in 1944, the Allied Nations got together and devised the [link=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretton_Woods_system]Bretton Woods System[/link].

Broadly and simply speaking, the Bretton Woods System established that only the U.S. Dollar would retain a (more or less) fixed convertibility rate with gold, and everyone else's currency would fluctuate in relation to the U.S. Dollar.

In essence then, in 1945, the World moved to a U.S. Dollar standard for their currencies, though the U.S.D. was still backed by gold...

...Until August 1971, when Nixon realised the U.S. had spent virtually all of its gold, mostly on fighting the Vietnamese, and decided, unilaterally, to terminate the convertibility of the U.S. Dollar to gold.

This action, known as the [link=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_shock]Nixon Shock[/link] meant that the U.S. Dollar then became the World's [i]de facto[/i] Reserve Currency.

And that was that.

A reserve currency is a currency held in, uh, reserve, by national economies, to both shore up the value of their own currency, and to be used for trading in global commodities such as oil, which are priced in that reserve currency.

The advantages to a nation having its currency used as the primary international reserve currency are many.

Primarily, it means that you pretty much have a blank cheque-book.

Think of it this way:

If I write out a £100 cheque and give it you for services rendered 'Slacks, I'm not actually going to be £100 poorer, until you decide to cash that cheque.

Now let's say that I have also written out a £50 cheque to Wunder, for goods that I have bought from him. Again, until he cashes that cheque, I've still got that money in my bank account.

*NOW* let's say that you buy £50 worth of goods from Wunder. Instead of writing out a cheque from your own bank account, you give him the £100 cheque that I gave you; and in return, he gives you the £50 worth of goods, *AND* the £50 cheque I gave him previously.

You both know that I'm good for the money, so neither of you rushes to cash the cheque. In fact, you both know that I'm probably better for the money than either of you are. You know that if you cash the cheques I've written you, you'll probably piss it up the wall, or spend it on an instantly regrettable fuck with a lady of negotiable affection.

So the longer you keep the cheques from me un-cashed, the longer you'll both stay rich. And you can put off cashing the cheques from me, because you know that those cheques function as a tradable currency, because *EVERYONE* trusts the Holy Bank.

The advantage that I get from having this blank cheque-book, is that I can keep writing cheques that everyone else will accept, for all the things that I need to buy for my own purposes. So I don't ever really need to worry about money, so long as my reputation and standing in the World remains strong and confident.

However...

If for any reason I lose my reputation and others lose their confidence in me, then I would face the very real prospect of, suddenly, everyone cashing in all of those cheques that I'd written out.

And having written out so many cheques, for so long, I might not have exactly as much money as I need to cover them all....

Which would be a bad thing for me.

On top of all those un-cashed cheques suddenly turning bad, I would also suddenly find that people weren't accepting any further cheques for me. In fact, they might insist that the only cheques they would accept from me would be any cheques that I had from the Wunderbank, or the Bank of 'Slacks - assuming I hadn't rapidly cashed any cheques that you may have previously written to me.

In short, instead of being able to pay for all the imports I require with my own cheques (my own currency), I'd have to first convert my cash into another person's cheques (currency). And at this point in my financial life, I might not be able to get a very good exchange rate...

It would be very much a situation like a balloon bursting too. All that confidence I'd spent years building up, would suddenly go pop, as everyone rushed to get those cheques of mine they held cashed first. It would be a very rapid deflation.

Of course, I'd previously relied on the fact that I had written *SO MANY CHEQUES*, that those holding lots and lots and lots of my cheques, wouldn't want to precipitate a collapse in my finance's reputation, because they would also stand to lose out, as the value of their Holybank cheque holdings would also decline, almost certainly faster than they could get rid of them.

So whilst my creditors might facilitate an *ORDERED* deconstruction of my finances, that would only be to minimise their losses. But once the event horizon was crossed that essentially marked a "Run" on my finances, they would then just cut their losses and sell whatever remained of their Holybank holdings for whatever pennies they could get on the Holypound.

So the situation we have now, is that the 2nd largest economy in the World (China), has agreed to cease using the U.S. Dollar in its bilateral trade with the 7th largest economy in the World (Russia). This follows an agreement earlier in the year between China and the 9th largest economy in the World (Brazil) to do the same thing.

Following this deal between Russia and China, it is highly likely that India - which (currently) holds observer status within the [link=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_Cooperation_Organisation]Shanghai Cooperation Organisation[/link] - will shortly follow suit with its bilateral trade arrangements with both China, Russia and Brazil.

Indeed, it is likely that the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation will soon become a new Economic bloc of the same type as the European Economic Union (which is not the same beast as the Eurozone). At that point, there would be no reason on Earth why the SCO members would continue to use the U.S. Dollar for trade.

The action that has really kicked all of this off - beyond the U.S.'s obvious financial problems - is the announcement by the Federal Reserve of the [link=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_easing]Quantitative Easing 2[/link] program.

The justification sold to the American public for the QE-2 program was that it would make it easier for them to borrow money from American banks that are backed by the Federal Reserve.

However, whilst the American public may have a limited grasp of economics, the American banks do not.

The banks realise that it is far more profitable to take the money that it can borrow at very low interest rates (0 - 1%) from the Federal Reserve, and lend it out to institutions in countries like Russia, China, Brazil and India, where they can charge 8 or 9%; as opposed to lending it out to already hard-pressed retail customers in the U.S.

Banks borrowing newly printed money from the U.S. Federal Reserve can, in fact, make a healthy 7 or 8% return, for doing precisely fuck all.

The downside of this for the emerging economies (and not just the big BRIC economies), is that it is driving up inflation in those countries. And those countries, led by China, have been politely asking the U.S. Government and the Federal Reserve to kindly stop minting low interest Dollars, unless the Americans want a fucking Trade and Currency War.

Unfortunately, the Americans, as we now know from Wiki-leaks, have a rather contemptuous attitude towards foreign diplomats and nation states.
