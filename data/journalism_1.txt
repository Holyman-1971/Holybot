I think it rests upon our definition and/or personal interpretation of the purpose and function in Society of Journalism.

It is important to separate the two definitions of "Reporter" and "Journalist", since they have two different meanings.

In the truest sense of the term, a reporter is someone who reports fact.

His personal subjectivity exerts itself in his choosing which facts to report, and even, which facts to be aware of. Other than that, he is merely a human cog in a mass communications exercise.

A good definition of a journalist is someone who reports fact, with context.

A journalist's subjectivity is obviously and clearly apparent in the context that they use to report on, or comment upon facts.

Ethically, the issue is about who gets to choose the context, in and with which new facts are reported to us.

And if it is the journalist or the journalist's employer who chooses the context or the facts, how far should or can that context and those facts be trusted?
