I think that's the problem right there though AV.

You are starting from the position that the ideology you [b]*BELIEVE*[/b] in provides you with the most correct and authoritative response. And so that's where your thinking ends.

Try to set aside your ideological predisposition for a moment, and try and consider it as a purely practical, even mathematical problem:

Let A = The Private Sector.

Let B = The Public Sector.

Both A & B are capable of create jobs.

Jobs create personal incomes.

Incomes are spent on goods and services provided by A.

Incomes are also used to pay taxes to B.

A uses the income it receives to create jobs. (B also uses the taxes that it receives to create jobs.)

[b]More income for A means more jobs; and more jobs means more income for A.[/b]

Now when A is receiving less income, it creates less jobs, and eventually has to eliminate jobs.

Less jobs mean less incomes.

And we arrive at the final, deteriorating loop:

[b]Less income for A means less jobs; and less jobs means less income for A.[/b]

That's it. That's where we arrive at. It isn't important at this stage how we arrived there - whether by Bubbles created by speculation or stimulus. The only thing that is important is how to break that downward spiralling loop.

At no point in the equation do we see that the reason A is not creating new jobs/incomes, because of the prevailing rate of taxation. Private investors are not investing in A, not because their investment returns will be subject to taxation, but because there is no point in investing in something that no-one can afford to buy.

No private investor - or group of private investors - possesses sufficient capital to invest so heavily in private enterprise that it can create the jobs necessary to create the incomes required to return the Economic Cycle to its positive phase.

We see only that A cannot create jobs if it is not receiving, or likely to receive any income.

Keynes' solution (which carried the "Holyman Seal of Approval") to the problem is to have B create jobs by borrowing money to create them. This because B [b]*IS*[/b] able to borrow sufficient funds to stimulate the Economic Cycle back to its positive phase. Then:

[b]Jobs created by B produce income for A.[/b]

And:

[b]Income for A means more jobs; and more jobs means more income for A.[/b]

And the cycle starts all over again.

"Your" - or rather your ideologues' - solution in the above situation is:

[b]When A is cutting jobs (and subsequently cutting incomes); then B should also cut jobs (and further cut incomes).[/b]

Now perhaps I'm missing something here AV, but I really don't see how B cutting jobs at the same time as A is cutting jobs stands any chance of reversing the decline.
