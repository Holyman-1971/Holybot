The Banking Crisis wasn't caused by the bank's lending money out to dodgy borrowers so that they could buy houses.

At the peak, in 2007, the total amount of outstanding mortgage debt in the U.S. was $14 trillion (coincidentally the same as America's national debt today). Of that $14 trillion, around 20% - just under $3 trillion - was loaned out to what were called "Sub-Prime" borrowers.

And of that $3 trillion of sub-prime mortgages, only about 20% - around $600 billion - ever went into default.

$600 billion Foota. That's how much the feckless and greedy fleeced the banks for.

Meanwhile, off the back of $14 trillion worth of mortgages, the Banking System created a derivatives bubble worth more than [b]$60 trillion[/b]; and a further [b]$70 trillion[/b] in unregulated insurance securities called Credit Default Swaps.

It was the collapse of the derivatives and securities market that threatened to collapse the entire Global Financial System; *NOT* the failure of a relative handful of sub-prime borrowers to keep up the payments on loans they should never have been given.

But they were given those loans because the banks needed an originating loan on which to stack a pyramid full of derivatives and securities. And the banks *PREFERRED* Sub-Prime borrowers, because they could then charge higher interest and fees for the resultant Mortgaged Backed Securities.
