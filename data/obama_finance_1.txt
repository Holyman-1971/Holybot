[b]Let's start with the Banking Sector.[/b]

Banks make money by lending money.

That is: Banks earn money from interest payments made by the borrower; but Banks *ALSO* - and very literally - *MAKE* money, by making the loans (on which they will also collect interest payments).

Here's how it works:

[url=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_reserve_banking]Fractional Reserve Banking[/url] is the current form of banking used in all countries worldwide.

There are two types of money in a fractional-reserve banking system operating with a Central Bank:

[b]Central Bank Money[/b]: is any money created or adopted by the Central Bank (regardless of its form); with the fiat currency system that is prevalent in most Global economies, this generally means electronic money, loaned to commercial banks at the "Base Rate" of interest that is widely publicised as "The Interest Rate".

[b]Commercial Bank Money[/b]: is (essentially) money deposited in the commercial banking system by corporations and private citizens.

When a Commercial Bank borrows money from a Central Bank, it deposits that money into its own reserves, simultaneously creating an equal amount of new Commercial Bank money to the borrowed Central Bank money.

And now the miraculous money creation process (called "deposit multiplication") kicks into play:

When a loan is made by the Commercial Bank, it is required to keep only a fraction of the money loaned in reserve (hence: Fractional Reserve Banking). For the ease of the mathematics here, let's say that this fraction is 10% (though in reality, in the U.S. and U.K., it is actually around 3% these days...).

What this means is that for every $100 of Central Bank money that the Commercial Bank borrows, it can loan out $1000 to corporations and private individuals. So whilst the Commercial Bank is only on the hook to the Central Bank for $100 of debt, it has its own debtors who are on the hook to it for $1000.

In the Olden Days of course, this process would be enacted by the Commercial Bank ordering $1000 of printed currency notes from the Government's Mint, which would duly be dispatched to it. And even though the Commercial Bank would receive $1000 in currency notes, it would still only owe the Central Bank $100. This because the paper currency notes have no intrinsic value: they are only notional scripts used for moving monetary value around the Economy.

But nowadays, the Commercial Bank doesn't need to order anywhere near as much paper from the Mint, because it just transfers the $1000 to the borrower(s) electronically; just as the Central Bank electronically transferred the $100 to the Commercial Bank.

The [url=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_reserve_banking#Money_creation_process]Money Creation Process[/url] is a little more technical than I've outlined above, but the results are essentially as I've outlined. Point being that it is the Commercial Banks who create the money that you and I earn and spend every day; not our Governments' Central Banks or Mints.

So the "Social Service" provided by Commercial Banks is that it manufactures and distributes the money that we ordinary mortals use in our day-to-day lives. And the material benefit that the Commercial Banks "earn" for themselves, is on the amount of interest they charge to corporations and private individuals who borrow the "money" that the Commercial Banks create out of thin air.

(Though even "thin air" has a slight molecular substance: better to say that the Commercial Banks create the "money" out of Pure Thought!)

The key point here is:

Commercial Banks *ONLY* earn money by lending out the money that they create as part of their function within the Fractional Reserve Banking System.

If Commercial Banks can't or don't loan money out: they don't earn any money themselves. Encouraging and facilitating corporations and private citizens into taking on debt is the *PRIMARY* function of Commercial Banks. If Human Society ever got to the point where it no longer needed or desired to borrow money: the Commercial Banks would go out of business.

This is an absolutely fundamental understanding.

Another way by which Commercial Banks can earn money, is by setting up their own "Investment" operations: by becoming Investment Banks.

Equally, very successful Investment Houses can bypass the unnecessary latency created by the need to secure funds for the purchase of stocks on margin, by establishing themselves as Commercial Banks.

From either direction, the barrier between an Investment House/Fund Manager operating a combined operation as a Commercial Bank, or between a Commercial Bank operating as an Investment House/Fund Manager, disappears.

Of course, all facetiousness aside, the inherent dangers of this dynamic was very well observed following the Wall Street Crash and ensuing Great Depression. And it was for this reason that the [url=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass%E2%80%93Steagall_Legislation]Glass-Steagall Act[/url] was passed in the U.S. in 1933.

The Glass-Steagall Act (actually, just four provisions within the [url=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_Act_of_1933]1933 Banking Act[/url] ) stipulated (sensibly) a prohibition on Commercial Banks operating as Investment Banks, and vice versa.

It meant that Commercial Banks couldn't borrow (as an illustrative example) $100 from the Central Bank, in order to then make a loan of $1000 to its Investment Bank operation, which could then be used to speculatively purchase stocks that were anticipated to appreciate in value. In essence, it prevented the Central Bank from being used as the source lender for Margin purchases of stocks.

This is where it all gets a bit "Alice in Wonderland"... See if you can follow the White Rabbit:

Commercial Bank borrows $100,000 from the Central Bank.

Commercial Bank then loans out $1,000,000 as a mortgage to a residential property buyer.

Commercial Bank then "sells" the $1,000,000 mortgage agreement to its Investment Bank operation.

The Investment Bank operation "buys" the $1,000,000 mortgage agreement from its Commercial Bank operation, using money it has borrowed from its Commercial Bank operation (money the Commercial Bank created by borrowing $100,000 from the Central Bank).

The Investment Bank then bundles the $1,000,000 mortgage agreement up with a bunch of other mortgage agreements, into a financial instrument called a Mortgage Backed Security.

The Investment Bank then pays a Credit-Rating Agency like Moody's or Standard & Poor's to give the newly-created MBS a Triple-A rating.

It then trades the MBS as a security, encouraging investors to buy shares in it, on the promise of income to be derived from the underlying mortgage repayments.

Having passed off the mortgage agreement to its Investment Banking operation, the Commercial Bank then removes the loan from its loan-book, freeing it up to make further loans without breaching its Fractional Reserve Requirement.

So from the original $100,000 sum borrowed from the Central Bank, the Commercial Bank can create $1m mortgage after $1m mortgage, provided that at each step, it passes off the $1m mortgage agreement to its Investment Bank operation, for bundling and resale as (part of) a Mortgage Backed Security.

This isn't really (or safely...) how Fractional Reserve Banking is supposed to work... But you know what these Financial Masters of the Universe are like: Devils for spotting and exploiting loop-holes..!
