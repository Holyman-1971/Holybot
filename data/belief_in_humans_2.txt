The beauty and efficacy of the U.S. Constitution is found in the Framer�s contempt for centralised authority and unnecessary bureaucracy. Having thrown off the yoke of tyrannical-Monarchical authority, the Founding Fathers recognised the limitations of individual human beings.

Long before the Historian Lord Acton coined the phrase �Power corrupts, and absolute power corrupts, absolutely�, the U.S. Constitution framed this understanding in a system of Government that was absolutely unique in the World.

Everyone knows about the �System of Checks and Balances� that is supposed to underpin the egalitarian function of the U.S. Government. But more important than that was the de-centralisation of power and authority inherent in the Federal structure of the Union of the States.

In a time before rapid transport and communications, it was simply impractical and inconvenient to have too much authority and power vested in a single geographic area, let alone a single individual. Under the Federal structure of the United States, State legislatures and Gubernatorial Executives took care of things at a local level. Only military and foreign policy, and to a lesser degree economic policy was, or needed to be determined by the Federal Government.

Thomas Jefferson understood this dynamic very well, and is perhaps more verbose on the subject of political parties than any other early American leader:

"Men by their constitutions are naturally divided into two parties:
1. Those who fear and distrust the people, and wish to draw all powers from them into the hands of the higher classes.
