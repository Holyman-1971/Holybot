I think it's worth bearing in mind that Economics is probably the most tedious aspect of Human Life we are all required to deal with.

Like Politics, if any of us really had a choice, we'd never give Economics a second thought. Of course, many of us try [b]*REAL*[/b] hard not to give Economics (or Politics) any thought anyway; but since our Existence is inextricably entwined with and influenced by Economics, there really is no escaping it.

We all know how much it costs us to do the things that we want to do. We all know how much it costs us to do the things that we [b]*NEED*[/b] to do. And we are all very aware that some others don't ever have to worry about how much it costs to do the things they need or want to do; and many, many more others do little else in their lives, but worry about how much it is going to cost to do the things they [b]*MUST*[/b] do, if they and their families are to survive.

This existential and sociological necessity to monitor the economic factors affecting (and in many cases, dominating) our lives is tiresome at best, and soul destroying at the extreme.

All of which means that given the option to delve deeper than we absolutely must, into the apparently complex and arcane world of Economics, most of us would really rather not. And it is this sense of repulsion that acts as our first obstacle to understanding Economics, and what is taking place within our Human Economic Systems.

There are other obstacles, of course, but perhaps the greatest comes from those who [b]*HAVE*[/b] crossed the threshold into the world of Economics, and (apparently) been initiated into its Mysteries.

Really, those who work at the "heights" (depths...) of our Economic Systems, really don't want any more people than necessary, to understand exactly what it is they are getting up to. This, not primarily because they are usually up to no good (though it is true that they usually are); but because they don't want to allow any more competition than is absolutely necessary to keep the gears of Economics turning.

And to this end - just like politicians - they make the world of Economics seem complex, mysterious, arcane, complex; and if all else fails: deeply, deeply dull.

But here's the thing: Economic systems are just like any other system, baffling to the uninitiated, but actually fairly straightforward to those who spend a bit of time learning the ropes.

And I'm not talking about a lot of time, just a little.

The [b]*REAL TRUTH*[/b] about Economics is that [b]*NO-ONE*[/b] fully understands how the Whole System works. And that is odd, if you think about it, because Economics are entirely a Human abstract construct; all data is empirical; and there are (apparently) no factors influencing Economics, other than those that can be quantified.

But the reality is that our Human Economic Systems are as unpredictable and volatile as our Planet's Meteorological System.

Those who work in the (entirely Globalised) Finance Sector, don't claim to understand how the Whole System works; instead, they focus on their own little corners of the overall System, trying to predict and guess future events. In this, they are the 21st Century equivalents of scryers and sooth-sayers; and are equally elevated to exalted positions within our Social Hierarchy. And whilst they are technically rewarded according to their overall success rates, their "successes" always come down to a dose of 50/50 luck: they either guessed right; or they guessed wrong.

Now, all of this isn't to say that there aren't some hyper-complex financial instruments that have been created by the High Priests of Human Economics: there certainly are. Indeed, as the more people came into the Financial Markets at the lower-end of the Food Chain - Internet Speculators and Day Traders, for example - the Top Predators created more and more arcane and opaque ways of accumulating (temporarily) more and more capital wealth.

The removal of and failure to re-impose adequate oversight and regulation on the Financial Markets by Governments, have in the first decade of the 21st Century, created some of the most complex mechanisms for making money out of money that the World has ever seen.

I haven't got a clue how the majority of these new instruments work.

And neither have our Governments.

Or, indeed, has anyone else outside of the Inner Circle of the High Clergy of Finance.

And there's quite a lot of evidence to suggest that those [b]*INSIDE*[/b] the Circle, who actually [b]*CREATED*[/b] these exotic markets, mechanisms and instruments, and who operate them on a day-to-day basis, [b]*ALSO*[/b] have no real idea how they all work...

However, the good news is that you and I don't really need to understand how things like "Structured Investment Vehicles", "Credit Default Swaps" and other Economic Frankenstein's Monsters work. No, all we really need to understand are the basic systems and methodologies that underpin the whole road-show.

And whilst I'm not saying that is incredibly easy or straightforward, it certainly isn't as difficult as our innate repulsion with Economics, or the Custodians of Arcane Economic Knowledge, would have us believe.

There are plenty of us who play MMORPGs, and other very complex Computer Games. And because we have a passion and an interest in those Games, we take the time to really understand the underlying systems that provide and control the mechanics of those Games.

Whilst I'm not saying that understanding our Global Economic System is easy or particularly engaging; I would say that it was easier than really getting to grips with all the ins and outs of a Game like "World of Warcraft", for example; or even the nuances of "Civilization IV".

I am [b]*NOT*[/b] an Economics Genius - far from it. But as with Politics, I take the view that the Whole World, and the Human Condition it supports, is a [b](*REALLY*)[/b] Massively Multiplayer On-Line Reality. And if I take the time to explore and acquaint myself with the underlying systems (we Humans have) created to support Existence, it not only gives me something of a competitive advantage, it also enhances the enjoyment of my participation.

I know a lot of people question my apparent obsession with politics, but as with Economics, it just seems to me to be an absurd idea that I wouldn't try to really understand [b]*EXACTLY*[/b] what is happening with the Systems that ultimately have such an impact on my Life, and the Lives of those I Love.

It doesn't take a huge brain to get that understanding; it just takes a bit of passion for, and commitment to, The Game.
