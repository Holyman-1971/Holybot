Oh, now, see I saw BSG as an allegory of Humanity's *INTERNAL* struggle to find an equilibrium between the Rational, Functional Ego, and the Emotional, Creative Spirit.

The Cylons represent Humanity's attempts to create better and better iterations of itself (the Eastern philosophical flow of Samsara), corrupted by the need of the individual to achieve dominance over the collective.

The Cylons can be measurably better than their human creators on every quantifiable scale - the same scales that humans use to measure relative intra-species worth - but lacking the impossible-to-synthesize "Spark" that is the Human Spirit, the Cylons are doomed to an eternity of: Capability Without Purpose.

Conversely - and perhaps perversely by dint of their being the products of Pure Thought - Cylons transcend individual Ego and the Illusion of Separation, tempting and tantalising Humans with the achievements made possible by the Cylons' fluent and harmonious collective identity.

Humans can achieve anything they can imagine themselves achieving, if only they could stop arguing over who has the *RIGHT* idea of "What the Future Should Be"; if only they could stop arguing over who will get: The Credit.

Humanity has the Capability, but it cannot agree on: The Purpose.

The inability to reconcile these two apparently different aspects, confines Humanity to a repeating cycle of Creation and Re-Creation, in a "Reality" where Time and Space are essentially irrelevant.
