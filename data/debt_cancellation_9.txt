No Slates, the case I illustrated was that it is better to rent and save, than buy on a mortgage.

It wasn't "Rental versus Ownership"; it was "Rental (and Saving/Investing) versus Mortgaged Purchase".

There are two states of affairs: one is having to pay something from your income, to live in the property you call home; and the other is [b]*NOT*[/b] having to pay something from your income, to live in the property you call home.

Obviously, the second state of affairs is clearly the better one: being able to live in your home, without having to pay anyone for the privilege.

Whilst aiming to arrive at that second state of affairs, you will be in the first state: having to pay out from your personal income, either rental or mortgage payments.

If you pay rent, then the money goes into the pocket of your landlord; if you pay a mortgage, then some of the money goes to paying for your home, and some of it goes to paying interest on the money you have borrowed to facilitate paying for your home.

My illustration and explanation showed that so long as rental payments on the same property are 75% or less than the equivalent mortgage payments, then it is more cost-effective to rent and invest the saving, than take out a mortgage.

But once you've reached the point where you can own your home outright, and cease paying either rent [b]*OR*[/b] mortgage, you are clearly in the best possible position.
