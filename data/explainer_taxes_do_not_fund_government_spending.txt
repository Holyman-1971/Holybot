[b]Housebound[/b]

OK, so "Common Knowledge" informs us that our governments must collect taxes (or borrow money), in order to fund whatever programs it desires to spend money on.

That is: money first flows into government coffers through taxes collected; and is then spent "back [sic]" into the Economy, through various programs and purchases.

All the arguments are about how much money a government should and does collect in taxes, from whom those taxes are collected, and then on which government programs that money is spent.

Such arguments, when carried out by politicians, pundits, talk-show hosts, and regular, non-economist people, will almost always be presented in the paradigm of the Household.

We are comfortable with the household paradigm, because it reflects our everyday Reality; and because it means we do not have to leave the Comfort Zone of our analogical homes, and venture into the murky and mystical forest of Economics.

It is obvious that any Household has to get money from somewhere, if it is going to have money to spend on stuff. And so why should it be any different for governments? Yes of course there are all sorts of complicated political, logistical and economic factors around Tax Collection and Government Spending...

...But the basic principle is still the same, isn't it?

Well, no, it isn't.

And this is the crucial point that I know Hoff has made several times previously:

A government with its own Central Bank can *CREATE MONEY*.

Households cannot.

This is the "teensy little huge fact" that makes the "Household-Government" finance analogy wrong and wildly misleading. We have to reject this "Household" analogy whenever it comes up, from any source, including our own minds, where it will sneak in.

Where to start de-programming our minds then?

Well, think of how different things would be for you, if your household [i]could[/i] create money.

I don't mean "creating money" by earning it: I mean creating money out of thin air. Would make quite a difference to your Life, and the lives of the people living in your household, wouldn't it?

OK then.

Since [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_shock]Nixon ended the Gold Standard[/url] in 1971, the U.S. Dollar has been a fiat currency (just like every major currency is).

This means that dollars are now created by the Federal Government, with no concern for representing any physical commodity. And the Federal Government can create as many Dollars as it wants, [b]as numbers in electronic account ledgers[/b], without "collecting" any Dollars in advance.

Whatever economic reason there is to limit the amount of Dollars the Government create (see below), it is *NOT* because the Government hasn't previously "collected" enough of them in taxes (or fees, loans, &c.).

Here is the Key Understanding:

As a matter of Economic Logic, taxes do *NOT* precede and provide the money for government spending: government spending precedes and provides the money that is later collected in taxes.

It is not: "Tax and Spend".

It is: "Spend and ([i]then[/i]) Tax".

That is the sequence.

Every Dollar paid in taxes, is a Dollar that was created by Government Authority.

[i]There is no place else it could have come from.[/i]

OK? That is the Key Understanding.

If your Government hadn't first created those dollars you are about to pay in tax... How would you pay your tax bill?

With a fiat currency, spending and taxing are economically separate, parallel activities.

The Government does not need taxes in order to spend: it just needs a decision.

It isn't the amount of taxation revenue raised that determines where and how a Government will spend money: but the political will of that Government.

The Government can create money for any program it has the political will to fund, whether healthcare or nuclear weapons, prior to and apart from collecting any taxes.

So... If taxes are not a revenue source on which Government Spending depends, what are they for?

Taxes are that portion of the money the Government *HAS* created and spent into the Economy, which the Government then withdraws for reasons that have [i]nothing to do with needing to collect money to spend[/i].

Economically, taxes are for controlling inflation; and socio-politically, for promoting valued public policies e.g. controlling/preventing Wealth Inequality.

And to be clear about that socio-political purpose of "controlling/preventing Wealth Inequality": that does *NOT* mean, "to fund social programs" or to redistribute wealth more evenly.

It means, simply and elegantly, to prevent people from becoming too rich.

By which is meant: to prevent currency inflation from turning everyone into Dollar Millionaires.

Because it is not primarily Central Bank Interest Rates that control currency inflation, but taxation.

Think about it...

If the Government announced that the Minimum Wage was now $10,000 an hour (and the Government would create whatever amount of money was needed to support that), Currency Inflation would go through the roof, wouldn't it?

Because every American working in a Fast-Food outlet, would now be earning $80 - $100,000 a day.

But if at the same time, the Government also announced that it was raising the Basic Rate of Income Tax to 99.9%... For every $10k earned by the guy working in KFC, he would pay $9,990 in tax. Meaning he's still only going to be taking home $10 an hour.

That's an extreme illustration to emphasise the point that taxation is used to limit how much of the money that a Government creates and spends *INTO* the Economy, is retained within the Economy.

Point being that with a fiat currency, Government spending and taxation are separate, parallel activities. Taxes do not take money out of one activity, in order to pay for another.

Taxing citizens does not transfer funds directly to pay for another activity like Healthcare; but it does help configure the money supply on a macro level to enable more Social Spending...

...Because it averts the inflation that would occur if both a lot of spending on healthcare *AND* the infinite appropriation of money by individuals were tolerated.

Taxes don't "pay for" anything.

But they are a means to "un-pay" citizens.

A more realistic example then:

If Government decides to spend $100 billion a year on Healthcare Provision, it will create that $100 billion at the stroke of a few keys, and then move that newly created money into the Economy, by spending it on Healthcare providers, professionals and supplies.

Ker-ching! The Economy has now expanded to the tune of $100 billion, as all those people and corporations receive the money that the Government has just created.

But...

If the Government keeps creating multi-billion dollar amounts of "keystroke money", and keeps moving that money into the Economy via Government Spending...

...Sooner or later, Currency Inflation will become a problem. And we've all been taught to fear the Spectre of Currency Inflation, right..?

We all know about Germans in the Weimar Republic using wheelbarrows to move the amount of cash needed to buy a loaf of bread down to the bakers... Only to have another Weimar German tip out all of the cash and steal the wheelbarrow.

So...

The most effective way to withdraw the money the Government created and moved into the Economy, is to use taxation to remove a sufficient quantity to prevent currency inflation.

Simples.

So what does a government *DO* with the taxes it collects?

Nothing.

What's done is done, *IN SPENDING*.

A dollar the government takes - the number it transfers *FROM* the Economy - in taxes, cancels out a dollar previously issued - the number it previously transferred *TO* the Economy.

The result is Zero.

The dollar disappears, is zeroed out.

That's how taxes control inflation.

When it comes to governments spending the money they create, the "disciplining" economic consideration for avoiding inflation, is to assure that the amount of money the government creates, correlates with the *VALUE* that Economic Activity in Society creates.

It is an error to confuse money, which only the Government can create, with Value, which is created in the Social Economy.

Monetary policy should be designed to facilitate the exchange of values produced, and encourage the production of new value.

Money circulates as a lubricating oil in the gears of the productive economy: too much, and it floods the processes; too little, and the processes seize up.

Think about Corporate "Loyalty Schemes", like "Frequent Flier Airmiles".

Frequent Flier miles aren't anything but numbers, points in a game created and controlled by the airline.

Delta (for example) can generate as many points as it wants. But the amount of points that Delta issues should be sensibly correlated with the amount of seats in the planes that Delta is operating.

The airline company doesn't "collect" those points from anywhere, and it cannot run out of them.

Any conditions on who receives them and how, are entirely Delta's decision, subject to revision in accordance with Delta's corporation policy imperatives at any time.

And who needs 50 billion frequent flier miles?

Delta can put any limit it wants on the points an individual can collect, in order to assure a fair and rational system that won't piss of its customers, and gum up its own business operations...

...by allowing a few families to book up 90% of the seats on every flight that Delta operates.

Any government can control its "points" - dollars, pound, euros &c. - at least as much as Delta does, and it should.

[b]Debt Sealing[/b]

So let's talk about Government Deficits: that great big Bogey-Monster that we're all programmed to fear and despise, in spite of the fact that very few of us understand what a Government Deficit is.

Here's the key point:

The Government's loss is the Economy's gain.

The government's deficit is equal, *TO THE PENNY*, to our surplus: the amount of dollars the government has created, spent into and *LEFT* in the Economy.

The government's deficit is "our" net savings after taxes: dollars that haven't been zeroed out; points that we can use.

As [url=https://www.opednews.com/articles/Paul-Ryan-He-s-the-Fiscal-by-Robert-Bostick-Congress_Fiat_Government_Paul-Craig-Roberts-161028-837.html]Robert Bostick puts it:[/url]

[quote]It's essentially interest-free money for us to spend as we choose. That's why the Financial Sector/Commercial Bankers hate deficit spending. Americans benefit from deficit spending and therefore, don't need to go into debt to maintain living standards.[/quote]

This is why "austerity" policies are ridiculous, self-defeating and immensely harmful.

The last thing we want is for the government to have a "balanced budget" policy, let alone for the government to run a surplus: because that would be *OUR* deficit.

For a healthy, growing and/or recovering Economy, a government should avoid retrieving as much in taxes as it has spent into the economy. And there is no economic need to so, or to borrow money to make up the difference.

The government spends more than it gets back? So what? Some dollars haven't been zeroed out. To whom does it owe those zeros?

So there you go. I think that's probably enough to be getting on with for now.

A government that can (and does) create its own money can never run out of money, can it?

If you could create your own money out of thin air, you would never run out of money, would you?

But you can't create your own money.

Governments can.

Governments can create and spend as much money as it is politically inclined to do.

The health of an Economy that a Government is responsible for stewarding, is fully dependent (to the penny) on how much created money that Government chooses to withdraw from the Economy via taxation.

It is as simple as that.
