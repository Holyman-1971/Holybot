It costs money to construct a building. Design, material and labour costs have to be paid. The same as any other manufactured commodity. However, unlike the majority of other manufactured commodities, once built, the on-going value of a building continues to rise and fall, often with little or no connection to the original cost price.

And contrary to popular opinion, a building's location doesn't have a lot to do with price fluctuations either.

A geographic location's desirability or fashionableness is not something that changes suddenly or regularly. It takes a shitty area quite a while to become a desirable area, and vice versa.

Once a building is constructed, its location is fixed. And given the relatively slow moving tides of location fashion, the value that a particular location contributes to the overall worth of a building also changes relatively slowly. A building in London's Chelsea, New York's Manhattan, or Sydney's Eastern Suburbs, is always going to carry a high location premium. Not much short of radiological contamination is going to cause a sudden or precipitous decline in property values in those areas.

And at the other end of the scale, whilst the gentrification of once shitty working-class suburbs into something fashionable enough to convince the upper middle-classes to relocate is the property developer's dream, it is a long and uncertain process.

For all intents and purposes, property location, like property size and form, is a price fixture, not a variable.

Another misunderstanding about property prices is that they are substantially affected by the dynamics of Supply & Demand.

Everyone needs a home to live in. And generally speaking, population size only changes in a single direction: Growth. And tsunamis, earthquakes and volcanoes notwithstanding, the amount of land available to build on also, generally speaking, only increases.

And all those property developers and construction companies, well, generally speaking, they like to keep themselves busy by building as many properties as they can too. The individual or organisation who builds a property only makes money of the property once: when they sell it. So they generally like to keep building additional properties, wherever they can.

Oh, they might vary the concentration of individual properties within a single property development, in order to attract the right kind of buyers and peg exclusivity at the right kind of level for those prospective buyers. But they do not *NOT* build new properties, because they don't want to diminish the residual value of properties they have already built - and sold.

So, demand for residential properties is consistent and predictable, and supply is rarely, if ever, subject to restriction, artificial or otherwise.

So what is the primary determining factor for property prices?

It is, in fact: [b]*THE AMOUNT OF MONEY THAT PURCHASERS ARE ABLE TO OFFER*[/b].

Along with Art, exotic motor-cars and boats, fashion and jewellery, property purchases are subject to huge Emotional factors. This is because when one purchases any of the aforementioned, what is in fact being purchased is not the commodity itself, but something far more valuable:

Social Status.

The kind of building you live in, the type of vehicle you have parked in your driveway or marina, the fashions with which you decorate your home and body, broadcasts to the World an accepted message about your personal success in Life.

And many people are prepared to pay any cost, to underwrite the impressions of success that they communicate to their fellow Human Beings.

So, when somebody locks onto the idea of possessing a certain property they have identified as being capable of broadcasting the message they would like Society to receive about them, rationality goes out of the window, and possession of said property becomes an obsession.

At this point, the objective of owning a Home You Can Call Your Own, is displaced by the objective of being able to advertise to the World, the measure of your success, ability and status. It becomes a matter of scraping together all of the cash that you can collect, and making the seller an offer that they can't refuse. *ANYTHING* to get your hands on that commodity that will symbolise to the World, just what a great fucking person you truly are...

It's all relative of course. It's relative to the possessions and perceived status of the people you are trying to impress. If your peers aren't ultra-wealthy bankers that own whole islands, then you're not going to get upset about your inability to own an island of your own. But if your peers own properties that *ARE* conceivably or feasibly within your ability to match or exceed, then that is what you will aim for. Nothing less will do.

So now, it just comes down to your ability to gather together sufficient funds to make that offer that isn't, or can't be refused.

Now who has that kind of cash lying around?

Chances are: not you.

So you're going to have to borrow it from someone.

Now somehow, someway, somewhere along the way, it became the accepted belief that Money-Lenders are a benevolent breed, providing an invaluable and vital social service. It's why when you apply for a mortgage... You *APPLY*.

Let's realise that a mortgage is likely to be the greatest and most frightening financial transaction you are ever likely to enter into in your Lifetime. You are going to sign-up to borrow a *LOT* (relatively speaking) of money; which will take most of your Lifetime to repay; and on which you are going to pay a *FUCK* of a lot of money in interest.

And let's be very, very clear on that last point: [b]YOU ARE GOING TO PAY A *FUCK* OF A LOT OF MONEY IN INTEREST[/b].

Let's illustrate it:

Let's say you borrow 200,000 (in your currency of choice), over 25 years, at a 5% interest.

Your monthly repayment will be 1,182.54.

You will repay a total of 354,762.

Over 25 years, you will pay 154,762, for the privilege of being allowed to borrow 200,000.

And that's assuming the interest rate remains fixed at 5% for 25 years.

If you borrow 200,000 (in your currency of choice), over 25 years, at 10% interest:

Your monthly repayment will be 1,836.13.

You will repay a total of 550,839.

So over 25 years, you will pay 350,839, for the privilege of being allowed to borrow 200,000.

But it is *YOU* that has to apply; and it is *YOU* that has to be accepted by the Money-Lender.

Despite the fact that it is you that is going to be funding the Money-Lender for the next 25 years, it is *YOU* that is on the back-foot from the beginning of the transaction. It is *YOU* that will be penalised if you don't keep up your repayments. And it is *YOU* who is made to feel like the recipient of a favour.

Now as a further illustration, let's say that instead of buying the (£/$/?)200,000 house, and paying 1,182.54 per month at 5% interest for 25 years, you [b]*RENT*[/b] a property for 600 per month. And that is a completely typical ration between mortgage repayment & rental price for similar properties.

You are now living in the same kind of property, but you are spending 500 less per month to do so.

So take that 500 every month, and stick it in a long-term, 25 year investment scheme, that guarantees a minimum annual interest rate, also of 5%.

After 25 years, your 500 a month will have created a nest-egg of...

297,754.85.

Enough to buy a 200,000 house outright, and with enough spare to decorate and furnish it.

Furthermore, financial institutions will be applying to *YOU* for the privilege of investing *YOUR* money. If interest rates go up, you will end up with even *MORE* money. You stand no chance of losing a property you have invested your Life into, because you never pretended to own a property in the first place (you don't own a property you bought using mortgaged funds, until you finish paying the mortgage).

And lest you might think this is all some arcane, improvable, risk-riddled scheme for long-term home ownership: understand that, in fact, this *IS* how things operate in most of the non-Anglo-Saxon World. It is how most Europeans that don't live in the British Isles do it. It is how it is done throughout most of Asia (except Japan), South America and Africa. And it is how it *MUST* be done in the Islamic World, where usury is a Koranic prohibition.

It really is as simple as this. No hidden catches; no unforeseeable factors; no long-term risks.

Over the long-term, the value of a property *MIGHT* experience appreciation over and above currency and retail price inflation. But equally, at any point during that term, there is a very good chance that a property will lose value. Alright if you're in it for the long-term, but if you need to move unexpectedly, or if there comes a point where you can't keep up your repayments, that can create quite a few problems.

By contrast, whilst a savings or investment scheme might occasionally not perform as well as expected, earned interest can only ever reduce, not turn negative. And your capital will always remain inviolable.

So having established that mortgage purchases are more or less a complete con, with the risk being almost exclusively saddled to the borrower, let's also consider how the provision of credit impacts the general value of property.

The *ONLY* reason why property prices have spiked so dramatically over the last decade, in the UK, US, Australia, Spain and other regions, is because Money-Lenders have been falling over themselves to sign people up to long-term mortgages.

Because it became so much easier to borrow money, it became much easier for prospective purchasers to make those offers that couldn't be refused by property sellers.

When property prices are booming, Money-Lenders see mortgage lending as a risk-free operation. If the person taking the mortgage fails to keep up with the repayments, then the Money-Lenders take possession of the property and sell it on for more than the original amount of money loaned. This is, in fact, how they sold the mortgages to the wary buyer in the first place:

[i]"Don't worry, if you can't keep up the repayments, just sell the house, pay back the mortgage, and you'll probably make a little money on the whole transaction anyway."[/i]
