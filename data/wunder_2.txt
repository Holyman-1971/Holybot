The way I see it, Wunder is upholding a fine British comedic tradition of mocking [b]*EVERYTHING*[/b].

See a Sacred Cow: Shoot it!

It's exactly the same sort of thing that Lenny Bruce and Bill Hicks did, as well as the likes of Peter Cook, Billy Connolly and Rob Newman.

It's just that the particular "sacred cows" that Wunder is going after in this instance, is the sacrosanct topic of dead Americans.

It isn't a question of "choosing sides"; and some could consider how their plaintive crys of distress about who's on who's side, is pretty much the epitome of what Wunder is talking about.

Wunder isn't disrespecting anyone's personal grief here.

I couldn't guarantee it, but I'd put money on the fact that if anyone on this forum related to us how a close friend or relative had died in Iraq or Afghanistan, Wunder wouldn't be so callous as to keep at this particular line of comedic reasoning.

But no-one is expressing indignation about a personal event in their lives; rather they are indignant at Wunder's disrespect for an event they have [b]*MADE*[/b] personal: the deaths (generally) of U.S. Service personnel.

And it is the individual act of [b]*MAKING*[/b] that subject personal to one's Self, that Wunder is attempting to puncture, for those still encased in that particular bubble.

Wunder isn't saying anything different to what I talk about.

He's just saying it more directly, much more humorously and much less ambiguously.

This particular subject isn't personal: it is Nationalist politics.

But as far as several Americans around here are concerned: that is the same thing.
