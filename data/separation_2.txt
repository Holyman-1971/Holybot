At first I thought that you were saying the solution will only come when everyone changes the way they think about the world and each other, creating unity. I was referring to a change of mental attitude rather than a change of ideas or beliefs JF.

Specifically, I was talking about changing our attitude towards each other, about changing the attitude that we might and could exist [i]separately[/i] from one another. And that isn't so much an ideology or belief system, as much as it is a mindset.

To me, it isn't about changing our beliefs and ideas about God, science, money, the Climate, morality, war, nationalism, or whatever. The diversity of human thought on those subjects is what gives our Society its substance and vibrancy. I think we can and should keep all those ideas and beliefs that no-one has yet managed to persuade us to relinquish.

But it [b]IS[/b] about changing our attitudes that we don't need or can live without those who disagree with us.

It is the attitude that tells us we can mentally dismiss or marginalise those who disagree with us; or, in the case of our worst disagreements, destroy them altogether. We believe that, eventually, we can either ignore or eliminate everyone who disagrees with our way of thinking. And indeed, the only thing that is preventing us from enjoying a perfectly Peaceful and prosperous Existence, is the fact that we haven't yet successfully destroyed everyone, intellectually, politically or physically, who disagrees with us.

We expend a tremendous amount of our energy trying to establish the supremacy of one set of ideas over another. The sense is that we must first prove ourselves more right than anyone else, so that The Decision can then be taken to follow our idea, and our idea alone.

But this ignores the obvious fact that we [b]NEED[/b] people who disagree with us, in order to strengthen our own ideas.

Look at our Community here at =PBF=. There isn't one single prevailing political, religious or social ideology around here, there are many.

And the Substance of =PBF= is the dynamic [b]BETWEEN[/b] those different political, social, religious and moral ideologies. Without disagreement, debate, argument and even a little discord from time to time, there wouldn't be much =PBF= to write at home about.

So for example, in my dialogue with say Foota, rather than my aim being to come up with that one, single, killer fact that silences him for once and for all - or worse: converts him completely to [b]*MY*[/b] way of thinking! - I actually benefit from the dynamic tension between the two of us. If nothing else, it is good mental exercise; but it also causes me to strengthen and develop my ideas, and more thoroughly research them. I may choose to not learn a great deal about Foota's point of view or ideology; but I make damn sure that I thoroughly understand the views and ideas that I am a proponent of.

Now what opportunity for that kind of growth, if Everyone I met just agreed with me?

I [b]NEED[/b] other people to disagree with me; just as the kind of debates that Humanity has to have with itself, needs people to disagree with one another.

Now, I know we don't have any problem disagreeing with each other; either here at =PBF=, or in the World at Large. :)

But I think that we [b]DO[/b] have a problem recognising or remembering just how vital it is to ourselves, that we can always find people who do disagree with us.

See, the form that political dialogue takes, either at the national level in our Parliaments and Congress, or at the international level such as at Copenhagen recently, is one where the motivation is for one party or delegation to dominate and silence those other parties that disagree with them.

Here at =PBF=, that is the kind of behaviour that is most keenly exhibited by your brother.

In actual fact, our strength as a Collective comes [b]FROM[/b] our disagreements with each other; provided those disagreements remain respectful, civilised, and most importantly of all, non violent.

[quote=JFitch][color=darkred]The issue I have with that, though, is that the diversity of beliefs about what is and is not truth is a barrier to coming to a consensus on what the problems are that we are facing, and what we should do about a solution.[/quote][/color]

I think that is only a problem if we are all absolutely certain that there can only ever be a single attempt to find a single solution to a particular problem. Or if we are convinced that we already have found the solution to a problem, and we just need others to stop trying to solve it their way, in order for us to be able to get on with solving it our way.

Perhaps it is because we feel we are restricted by funding. Perhaps we feel that there simply isn't enough money or resource to attempt multiple solutions simultaneously to try and resolve some of our greatest challenges. Perhaps we have been trained to believe that we need to compete for funding, in order to research or implement solutions, so we need to first establish the supremacy of our solutions over anyone else's, in order to secure that funding.

Or perhaps it is just that we want to be Right about our solution (particularly if we do get awarded the funding), and we don't want to run the risk of anyone else implementing a more successful solution before ours has had a chance to work.

Yes, I'm pretty sure that last is probably what it is.

Nobody seemed to worry too much about finding the $15 trillion needed for the "solution" to the recent banking crisis. Even though everyone understood that the "solution" was only a temporary fix at best, our governments came up with that $15 trillion in pretty short order.

So the arguments about who is going to come up with a $100 billion to try and tackle (/research further) Climate Change, can't really be about the cash, can they? $100 billion (to potentially save the species) is a drop in the rising oceans, compared to the amount of money found to (temporarily) keep the bankers afloat.

No. It's only common sense that we try out as many different proposed solutions as we can. After all, a solution is only a Solution, once we know it has worked.

Arguing about what we think is most likely to work is one thing; but we seem to put most of our energy into arguing about what cannot possibly work, either because a certain solution is too expensive, impractical, or just plain unnecessary.

The fact is that for good and ill, we ARE all in this together. We are all part of the same species, living on the same Planet, one way and another, for one reason and another.

We expend more of our Energy trying to prove each other wrong, than we do trying to prove ourselves Right. And there is a subtle but very important distinction there.

If we can all adopt the attitude that we are working together, even and especially when it seems like we are all working in our own separate streams of activity, then I think we'll get much closer to securing the Peaceful and Sustainable Future that, ultimately, We All Want.
