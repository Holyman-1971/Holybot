I understand your point. But to me, it's like taking an RPG(role playing game - things could get confusing with the whole rocket propelled granade thing) element and putting it into BF. It's hard to get new people into the franchise if they catch on a bit late and they see dudes on servers who not only are better because they have played the game longer, but they have a better weapon. I don't like fact that player can have a weapon advantage, however slight it may be.

I think that's an absolutely crucial point Eric.

The last big On-line Game I played before BF2 was Earth & Beyond (a space-based MMORPG for those unfamiliar). The reason why that much-loved Game was terminated by EA was pretty much entirely down to it being hi-jacked by a hard-core of lock-ins, who played it for hours and hours and hours, and skewed the interal Game dynamic so that any new player would find it impossible to participate on an equal or even practical basis.

The bottom line is that On-line Games are commercial operations, and are intended to appeal to as wide a base of players (payers) as possible: more players = more $$$. To translate incessant and possibly terminally long Game-playing careers into a significant in-Game advantage would be to create a barrier-to-entry for new players.

Let's say that after x,000 hours of Game-Play you get promoted to Uber-Obersturmbannfuhrer, and unlock the Mega-wattage-ultra-kills-r-us-Sniper Rifle. Only very, very few players are going to put in the length and density of play-hours required to get to that level. However, those few, rather than concentrating together on single, Uber-servers for the low-on-social life, where they can combat players at their own level, are going to distribute their selves amongst the general mass of casual gamers, where the pickings are easy, and they don't run the risk of bumping into players of their own level, who might stand a chance of spoiling their fun. New players then, joining their first BF2 server, will find their selves being spawn-killed instantly, as the Uber-Obersturmbannfuhrer shoots his prized unlock from 1000m away.

Basically this kind of system would create a whole load of E-Bullies - and no-one likes a Bully, certainly no-one likes playing with a Bully.

Of course the E-Bully might suggest that if everyone worked together, they could overcome him, and that he was in fact adding a more challenging dimension to the Game. But this kind of cognitive dissonance is just a way of denying to their selves that their main aim in playing the Game - and in ascending through the ranks - is simply to have their ego's stroked as frequently as possible. Nothing would please them more than to have a dozen noobies quaking at the sight of their connecting to the server; or having the entire dynamic of the Game focused on dealing with them � ask PlaneWhore.

The irony is, of course, that most of those willing to put the time into an RPG that is required to become an E-Bully, are most likely victims of bullying in their off-line life. As clear an example of the abused becoming the abuser as any.

Whilst the E-Bully makes Gameplay miserable (sorry - "Challenging") for noobs, they will console their selves, like a Drill Sergeant, that those who survive this "training" will become better and more proficient players. But what they forget is that this simply doesn't fit with the cost model that the suits and bean-counters that put up the capital to operate the Game, have in mind. They would like as many people as possible to play (pay for) the Game, and if word gets out that the presence of Uber-Players/E-Bullies make enjoyment of the Game impossible, noobies will just ignore the Game and choose something else.

In EA's experience, this is precisely what happened with Earth & Beyond, Motor City Online, and to a less dynamic extent, with the Sims Online. But it is something that is prevalent in many, if not all On-Line RPG�s.

At the end of the day, Battlefield 2 suffers slightly from something of an identity crisis. It straddles several genre-definitions, from twitch-shooter thru squad-shooter to RPG. This in turn attracts a variety of players that each favour those different aspects of the Game, and who all, almost without exception believe that the future development of the Game should be directed towards their own particular preference.

The different flavours of the post-patch discussions on this and other BF2 forums undoubtedly reflects this.
