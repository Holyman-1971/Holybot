Well, I think SomeGuy - a living, breathing argument in favour of REALLY late term abortions if ever there was one - pretty much validated the point I was making Foota, with his response: it's not about the cause, it's about the fight.

See, everyone I know, and that includes everyone who has posted in this thread, sees abortion as a very difficult, very unpleasant, very emotional and deeply serious issue. No-one I know or have heard from treats the issue lightly, save perhaps for the likes of SomeGuy who just use it as another brickbat in their Pavlovian diatribes.

Abortion [b]IS[/b] a serious moral issue. Abortion [b]IS[/b] a deeply challenging social question. And Abortion is something that cannot be lightly discussed.

Anyone who has been through the deeply personal situation of having to consider an abortion will say what an enormously difficult experience it is to go through, regardless of which decision they ultimately made. No-one is seriously suggesting that morality has no position in the Abortion debate, or that the views of the morally-minded should be discarded as thoughtlessly as the morally-minded imagine people who support a woman's right to choose would discard an unborn foetus.

And it is childish and counter-productive to suggest or behave otherwise.

My point was not "Abortion is Great! Hooray!!", nor was it "Abortion is MURDER!!!": my actual point was that the Abortion "debate" is unwinnable.

Abortion will always and [b]MUST[/b] always be morally questionable. It can never be allowed to be a procedure that is used freely or relied upon lightly. And to the best of my knowledge, no-one is suggesting that it is.

But we also know what Life was like - and in many areas of the World, still is like - before women had access to institutions where they can get a legal, medically safe and psychologically counselled abortion procedure.

It isn't about establishing whether abortion is "Right" or "Wrong": we all already know it is [b]ALWAYS[/b] wrong when a woman finds herself in the position of having to decide whether to terminate a pregnancy or not. In an Ideal, Civilised, Spiritual Society, such a situation should never arise.

But we don't yet live in an Ideal, Civilised and Spiritualised Society, and there are sadly too many occasions when individual women find themselves having to decide whether to carry a pregnancy to full term.

And frankly, it isn't anyone's business but the woman's what they decide. It is completely bizarre that so-called "Conservatives" in the U.S., who allegedly believe in small and unobtrusive government, believe that such a deeply personal decision should be made for the woman at the highest levels of government. It is impossible to conceive of a way in which a government could behave more intrusively.

No, my point was that certain individuals like SomeGuy, and factions of particular political bent use the abortion "Debate" as a means of firing up and maintaining a fighting stance. No clear consensus can ever emerge on the Abortion debate, since abortion will always be as morally wrong as it will always be an absolute social necessity. You just can't square the circle.

The "Debate" is used as a way of firing people up; of getting people to choose sides; of painting people into rhetorical corners; or maintaining moral outrage and indignation at a simmering point that obviates rational sociological discourse.

It gives unfortunately un-aborted foetuses like SomeGuy the opportunity to go on a rhetorical rampage, with a degree of moral certainty that is missing from most of the other Right-Wing canards.

And it makes the World feel like the kind of place that no-one in their right mind would want to bring up kids in.
