The issue is one of social sustainability, and indeed, Social Stability.

Forget morality. Morality is just a question of perspective.

Chances are that if an when people move into the top 1%, their view will be that disparities in wealth are just an unavoidable and possibly unfortunate side-effect of "The Only System That Works".

And of course, should someone fall precipitously *OUT* of the top 1%, then they may well change their view about the righteousness of so much wealth being stacked at the top of the socio-economic pyramid...

No, it's a practical issue about just how long a Society can continue to function peacefully, before obvious imbalances begin to upset the Apple Cart.

The problem with wealth is that it is a Zero Sum Game. In order for someone to have lots of it, someone else has to have less of it.

Wealth isn't the same as money. Money is just a conceptual device, not really rooted in reality anymore, and subject to all manner of abstracts that affect its value. But wealth is a relative term.

If Everyone was wealthy, then no-one would be wealthy. If no-one was poor, then Everyone would be poor.

So, the more wealth that accumulates at the top, the less wealth there is to go around at the bottom. And since you pay the same price for basic commodity goods whether you are wealthy or poor, this means that as prices go up because the wealthy can afford to pay them, basic necessities become unaffordable for the non-wealthy.

And it's around about then that the non-wealthy start getting angry.

And telling them that they should just eat cake, doesn't make them feel any less angry.
