Was it to eliminate a WMD threat? Was it to liberate Iraqis from Saddam's rule? Was it to remove a threat to the Israelis? To remove a threat to the Saudis? Was it a prerequisite for an attack on Iran?

Was it to secure access to and control of Iraq's energy resources?

Both countries possess a WMD capability; the North Korean population is more miserably oppressed than Iraqis ever were; and Pakistan is a more unstable Islamic regime than Iran ever could be.

We are asked to believe that had Iraq been an island nation in the Indian Ocean, whose chief export was bananas, then the 2003 Invasion would still have gone ahead.

Iraq's massive oil reserves simply can't be excised from the equation. Not just from the run-up to the Invasion, but from sixty years of American Policy towards the Middle East. To try and ignore America's thirst for oil when considering its policies and activities in the Middle East is just going to make your brain hurt.

But it is also startlingly obvious that if Iraq did not have the reserves of sweet crude oil that it does, then the Invasion [b]DEFINITELY[/b] would not have taken place.

There isn't any conspiracy theorising here, or hippie-fied counter-culturing going on: there is no need for that.

The Neo-Conservatives expressed their desire with startling clarity in their "Project for a New American Century" manifesto that securing (for U.S. interests) Middle Eastern oil supplies was a critical strategic objective. The most hawkish American politicians who supported the Invasion, very openly stated that the Invasion would/could be paid for by Iraqi oil revenues.

To discount or dismiss America's massive dependency on imported oil, when considering its policies towards and activities in the Middle East is to engage in considerable self-deception.

It really isn't an attempt to justify their crap Vaxx.

Nor is it even an attempt to sympathise with them either. Rather, it is an attempt to understand the considerable animosity that exists towards the West, in the Middle East.

It has to be a basic tenet of military or diplomatic strategy to try and understand the motivations of one's enemy or opposition.

Rolling out canards like "They Hate Us For Our Freedoms" doesn't help matters at all. It reduces to cartoon caricatures, the individuals who pose a real threat to our lives. And that doesn't aid us in drawing the situation to a stable, peaceful and long-term solution.

I'm a Pacifist Vaxx, as you know, but I'm also a pragmatist. I accept that there is nothing I can personally do to halt the violence projected by all sides of the Human Race, in the conflicts that revolve around access to energy resources and material wealth. Not in the short-term anyway.

If I thought for an instance that Peace really could be achieved through "Superior Firepower", you know what? I would support it.

I don't support Western military violence because of a moral objection, but because clearly, patently, that violence is ineffective and undeniably counter-productive.

What weapons exist in the Western military arsenal that are capable of achieving the long-term objective of a Peaceful, stable and secure Middle East? Certainly none that have been deployed to date.

So the Hawks say, "We must try harder. We must be more resolute, more committed, more united." But all the while, our lives and the lives of our families are threatened by those intent on using their own violence to try and dissuade us from supporting our governments' objectives and activities.

The object of the exercise then is to arrive at a situation where we can ALL live in Peace and security. If the military strategy is not working, or is not working quickly enough, then what other strategies are available to us? Are there any other strategies that are available that don't actually mean halting the current military strategies?

Of course there are. And one of those strategies is trying to understand what motivates and fuels the actions of those engaged in terrorist activity. Discerning and discussing those motivations is NOT the same as justifying them, nor excusing the actions that arise from those motivations.

I don't believe that I am giving "Aid & Comfort" to those people who could just as likely kill a member of my family, as they might kill a member of yours, just because I am trying to get inside their heads to understand why they behave the way they do.

I don't really have a problem with the idea of America or the U.K. using its military to secure access to Middle Eastern energy supplies. What I have a problem with is the deliberate dishonesty that surrounds those efforts. By trying to deceive ourselves about the true nature of our conduct in the Middle East, we are preventing ourselves from ever reaching the stated objective of securing Peace and stability for our societies.

It is [b]OUR[/b] military forces who are in [b]THEIR[/b] nations, clearly supporting [b]OUR[/b] business interests and [b]OUR[/b] thirst for Energy.

The idea that it is just a coincidence that the places in the World we are engaged in Humanitarian Intervention and Counter-Terrorism just [i]happens[/i] to be where the Energy supplies we are wholly dependent upon, is a gross act of self-deception, which is ultimately self-harming.

The way I see it, we have only two options:

The first is just to be honest with ourselves. To admit that our societies are addicted to oil, and that we will use our military and economic superiority to appropriate the oil from those people whose territories the oil happens to be located on.

With such a clear and honest objective, our military forces can get on with the job at hand and achieve what is asked of them.

The second option is to put our efforts into weaning ourselves off our addiction to oil: reduce our consumption and develop renewable Energy sources, thereby reducing our dependency on Middle Eastern oil supplies and our business interests in the region.

It should be blindingly obvious to us by now that continuing to project our military force into the Middle East, under the self-deceiving notion that we are trying to do the people of the Middle East a favour, is just a recipe for more of the same long-term instability and insecurity.

We ourselves reject any attempt by any other society to interfere in our affairs; why should we imagine the people of the Middle East feel any differently?
