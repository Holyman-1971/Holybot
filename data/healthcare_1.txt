It isn't.

As far as I am concerned, any Society that wants to call itself Civilised [b]*SHOULD*[/b] provide those resources and services essential for Life, on a means tested basis, free of charge to those that are in need of them.

My original question asks is it appropriate to financially profit from another human being's [b]*NEED*[/b] for medical treatment.

Clearly I would answer that no. The same goes for food, energy and water: I do not think it is appropriate to [b]*FINANCIALLY PROFIT*[/b] from another human being's [b]*NEED*[/b] for life essentials.

Now if an individual, like you or I, is capable of securing those essentials for themselves, whatever the cost, then they have no [b]*NEED*[/b] for those essentials, because they already have them. At this point, if competing healthcare companies want to offer those services on a commercial basis, with value-added and profit gained, that's just fine: I'll pick and choose where I wish to spend my money, and which company I'm going to enrich.

Same for food, energy and water: I'm rich enough to buy fine foods, consume energy in a luxurious fashion, and drink bottled mineral water - my lavatory also functions efficiently.

But what if I couldn't afford those resources at a basic, essential level? What if I really, really [b]*NEEDED*[/b] them?

How would I feel about the fact that I couldn't have them, unless I was able to ensure someone else profited financially from my need?
