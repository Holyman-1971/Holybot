The amount of goods being delivered by road in the UK is obviously considerably less in total, than in the United States. As well as having fewer consumers, there are also less road miles that goods can actually be hauled over. However, that also means that it is more difficult for the many independent road haulage companies to pass on the additional costs to their customers, than is possible in the U.S. Only last week, Central London was blockaded by many independent road haulage contractors protesting at the amount of tax levied on fuel purchases by the U.K. government, and similar scenes are playing out across the continent.

The U.K. Government can no more afford to cut fuel duty revenues, than British retailers can afford to increase their prices in the current economic climate.

As for the individual car owner, well, on the whole, European engine sizes are on average much smaller than American equivalents; and fuel economy is generally much greater. A typical, modern European family car is expected to get 40 - 50 miles out of a gallon of petrol, and more out of a gallon of diesel. Compare that with the average American SUV, and you can see why the impacts of fuel price increase, whilst serious for Europeans, is nowhere near as catastrophic as it is for the average American.
