Umm, yes, it probably should bother you a bit.

Your Government runs up its debt by selling debt instruments like Treasury Bills and Treasury Notes, which are bought primarily by other National Governments - particularly the Chinese at the moment - as well as Sovereign Investment Funds (Financial Investment Vehicles belonging to National Governments), and to a lesser extent, private investors.

As of the end of last month, the value of these debt instruments held by foreign investors had just sneaked passed the $10 trillion mark.

This means that every citizen (taxpayer, non-taxpayers, children etc.) owes just under $33,000 to foreigners.

But the true figure of your National Debt is the amount of spending that your Government has already committed to paying, on top of any debt instruments it has issued.

If you add in unfunded Medicaid, Social Security, Medicare and similar obligations, this figure rises to a little under $60 trillion - which is just over half a million dollars owed by each American household.

To put the scale of National Debt into a perspective you [b]*MIGHT*[/b] be able to relate to, try to think of your country's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) as an individual person's salary; and then the amount of debt owed as a percentage of that annual salary.

In this case, for the "Public Debt" (the amount owed on debt instruments issued by the Government), the figure is about 37% of GDP - a little over a third of your annual income; and total debt (of unfunded obligations), is around 66% of GDP.

And of course, if GDP (salary) falls - and it does in a recession or depression - the percentage of debt to income will rise.

OK, but debt is manageable right? After all, who are foreign investors going to send to the U.S. to "Collect"?

Well, unfortunately, as with all debt, in addition to a requirement to (eventually) repay the Principle Amount; there is also the matter of interest that needs paying.

The payment of interest on U.S. Public Debt is the 3rd highest expenditure in the Federal Budget - after "Defence" and Social Security.

For the Fiscal Year End 2007, the U.S. Government paid just under $430 billion, just to pay the interest on the Public Debt.

http://www.treasurydirect.gov/govt/reports/ir/ir_expense.htm

$430 Billion equates to about $3,300 per U.S. Taxpayer.

It also equates to more than the total Government spending of each national government outside of the G7, and is the same as the total annual budget expenditure of France, Italy and Canada, and only a little less than the total annual budget expenditure of the U.K. and Germany.

And all of those countries fund a National Health Service from within those budgets...

So even if you don't worry too much about the outstanding Principle; you might want to dream about what your Government could spend [b]*YOUR*[/b] tax dollars on, if it wasn't spending nearly half a trillion dollars every year, on interest payments alone.
