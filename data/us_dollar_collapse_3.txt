In 1945, the economy of the British Empire was still the [b]*LARGEST*[/b] in the World; but its status was in terminal decline. It was neither strong or healthy. By contrast, at the same time, the economy of the United States was still relatively small in proportion, but it was getting larger, stronger and healthier with each passing day.

That situation is more or less a simulcrum of the relationship between the economies of the U.S. and China today.

The U.S. is still the largest economy in the World by a considerable margin - with a nominal GDP approaching $14 trillion; compared to China's nominal GDP of (only) $3 trillion. However, the Chinese Economy is experiencing double-digit growth that is expected to continue for the forseeable future; and has financial surpluses across the board in trade, budget and current account terms; whereas the United States is struggling to stay out of recession, has record-beating deficits in its trade, budget and current accounts, and has a National Debt ($10 trillion) that is fast approaching the same figure as its GDP.

One of the best ways to try and make sense of Global Economics is to view national economies as personal/individual economies. So to explain the above in more familiar terms:

Mr. United States has an annual income of nearly $14 trillion.

However, Mr. U.S. also has personal debts amounting to more than $9 trillion, and is adding to this by borrowing $1.5 billion [b]*A DAY*[/b].

Mr. U.S. is spending more than he is earning; is buying far more than he is selling; and perhaps most significantly of all, has close to [b]*$60 TRILLION*[/b] of un-presented (un-cashed) cheques being traded amongst other people.

(This last is the amount of US Treasury Dollar-denominated instruments and scrips held in Foreign Reserves: as the Dollar goes into decline, more and more holders of these Dollar "cheques" will present them for en-cashing, and the Federal Reserve will suddenly have to deal with all the dud-cheques it has "written" in the last six or seven years.)

By contrast, Mr. China, on the other side of the Pacific Street, has an annual income of only $3 trillion dollars.

However, Mr. China has little or no personal debt to speak of, and in fact, is a net lender of money to, amongst other people, Mr. U.S. over the road. Mr. China is not making any interest payments on a daily basis, but is in fact [b]*RECEIVING*[/b] interest payments on a daily basis: mostly from Mr. America.

Furthermore, whilst Mr. America's income is beginning to shrink, and his "job" is looking increasingly redundant; Mr. China's earnings are going from strength to strength, and look set to overtake his neighbour, Mr. U.S.A., within the next 5 to 10 years, just as he has recently overtaken his other neighbours, Mr. U.K., Mr. France, Mr. Italy and Mr. Russia.

Now all of these guys are doing the best they can to appeal to investors. Apart from Mr. China (the PRC does not allow its currency to be traded internationally), all the other guys write cheques out to other people too, and hope that those cheques stay in circulation for as long as possible, before they are presented for cashing.

They all (apart from Mr. China) want other investors to feel positive about their future prospects, and the robustness of their financial circumstances.

And [b]*THIS*[/b] is because the entire Capitalist System they are all engaged in, is based on confidence.

If investors are confident in Mr. China's ability to make them money, they will give Mr. China more of their money. The more money that Mr. China receives, the more he is able to develop his situation positively, by using that money to invest in the things he needs to do his job more effectively. The more effectively he does his job, the more confidence others have in him, and the more money they will invest with him. The more money invested with Mr. China, the more effectively he can do his job.

And so the positive chain reaction rolls onwards and upwards.

The flip side of that is what happens when investors lose confidence, as they have done with Mr. America. Then, it becomes a negative chain reaction.
