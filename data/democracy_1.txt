Alright, let's see if I can clarify my point so as not to offer any insult, direct or implied.

In a Democracy, the idea is that We, The People (or [i]demos[/i]) govern ourselves.

In order to do that practically, we elect representatives from amongst ourselves to fulfil the onerous responsibilities of governance, of governing ourselves.

However, in practice, when we elect our representatives, we send them into a second, elite tier of social existence, generically called "Politics". And as we all know, politicians are very rarely, if ever, representative of real Society, or real Humans.

The very act of sending them into the social crucible we know as "Government and Politics", is to consign those who are supposed to represent us, to the full-time occupation of self-preservation amidst a social strata of power-hungry elites.

In other words, those individuals we elect to represent us in government spend most of their time trying to protect and preserve themselves and their own self-interest; and very little time looking after the interests of those whom their positions exist to serve.

We, the plebeian masses, are left to take care of ourselves, whilst those inculcated into the incestuous and nepotistic world of politics, fight tooth and nail with each other, to garner and protect whatever power-base they are capable of defending.

It is for this reason that the vast majority of human beings have nothing but contempt for "politicians", and wouldn't stop to piss on them if they were on fire, much less bring themselves to vote for them.

OK, so that's the situation as I see it, and there's nothing particularly revolutionary or original in those thoughts. Truth be told, it has ever been thus. The sheer act of elevating those amongst us with a thirst for power and control, to positions of power and responsibility, is what must inevitably corrupt those individuals foolish enough to believe that they would use any such power and control gained, as a force for positive change in our Society.

So, what's the view from up in those "elected", "representative", "democratic" Ivory Towers look like?

Well, much as any idealistic politician would like to believe that they are one of History's servants, attending to the needs of the masses, in the most positive and benevolent way... They understand that the expediencies of political power demand that they must retain their positions of power, if they are to effect any positive change.

And in order to retain a position of power within a democracy, they must assure themselves of a democratic mandate to earn and exercise such power. And a democratic mandate means being more popular than "The Other Guy"; it means earning more votes than anyone else.

And right about now, there are some very clever - and very expensive - people who know all about the ways in which one person can earn more votes than another. And the primary technique that they advise upon and operate with, is the manipulation of the [b]*IMAGE*[/b] of a political candidate, and how he or she is presented in the mass media.

One of the core foundations of a strong image in politics is resolve. A "flip-flopper" or "fence-sitter" is a prime candidate for negative campaigning. Some who can be presented as not being able to make their mind up on important issues, can easily be portrayed as a weak and indecisive candidate, to weak to ever be trusted as a leader.

So any candidate that wants to stand a chance of even being nominated, let alone elected, must demonstrate absolute resolve and forthright and clear views on just about any issue you could care to mention.

What this means, in practice, is that a candidate must set out his or her ideological stall in their "Political Manifesto". Once their positions are "set in stone", woe betide the candidate who even contemplates changing their mind on any important issues.

And what [b]*THIS*[/b] means, Border, is that what plays out from the moment someone presents him or her self as a candidate for high office, to the moment of the Election: is [b]*ENTIRELY*[/b] a popularity contest.

It can be nothing more.

Assuming the candidate isn't already in power - as with the current U.S. Presidential Campaign - there can be no test or examination of performance or ability. And as I described above, there can be [b]*ABSOLUTELY NO CHANGES*[/b] in manifesto commitments, once articulated.

So exactly what information is it that an "Independent" voter waits for, before deciding whether or not to pull the Red or Blue lever, on the day of the Election?

Those expensive political consultants understand that there is nothing else but a candidate's popularity and the presentation (and durability) of their personality. The issues and positions are clearly defined on Day One; the rest of the campaign is all about making sure that the candidate does not end up any less popular than "The Other Guy".

So I'm sorry if you feel aggrieved at the implied insult Border, it certainly wasn't meant as such. I was merely describing how the elective process plays out in a two-party system; and how "Independent" voters are targeted and become the sole focus of campaigning within such a system.

Of course, if you are saying that you will eventually choose either the Red or Blue candidates, then you're not really an Independent voter, you are an Undecided voter, and that's really who I'm talking about: the Undecided.

A True Independent voter would never dream of giving his or her vote to either the Red or Blue candidates in a Two-Party system; they would, as you seem to be veering towards, give their vote only to the Donald Duck candidate, or mark "None of the Above".

And here's where we get into much more interesting electoral territory.

The "First Past the Post" system of electioneering that holds sway in the United States and United Kingdom, is well past its use-by date. Most other Western Democracies (including Israel) now use Proportional Representation (P.R.) as the foundation of their electoral processes.

"First Past the Post" both favours and maintains a Two Party System. If you don't like either Party or their candidates, then you are stuffed. You may as well vote for Donald Duck, or otherwise spoil your ballot paper; or, as most people choose to do, just not bother to vote at all.

But in any one of the numerous Proportional Representation systems being utilised in virtually every other Western democracies outside of the U.S. and U.K., virtually everyone's vote count.

I won't go into any more detail on Proportional Representation, because WikiPedia does that far more effectively than I could ever manage: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_representation
