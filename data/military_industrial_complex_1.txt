Actually, the figure that Americans get told their government spends on "Defence" is around $700 billion. But the true figure is closer to $1.2 trillion - around about the same as the entire GDP of the U.K.

And at $1.2 trillion "Defence" spending, that figure is getting close to 10% of current U.S. GDP too.

The breakdown for the 2012 budget:

[b]$558 billion[/b] for the Pentagon's annual "base" budget

[b]$118 billion[/b] to fund military operations in Iraq and Afghanistan

[b]$7.8 billion[/b] "miscellaneous" Defence Department spending

[b]$19.3 billion[/b] for nuclear-weapons-related activities (comes out of Department of Energy budget)

[b]$8.7 billion[/b] for "Overseas Contingency Operations" - basically: fighting the "War on Terror" (comes out of the State Department's budget)

[b]$71.6 billion[/b] for "Homeland Security" ($18.1 billion from Department of Defense; $37 billion from Homeland Security Department; $4.6 billion from Department of Health and Human Services; and $4.6 billion from the Department of Justice)

[b]$53.1 billion[/b] for Intelligence funding (CIA, NSA & other agencies)

[b]$129.3 billion[/b] for Veterans' hospital, medical care, disability pensions and education programmes

[b]$6.6 billion[/b] in military aid to foreign countries (mostly Israel & Egypt)

[b]$2 billion[/b] for "International Peacekeeping"

[b]$9 billion[/b] for countering WMD proliferation, combating terrorism and landmine clearance (amongst other foreign affairs spending)

[b]$48.5 billion[/b] military retirees' pensions

[b]$20 billion[/b] for Defence Department civilian employees retirement pensions

And finally:

Interest payment on U.S. National Debt for FY 2012 is projected at $474.1 billion. 39% or [b]$185 billion[/b] of this comes from borrowing related to past Pentagon spending.

Giving a Grand Total of:  [b]$1.237 Trillion[/b] - that the U.S. will spend on "Defence" for the financial year 2012.
