A point I made in another post in this thread is that [b]IF[/b] the kind of CCTV-monitoring systems suggested [b]WERE[/b] installed by Social Services, it would be in the homes of people for whom there is clear evidence that child neglect, abuse or anti-social behaviour is taking place. In other words, there is clear evidence that the subjects [b]ARE[/b] criminals, just that they are criminals engaging in (currently) low-level criminal activity that does not warrant incarceration.

The idea then, would be that rather than having to wait for their criminal activity to escalate to the point where a prison sentence would be appropriate, the installation of monitoring equipment would hopefully dissuade and discourage further criminal activity of any level.

The point being that the subjects [b]WOULD[/b] be convicted offenders, but the idea is to prevent them becoming actual prison convicts.

It might be helpful to think of such a scheme as a sort of pre-emptive probation.

When a prison convict is released on probation before the full term of his or her sentence has expired, he or she is required to be monitored by the Probation Service. They are expected to report regularly to their probation officer, and usually have restrictions placed on their conduct.

It would be the same thing here. A person, or multiple people in a single family, have been found guilty of a crime such as child neglect or anti-social behaviour. But rather than putting them in prison for the short period of time their crime would justify, they are instead put straight on probation. Only in this case, a 21st Century form of probation, which using the miracles of modern technology would mean having remotely monitored CCTV equipment installed in their homes.

As with all probation situations, regular reviews would be conducted to establish whether continued monitoring is still warranted, and where it is considered to no longer be necessary, it would cease.

So, aside from the fact that there are no plans to instigate such a scheme at present, the key fact to remember is that it is only being discussed in relation to people who [b]ARE[/b] convicted offenders.

There is no proposal, suggestion or discussion that CCTV systems would or could be installed arbitrarily in people's homes, just because the Government felt like it.

Well, firstly, the regulations are already here. In fact, in most cases, the Rules & Regulations have been around a long time, and most members of Society are happy to abide by them.

What we're really talking about is [i]greater enforcement[/i] of those regulations, and using modern technology to dramatically reduce evasion of those regulations.

For example, installing CCTV cameras in most towns and cities in the UK hasn't introduced any new regulations. It has always been illegal to fight in the streets, urinate in shop doorways, throw bricks through shop windows, break into parked cars, rape women and abduct children. It's just that nowadays, because of the prevalence of CCTV systems, it is just much, much harder to do that any of that sort of stuff and get away with it.

No new laws have been introduced. Nobody in the UK is being spirited off the streets by the Security Services, because they are suspected of sedition or disloyalty to the government. The only change, in fact, to the lives of most British citizens following the widespread introduction of CCTV cameras in their High Streets, is that they feel safer, because crime rates are down across the board.

You know, one thing to bear in mind about the whole "Big Brother"/State Monitoring idea, is the colossal amount of manpower that would be required to have every citizen in the UK monitored on a 24-hour basis. Sure, you can install cameras that cover every inch of the country, even inside people's homes, but who is going to be watching them all?

Facial recognition is one thing, but behaviour recognition is something completely different. You might get a computer system to be able to track the movements of people, but no computer technology can distinguish different forms of human behaviour, at least nowhere near enough to be able to tell the difference between criminal and non-criminal activity.

But even if it could, so long as we're still looking at the situation from a law-enforcement point-of-view, what's the problem?

Orwell's "Big Brother" system was conceived and expressed as a way of enforcing "Groupthink"; a way of making sure that everyone followed the Party Line, and were prohibited from exhibiting anything that might dangerously approach Independent Thought. It wasn't actually about Law Enforcement, other than that in Orwell's "1984", unquestioned loyalty to Big Brother [b]WAS[/b] the Law.

The whole "Slippery Slope" conversation is the purview of hysteria-inducing media "reporters", and those who devour such nonsense. There really is no historical precedent for entire Societies sliding guilelessly into an insidiously encroaching Police State. The Totalitarian Governments of the 20th Century - Hitler's Germany; Stalin's Soviet Union; Zedong's China - were all created off the back of seismic events that could be used to justify restrictions on individual freedoms virtually over-night.

But more than anything else, common sense tells us that there appears to be enough people watching out for those "Slippery Slopes", to ensure that it can't ever happen. Governments in the Developed World's Democracies struggle enough to be re-elected; in fact, re-election and the maintenance of power pretty much consumes their energies in totality. There really isn't much that could take place in an Orwellian manner, until we see the emergence of One Party States.

And I don't think anyone is inclined to allow that to happen again anytime soon. We'd be pretty stupid if we did, and would only have ourselves to blame for what followed if it happened.
