It doesn't go anywhere near *WHY* life evolves. It simply explains the process, not the catalyst or source.

Correct me if I'm wrong, but wouldn't adapting to a new or different environment be considered the catalyst for evolution?

Not really.

The term catalyst means (simplifying here) the thing that makes something happen.

The need to adapt to a changing environment isn't the thing that causes Evolution to happen. Rather, the production of a beneficial adaptation for a new environment is a chance/random by-product of Evolution.

In "On the Origin of the Species", Darwin demonstrated that offspring are not an exact copy of their parents, but always display differences. Most of the time these differences are so subtle as to be completely unnoticeable, and they do not afford any competitive advantage. But every now and then, a change will occur - like a longer beak, slightly thicker blubber or an enhanced ability to digest chlorophyll - that does provide a single creature an advantage over its fellow species member.

It isn't that environmental changes cause the mutation, but rather that the mutation allows an adaptation which gives the, uh, mutant, a greater chance of survival.

What *is* still a complete mystery to Science, is what the actual "spark" of Life is. Science hasn't begun to address what it was that turned essentially inert chemicals and minerals into living, organic matter.

The best guess at the moment is that Life was spontaneously generated by just the right mixture of chemicals, heat and light, perhaps in a subaquatic volcanic vent. But it is all entirely guess work at the moment. Mary Shelley's idea that you stitch all the component parts together and then raise them to the roof to be "vitalised" by lightning is as good a guess as any!

But Dr. Frankenstein's monster aside, it is this "spark" that seems to be the focus of the Creationism versus Evolution non-debate.

Unfortunately, whilst many people who have studied, understood and developed the Theory of Evolution (Darwin didn't write *the* Book, he just wrote the first book) have read the Book of Genesis; it is evident from their protestations that very few Creationists have read either "On the Origins...", or any of the more recent and up-to-date tracts on Evolutionary Biology. If they have read it, then they clearly haven't understood it, and ought to go back and read it again.

The point is that nothing in Evolutionary Biology addresses or even attempts to address what the "thing" was that kicked off the whole process. It just describes how the process is playing out.

There is a corollary in Physics, in that cosmologists are more or less agreed on what happened *after* the Big Bang, but nobody has addressed why or how the Big Bang took place in the first place.
