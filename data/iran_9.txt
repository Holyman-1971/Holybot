The Iranians and North Koreans aren't suicidal: they are just into self-preservation.

They don't want to be pushed around by a foreign power, and they understand that the best way to prevent it happening, is to possess nuclear weapons.

If the governments of the United States and Israel were really serious about not wanting Iran to develop its own nuclear [i]deterrent[/i], then they would stop trying to push Iran around. The U.S. has certainly stopped trying to push North Korea around since it revealed its nuclear capacity; why should the Iranians not be encouraged by that?

But here's the interesting thing about this whole "issue" - and Foota, you have kind of picked up on it - it's about the absurdity of the rhetoric that surrounds the possibility of a nuclear-armed Iran.

We have the absurd situation where Pacifists, such as myself, are presented as arguing [b]*FOR*[/b] a nuclear-armed Iran; and gung-ho militarists such as Foota, presenting themselves as Peacekeepers, arguing [b]*FOR*[/b] diplomatic pressure and sanctions on Iran (though one can't help but look for the "Big Stick" that is supposed to back up those "Soft Words").

Compounding the absurdity, we have the surely non-coincidental similarities with the run-up to the Invasion of Iraq: the IAEA, UN, and the U.S.'s own National Intelligence Estimate report that there is zero evidence of any Iranian nuclear weapons programme; the Iranian Guardian Council of Islamic Clerics has issued a fatwa declaring nuclear weapons un-Islamic; and the absolute certainty that if Iran were to use or allow to be used a nuclear weapon (that there is no evidence it will have any time in the near future), it would be immediately and totally annihilated.

And yet we have Pacifists like myself allowing for the fact that Iran [i]may[/i] be developing a nuclear deterrent, and understanding and accepting the reasons why that may be. And Hawks like Foota saying that everything that can be done diplomatically and without the use of force, should be done to prevent Iran possessing nuclear WMDs... Otherwise force will [b]HAVE[/b] to be used in response to Iran's immediate offensive use of its nuclear weapons capability.

It's all a bit Alice in Wonderland really.

And History suggests that such surreal and implausible accusations and rhetoric, is simply a way of diverting attention away from a power struggle far more likely to be rooted in the harsh realities of Economics and material gain.

And in this instance, the fact that oil-rich Iran stands at the economic and geographic crossroads between 4 major economic powers - China, Russia, the U.S. and India - can't possibly be ignored.
