[color=firebrick]Here is where I start to [i]disagree[/i]. Marx was not just some humble intelectual laying an ideological foundation for others. Marx [i]was[/i] driven by personality, just look at the lies and personal attacks he used to destroy poor Bakunin, an ideological ally, merely because he was taking the limelight away from Marx.

And even if it could be argued that Marx never intended for a repressive Soviet style system to implement his ideas of communist utopia through blind blood and fear, it is indisputably an outcome of his ideology--whether an unintentional consequence or not. The thought that was going around my mind when I wrote the above, was of a couple of somewhat-embittered intellectuals railing against the inequities of Human Society, and producing an idealistic philosophy that they convinced themselves would resolve all of Humanity's problems, once and for all.

I can appreciate the sentiment - I spend a lot of my own time doing much the same thing... I just don't think that they ever properly thought through the practicalities of implementation.

If I'd have been the third party to Marx and Engels' discussions, I would have pointed them squarely at the experience of the French Revolution - which was, to all intents and purposes, a "Marxist Revolution" that took place before Marx was even born. I would have reminded them that the ideals of the French Revolutionaries were not entirely dissimilar to Marx' ideals.

And then I would have hinted that actually, removing old, established orders, whilst nearly always requiring or even guaranteeing violent struggle, is also the really easy bit, compared to what has to come after: Establishing the New Order.

Not more than ten years after Louis XVI has his head lopped off, France gave up its ideals of a Citizen Government and selected an Emperor with even greater power concentrated in a single individual that had been the case under the [i]ancien régime[/i].

Same thing happened in the Soviet Union, with Stalin becoming a more powerful despot than any Tsar this side of Ivan the Terrible; and in China, with Mao Zedong being the most potent Chinese Emperor of the Second Millennium.

What, in fact, I'd probably do as I travelled back to Marx and Engels' coffee house, is take along a copy of George Orwell's "Animal Farm", and say:

"Look guys, don't waste your time writing that manifesto, trust me, it'll do more harm than good in the long run. Read this instead."

Would save a *LOT* of bother, one way and another.
