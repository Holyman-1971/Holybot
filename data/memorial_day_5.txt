Alright, look, Wunder began his first post in this thread with the phrase, "Fuck Your Dead".

I wouldn't have used those words, and I certainly don't agree with the sentiment expressed by such a phrase.

I wrote at the end of my first post:

[quote=Holyman2012][color=blue]Respect for the dead, particularly for those who died - or thought they were dying - in our name, should be a respectful, contemplative affair.

Not an extension of the Religio-Nationalist propaganda that fuels the whole War Machine, and keeps sending the walking dead back to their families to try and live a normal life, Free from nightmares.[/color][/quote]

So I'm clearly not agreeing with the sentiment expressed in Wunder's phrase.

But also, I don't think Wunder is, either. Not, at least, with the sentiment you're attaching to it.

I'm [b]*NOT*[/b] saying that you are misinterpreting such a clearly unambiguous phrase. But not being on the receiving end of it, or having had the Emotional reaction I would have had if I were, I can view Wunder's use of the phrase differently.

To me, Wunder used the phrase solely for shock effect; and to shortcut a drawn-out and well rehearsed discourse on the rights and wrongs of honouring those who die in human conflict.

We [b]*ALL*[/b] know that it is a tragedy when one human being kills, or is killed by another.

There is no argument on that. And demonstrating a bit of respect for those who have lost someone they Love, is the least we can do as compassionate human beings.

But here in the crucible of =PBF= R'nR, and in this thread, we are not discussing anyone's personal loss or bereavement. That isn't to say or assume that no-one here present hasn't experienced such a loss resulting from human conflict; just that up to the point that Wunder wrote what he did, nobody had personalised the subject.

Now on the subject of impersonal "loss", relating to the loss of life in human conflict of our fellow members of Society, we are all want to get somewhat sanctimonious. And it is this sanctimony that I believe Wunder, as ever, was trying to shock out of the thread.

Because we do get so sanctimonious about "our" War dead, it can make discourse on the subject very difficult. As politically correct as any radical socialist, we have to guide the conversation around any possibility of speaking ill of the dead, or the manner of their dying. And that makes it extremely difficult to discuss how and why they died.

It is as though their deaths in battle somehow legitimise the battle, or larger War. Even though we may understand that these people died ingloriously, in a pointless and criminal conflict; because we [b]*MUST*[/b] honour their "sacrifice", we can no more speak ill of the reasons why they died, than we can speak ill of the dead.

Now I'm not saying Wunder used the best tactic, or even used the tactic he did to any great degree of success; but he did utter a great taboo, and in doing so, gave everyone else the opportunity to indignantly reinforce their credentials, with respect to those who have died in conflicts fought in their name, or on behalf of their nation.

Great. Now everyone has had the opportunity to agree that those who die in human conflicts should be honoured, we can now move on to discuss the merits or futility of those actual deaths, without anyone questioning our respect for the dead.

I'm not here to make excuses for Wunder, and I may be well wide of the mark, but I do think he was upholding a fine British tradition of satirising people who are behaving unduly sanctimoniously.

And it does have to be said that you Yanks do have a tendency towards the sanctimonious, particularly in matters of national prestige.

But it's not a one-way street. Americans have a facility for puncturing British pomposity in a way that the French, German and Spanish can only look at with envy. And I think that's just as it should be, given all our shared History.

Fact is, once Wunder had played the "Bad Cop" card, and everyone was able to express their indignation at so offensive a phrase as he used, we were and have been able to move on and discuss the [b]*REALLY*[/b] important point, summarised by Steelhed phrase:

[quote=steelhed][color=red]Honor our soldiers... Hold our leaders accountable...[/color][/quote]

And that's all there is to it.

We honour our soldiers best, when we [b]*DO*[/b] hold our leaders and their Generals accountable. Failing to do so is to fail in [b]*OUR*[/b] duties as citizens to those who fight for us, in our name, and performing [b]*THEIR*[/b] duty for us.

This is the Covenant between Citizen and Soldier.

And clearly, we are [b]*NOT*[/b] holding our current leaders accountable for their crimes and errors; and consequently we are [b]*NOT*[/b] honouring our soldiers.
