One of the biggest social problems created by the current mess is that many retired people look to their pension (U.K.) or 401 (k) (U.S.) investments to provide them with an income when they are no longer working.

Many (if not most) of these pension/401 (k) funds have invested heavily in what were supposed to be Blue Chip stocks; i.e. the banking and financial institutions that were supposed to be the bedrock of our Economy.

The problem that is faced now is that so many corporations, banks and other previously stable institutions are in a moribund condition, and even if they don't completely fail and disappear, the value of their stocks and any dividends paid on those stocks have declined precipitously, with no end yet in sight for that decline.

There is a further demographic factor that amounts to a Double-Whammy for the retired community, and that is that due to significant rises in life expectancy, there are considerably more people past retirement age, looking to their investments for an income, than was anticipated when these individuals began saving and investing for their retirement.

The pension/401 (k) model for retirement is that people no longer earning a wage for their currently provided labour or productivity, rely upon incomes that are derived from their capital investments or savings. In other words, the idea is not to touch the capital - the raw cash savings or the basic pension/401 (k) fund units - but to live off the interest and dividends these savings and investments provide.

But where do the interest and the dividend payouts come from? It is money that has to originate somewhere. These aren't notional sums.

If dividend payouts cannot be counted on because corporations have either gone bust, significantly reduced or cancelled altogether their dividend payouts, what then for retirees who rely on an income from dividends?

If interest rates are set so low that they are barely above or perhaps even below the rate of inflation, then retirees who look to fund their retired lifestyles from interest payments on cash savings will need to chisel into their base capital funds to make up the shortfall.

Couple this with the "Demographic Time-bomb" of an aging population, and pension/401 (k) funds have little choice but to become (somewhat) legitimate "Ponzi Schemes": where the funds being invested by current workers are used to pay-out already retired claimants.

The challenge then is how to support an aging population that is no longer willing or able to earn their own keep?

And all this, [b]*BEFORE*[/b] we factor in the Economic devastation that we are now witnessing.

And bear in mind that this Double-Whammy of an unsustainable social pension system and a moribund Economy is an enormous problem [b]*BEFORE*[/b] we start talking about resetting and/or redesigning our Economic Systems.

It is clearly a situation that would have to be given very careful consideration by any programme to redesign our Social-Economic Paradigm.
