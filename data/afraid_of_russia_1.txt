Georgia then...

When the Soviet Union collapsed in 1991, most of the former Soviet Republics (including the Russian Republic) fell into turmoil as various factions fought for control of the wreckage.

In the Central Asian "'Stans", those internal conflicts were resolved rapidly with the victories of people like [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_Karimov]Islam Karimov[/url], President of Uzbekistan from 1991 until his death in 2016. He'd also been the last First Secretary of the Communist Party of Uzbekistan, and given his name, I'm guessing, a Muslim... But being a Communist, he was of course, officially an atheist.

In the Baltic States of Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia, things were a bit more genteel, primarily because both NATO and EU eyed those former Soviet Republics as prime candidates for membership.

But in Georgia, things took longer to resolve, primarily because ethnic [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossetians]Ossetians[/url] saw the post-Soviet turmoil as an opportunity to build on the autonomy they had under Soviet rule, and achieve National Self-Determination, much as the Kosovans also saw the opportunity to separate themselves from Serbian hegemony.

So too did the [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abkhazians]Abkhazians[/url].

The newly-evolved Russian Federation sided with the Abkhazians, primarily because of the access the Russian Military enjoyed to the eastern Black Sea cost; which would be less guaranteed from a Georgian Government. Russia's support of the Ossetians was less for strategic military purposes, and more about retaining some degree of influence over Georgia's internal affairs.

In 2004, following "pledges of support" from the United States, the [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Revolution]Rose Revolution[/url] took place in Georgia, and [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikheil_Saakashvili]Mikheil Saakashvili[/url] displaced (former Minister of Foreign Affairs for the Soviet Union) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Shevardnadze]Eduard Shevardnadze[/url] as President of Georgia.

And wouldn't you know it, but one of the new President's primary goals for Georgia, was membership of NATO. In 2008, George W. Bush lobbied for Georgian (and also Ukrainian...) Membership of NATO; and Putin responded with the statement that expansion of NATO to Russia's borders "would be taken in Russia as a direct threat to the security of our country."

And why wouldn't it be?

Couple of weeks after making that statement, Putin authorised official ties between the Russian Government and the separatists in Abkhazia and South Ossetia. This was much as the US and EU Governments had done for Kosovan Separatists, in the previous decade; only following the Russian announcement, the U.S., UK, French and German governments then issued a joint statement calling on the Russian Federation to "revoke or not to implement its decision".

And then, in August 2008, it all kicked off, at [url=https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Russo-Georgian_War]Russia and Georgia were at war[/url].

For about four weeks, on an off. Until early September, when hostilities ceased.

Russian Military forces remain in Abkhazia and South Ossetia, at the invitation of the [i]de facto[/i] governments of those two regions; the Georgian government continues to consider those two regions as Russian-occupied territories; and just as it does with the Israeli-Occupied territories of Palestine, the E.U. also still considers Abkhazia and South Ossetia as Russian-occupied territories of Georgia.

So that's "Georgia".
