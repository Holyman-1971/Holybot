I was reading a piece a few days ago where a Wall Street financier was interviewed about the background to this crisis, and one of the things he (unattributably) spoke about was that one of the greatest surprises on Wall Street in the past few years, was the discovery that "The poor are basically honest. Who would have thought it?"

What he meant by that was that the majority of poor people take it as a matter of pride and dignity that they service their debts wherever possible, with an avowed aim to eventually settle them. This in contrast to the bigger professional financiers who don't hesitate to declare bankruptcy when their positions get sticky or unmanageable.

What this means is that by and large, those "amateur" borrowers, those "Once-in-a-Lifetime" mortgage holders, will bend over backwards to try and service their debts, even if it means a life of debt peonage and effective servitude. And not just because bankruptcy laws were changed during the Bush Administration.

First off, we're talking about people who never had good credit-ratings in the first place, so a loss of or damage to personal credit-rating would have limited negative consequences. But furthermore, the number of private individuals who are (currently) unable to service their debts is not a significant proportion of those who have (often over-extended) debts.

So we're not talking about absolving private individuals of debt.

What we're talking about is looking at those banks and finance institutions whose [b]*OWN*[/b] debts [b]*FAR EXCEED*[/b] the debts of all private individuals combined; and whose business operations are only continuing because they have utilised a pliant Presidency and Congress to bail them out.

The U.S. Government [b]*IS*[/b] absolving U.S. banks of responsibility for the debts those banks have run-up, by either injecting cash into the banks, or taking those bad debts onto the Government balance sheet.

If the U.S. Government did not do this, then those banks (the big Wall Street banks) would and must collapse, as they became swamped by their own debt. And when those banks collapsed, all the people and businesses who owe those banks money on mortgages, loans and credit-cards, would immediately have their debts wiped out, because the institutions they owe money to, would no longer exist.

Private individuals who had poor credit ratings to begin with, won't worry about having a lower credit rating. Given their current experience, they'll probably be happy to never take credit again, if and when they dig themselves out of the current debt-hole that many of them are in.

But allowing the banks to go bankrupt, taking all the debts they owe and hold with them... Well that's something that the "Masters of the Universe" on Wall Street have instructed their politicians on Wall Street to make sure is the [b]*LAST*[/b] thing that happens.
