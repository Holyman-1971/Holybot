I feel that it is impossible to approach the issue of the Israeli-Palestinian Conflict with an attempt to establish which party to the Conflict is more morally righteous or correct in its aims.

There has simply been too much blood spilt on and by both sides. Emotions run too high for objectivity to be applied. It is impossible to bear witness to the lives of Israeli civilians living under threat of indiscriminate rocket bombardment, or Palestinian civilians living under threat of equally indiscriminate bombardment, and not feel and share the same sense of Emotional hurt and outrage that both Israelis and Palestinians feel.

This necessarily colours the information that is reported to the watching, outside World, from within the Conflict zone.

Faced with literally conflicted information, those of us not directly affected by the Conflict feel compelled to choose one side or the other. Having at once chosen sides, we view any reportage that seems sympathetic to the "other" side, as deeply suspicious; and we consciously and sub-consciously seek out and emphasise reported information that validates our choice of "side" as morally correct and assured.

But whatever else, since both parties to the Conflict are clearly culpable for projecting violence on innocent civilians, we end up in a "Race to the Bottom", trying to establish which side is the least worst.

Israel's supporters, whilst acknowledging that some of Israel's methods are perhaps regrettable, will support those methods as an unavoidable response to the terroristic provocation of the Palestinians. And equally, supporters of Palestinians, whilst perhaps lamenting militants' use of indiscriminate rocket attacks on the Israeli civilian population, will suggest that those attacks are a forgivable and understandable reaction to Israel's occupation and suppression of the Palestinian civilian population.

Trying to view the Conflict through the lens of "Right and Wrong" precludes the possibility of reaching a Peaceful settlement.

It's like Slates said above: [color=orangered]"...there will NEVER be Peace if either side can initiate aggression and then cite an event that happened 3 months, 3 years, or 3 decades ago as their excuse."[/color]

Trying to establish which side is least guilty of often horrific and always unacceptable crimes, is an academic exercise that takes place against the backdrop of continued violence inflicted on both Communities.

It is quite clear that neither the Israelis or the Palestinians are going to submit to violent intimidation or subjugation. And it is quite clear that every act of violence committed by one side against the other, provokes and legitimises a violent response. What is clearly required is a Third Party who will mandate that the current violence projected by [b]*BOTH*[/b] sides, is totally unacceptable. But there isn't - or hasn't been - a Third Party willing to or apparently capable of doing this.

As not only the World's foremost Economic and Military Power, but also the Nation that supplies Israel with more economic and military aid than it supplies to any other country, the United States of America is the only Third Party currently capable of mediating the Israeli-Palestinian Conflict effectively and objectively. But the U.S. has always been constrained in this respect, by its subjective and often unequivocal support for Israel.

It isn't that the United States should not support Israel, and certainly not that the U.S. should support the Palestinians [b]*INSTEAD*[/b] of supporting Israel. It is that biased support of [b]*EITHER*[/b] party to this (or any other) Conflict, simply prolongs it. Providing the partisan and subjective support of Israel that the U.S. does, can actually be seen to be working against Israel's long-term interests.

If the United States tries to play moral arbiter [b]*BEFORE*[/b] Peace is established in Israel-Palestine, that Peace will never arrive. Peace must first be enforced, equally and equitably on both sides, before the process of reconciliation and settlement can begin. Trying to negotiate a lasting Peace settlement against a background of continuing violence, with supporters on both sides trying to keep some kind of moral score concerning whose behaviour is more morally abhorrent than the other, is clearly (and obviously) impossible.

President Obama's dispatch of George Mitchell - an Arab-American - to the Middle East, may well alter this situation. As special envoy to Northern Ireland in the 1990's, Mitchell demonstrated an admirable ability to bring together the two sides of a Conflict that had been raging for much longer than the Israeli-Palestinian Conflict, and with no less enmity.

It remains to be seen how effective he will or can be, just as it remains to be seen how Israel will conduct itself following next month's elections.

But whatever else happens, we cannot afford to go on playing the "Who is More to Blame Game"; or the "Who Started It Game".

These are games that children should be playing themselves; not games that they are being killed by.
