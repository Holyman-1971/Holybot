Well firstly Fitch, thank you for your eloquent and clear response. It has certainly helped me begin to gain a clearer insight into your personal beliefs, and that can only be for the better!

I suppose the best way to describe my view of Fundamentalism is to describe it as close-mindedness. You write:

 I don't believe that I have everything 100% correct. I'm learning more every day and my understanding is growing as I get older.

And that means, in my view, you are not a fundamentalist. But you also write:

All fundamentalists are Christians�

And that maybe where our wires are getting crossed.

I think Fundamentalism is a mind-set, and people can be fundamentalist about all manner of subjects, though it is usually observed in relation to Religion, or Faith-based subjects.

I do Believe that someone having Faith in the purity and primacy of the Christian message does *not* automatically make them a fundamentalist, but I can see how my tendency for circumlocution may have given that impression.

I personally interpret the parable of the seed-sower as being about what we are supposed to do with our knowledge of the Word of God, as gleaned from the Bible, and from the work of Christians throughout the last two millennia. Since the �Seed� of the Word is Holy, all that grows from it is also Holy.

In other words then, I see the Bible as a foundation for all that has followed it. My difficulties with those of a Fundamentalist persuasion is that they often appear to be suggesting that the Bible is the *only* Word, and that anything else written outside of that sacrosanct tome is automatically and implicitly correct.

I think this is the basis of the whole debate about Creationism versus Evolution. The Old Testament was written around 3,500 years ago; and the New Testament 1,500 years later. If we limit our understanding of the World to the level of knowledge that was present at that Time, we become prone to allowing that Holy Seed to wither and rot. To automatically dismiss new understanding, simply because it is not mentioned in the Bible, is to walk blindfold and barren into our future.

There are a number of theosophical points on which you and I disagree, and I�ll just highlight a couple of those.

How do you know if something is from God? You compare it to scripture, but I don't believe God speaks to us audibly anymore, or "inspires" men as He did with the select few in the Bible...... Again, the Bible is the test of all things, and since it isn't to be added to, God's revelation to mankind is complete.

I Believe that God continues to speak to us every minute of every day. I Believe that the way God speaks to us has advanced in line with our capacity to understand new wisdom and enlightenment. I Believe for instance that through Charles Darwin, God was leading us to a new level of understanding about the nature of our Universe, and our own place within it.

And I Believe that God continues to reveal new understandings to us, as we become ready to understand.

I believe that Darwin was meant to say what he did. It's part of God's judgment by subjecting people to disbelief. Yes, I do believe that God keeps people from knowing the truth to exercise His justice on them. It's part of my calvinistic upbringing, and I believe it to be scriptural.

Now the God I Believe in does not try to mess with mind; rather, my God treats me with the care a Father has for His child. God explains Life to me in a way that I can understand, and as that understanding grows, so too do God�s explanations to me become more sophisticated and complex.

But wouldn�t you say that being a Calvinist means that you yourself are open to successive re-interpretations of scripture? Was Calvin not his self a Reformer? Is Calvinism not a reformist denomination?

Whilst I understand that a central tenet of Calvinism is that man is incapable of contributing to his own salvation � often, if unfairly labelled predestination � I don�t recall that Calvin was opposed to developing our understanding of scripture and the manifest destiny of God.

But there you go, I truly Believe that such theosophical discourse is, in itself, just another way in which God encourages us to develop our understanding, so find no fault in the difference between our beliefs: quite the opposite! :)

The "you" is a generic "you", and was not directed to "you" specifically. It was directed to all those who don't believe the Bible is the word of God, but use it anyway for whatever they want to justify (they ignore the parts where it says that it is the word of God).

Apologies then if you feel I have misrepresented or mischaracterised your Beliefs. I can assure you that was never my intention.

To summarise my own Beliefs then:

I Believe wholeheartedly in the magnificence and splendour of God. I Believe also that knowledge revealed must coincide with an ability to receive and understand. As individuals and as a Society, we are more capable now than ever we were to understand God in a more sophisticated way.
