Have you heard the tale about Philip K. Dick's story, "Flow My Tears, the Policeman Said"?

It was a story he wrote in 1974 and is set in a dystopian future United States, following a Second Civil War. Dick described how he wrote the book really fast; that it just flowed out of him, almost as though he were channelling it.

About four years after it was published, he was at a party where he met a woman who had the same name as the female character in the book. She had a boyfriend with the same name as the boyfriend character in the book, and she was having an affair with a guy who was a police chief, who had the same name as the chief of police in his book.

Which freaked Philip K. Dick out a bit...

Shortly after, he was going to post a letter when he saw a rather shady looking character standing by his car. Uncharacteristically, instead of avoiding him, Dick walked up to him and offered assistance. The guy responded that he was out of petrol. Dick pulls out his wallet and gives the guy some cash, something he said he would never normally have done. Then he gets home and realises that the guy wouldn't be able to get to a petrol station, what with him being out of petrol and all. So Dick gets back in his car, goes and finds the guy, and takes him to a petrol station.

As he pulls up in the gas station, Dick realises that this exact situation also was in the same book: the same station, the same guy.

So now, more than a little freaked out, Dick relates this to an Episcopalian priest friend of his. As he is telling the priest, the priest says, "That's the Book of Acts. You're describing the Book of Acts."

Dick had never read the Book of Acts, so he goes home and reads it, and the similarities between it and "Flow My Tears, the Policeman Said", and his recent experiences, were uncanny.

The theory he developed from this experience was influenced by his belief in Gnosticism, and his theory was that: Time is an illusion; that we are all actually still in 50 A.D. (when the Book of Acts was written); and that Dick had written his book whilst momentarily puncturing through the illusion of Time.

An elaboration on that Theory would cite that even the "50 A.D." bit is superfluous. That if Time *IS* an illusion, then all things are happening all at once. That the linear succession of "moments" that we conceptualise as "Time", is just a process of perceptual limitation operated by our consciousness to enable us to Experience the Eternal Moment of Now.

That we would see patterns emerging and repeating within our personal and collective experiences, would be a symptom of such a dynamic.
