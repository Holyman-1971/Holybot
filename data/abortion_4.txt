I'm afraid to say that a moral argument is an emotional argument.

Morality concerns how we feel about things. About how we feel about the things in our lives; about the way we feel about the way we live our lives; and about the way we feel about the way others live their lives. Every decision we make in our lives, about how we govern our lives, is either a rational, practical decision, based on empirical gain of some form or another; or it is a moral decision, based on how we feel about certain things.

Moralisers like yourself will often try to elevate a moral decision to the status of a rational, practical decision, but you simply can't make a silk purse out of a sow's ear. The practicality and rationality of an idea or decision is self-evident. It requires no external, ideological input. It is simply about what works, and what does not work. We have established a sound, though not completely perfect set of sciences to enable us to measure what works, and what does not work. On the basis of complete practicality and rationality can an idea or decision be self-justified. It needs no emotional appeals to "basic morality", it surpasses the need for morality to be an issue.

Doing what is practical and rational - i.e. where the positive consequences outweigh the negative consequences - is the height of morality. No morality exceeds the idea that we should do that which benefits us most. Of course, that goes hand-in-hand with the enormous task of responsibility and risk management, but damaging Society's morality comes far lower down the risk of priorities than just fulfilling practical and rational necessities. Therefore, any argument that tries to trump rational and practical benefit, must by definition be an irrational argument.

It isn't that your morality is bad or wrong Fitch, it is just that it is your morality, not mine, and not, I'm afraid to tell you, many other peoples either.

I won't condescend to you by trying to tell you what your morality is exactly, because it is your morality, and who am I to lecture you on it? I guess all I'm asking for is a little reciprocation on that front.

I've explained roughly what my "morality" is, and it is (approximately) doing whatever is practically and rationally justifiable, to positively increase our benefit from life.

That degree of rationality and practicality has to be the basis of Law and Government in a diverse, multi-cultural, multi-polar World. It has to be the basis because it is the only basis that can work. It is the only basis on which we all agree.

I'm sure your own life would be much more fulfilling and enjoyable for you, if you felt that everyone else shared your morality, I know I certainly would if I knew everyone shared mine. Problem is, we're stubborn bastards when it comes to our morality, and we refuse to budge an inch. I can't remember the last time I fundamentally changed my moral outlook on Life, can you?
