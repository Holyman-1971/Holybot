[quote=JFitch][color=firebrick]I would argue that the evidence for creationism is exactly the same as the evidence for evolution. What differs are the assumptions and presuppositions that go into each.[/color][/quote]

Umm, not really.

The basis of Creationism is that there is a - by definition - unknowable Creator who put Everything together. Creationism, a religiously founded and defined idea, invites people to just accept and believe that there is a Great Power behind Everything.

And apart from The Bible, the evidence that supports Creationism is pretty thin on the ground.

End of "Theory".

By contrast, there are quite literally mountains of evidence that supports the Theory of Evolution.

Like all good theories, the Theory of Evolution invites further enquiry and development, and encourages the interested to exercise their rational faculties in the full exploration of its strengths and weaknesses.

The assumption and presupposition that goes into Creationism is: Trust God, He knows what He is doing.

The assumption and presupposition that goes into the Theory of Evolution is: this is a good Theory, but it's not complete; why not see if you can either improve or disprove it.

I think the main thing that children are taught in school - or should be taught in school - is not a particular set of "scientific" facts; but rather the Scientific Method. It's just that subjects like Evolution are useful ideas for teaching that method.

It's the same as History. Whatever topic or era is taught in school History classes, the main aim is to teach the Historical Method.

To go into endless detail about the pros and cons of every nuanced interpretation of Historical or Scientific evidence would simply bore the children to tears. Evolution is the basic foundation of the modern science known as Biology, just as the "Big Bang" is the basic foundation of Physics. There are hyper-intelligent adult scientists who are still wrestling with the miniscule complexities of these theories - the Large Hadron Collider they are switching on at CERN is clear evidence of that.

But what, after all, are school teachers really meant to do? Stand up in front of the children and say: "Look, we simply don't know anything!"?

There's a reason why Philosophy isn't usually taught in grade and high schools, and it's usually to do with children's attention spans.

By contrast of course, what you get taught in Bible Study is [b]Absolute Certainty[/b]. That is, the absolute certainty that we are all God's play-things, and should forget about trying to fathom the unfathomable...

The problem with that is that Human Society really doesn't evolve, either rapidly or happily, on such a basis.
