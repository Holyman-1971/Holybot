Again, this isn't a "message" being crafted or designed by any politician, organisation or social institution: it is just a systemic consequence of an ideological belief in the "Free Market".

The "Free Market" is a complex beast Slates, and it frequently produces strange and unexpected events.

As I wrote in my previous post to SomeGuy, by and large, and proportionately speaking, there aren't that many people on Main Street struggling to service their debts; not when you consider the overall high levels of indebtedness amongst the general populace.

With interest rates at historic lows and unlikely to rise anytime soon, the only threat that most people face to their ability to service and ultimately repay their debts, is if they lose their jobs and can't find another one.

By contrast, the Wall Street Banks face - and have created for themselves - debt liabilities of historically unprecedented magnitude. The only possible course of action they have been able to devise is to ask the U.S. Government to take over responsibility for those debts (not exactly "Free Market" principles in action...).

If the U.S. Government refused to take on those debts, and/or refused to use public funds to shore-up or bail-out those banks, as the Free Market doctrine strictly dictates it must, then those banks fail.

And if and when those banks fail, any debt owed to them would be eradicated along with the bank.

This [b]*IS*[/b] the Free Market in action.

It would turn out that those consumers who took on debts that they couldn't really manage, would actually have made a rather fortuitous gamble; the kind of gamble that in happier times, is the normal course of affairs for the "Masters of the Universe" on Wall Street. Since Wall Street gamblers who short-trade stock engage in precisely the same kind of gamble, why would it be such a conflict that borrowers on Main Street should not also be allowed to benefit from the failure of these banking institutions?

Of course, it would be a pisser for those who supposedly did the "Right Thing", and kept their borrowings down, didn't over-extend themselves, lived within their means etc. But that is just the Rules of the Game, the "Way of the Free Market": there are winners and losers.

It might not be particularly fair or moral, but since when was Free Market Economics ever meant to be fair or moral.

If you want a fair and moral Economic System, then you need to choose a workable version of Socialism (if you can find one); one that is based on the ideas of fairness, morality and equitability.

The [b]*ACTUALLY*[/b] conflicting message that is [b]*CURRENTLY*[/b] being sent out, is that there [b]*IS NO FREE MARKET*[/b]: that Wall Street Banks can always rely upon and count on the State to bail-out its bad decisions and investments; and that people who borrow money will [b]*ALWAYS*[/b] be held to their debts, because it is a matter of moral and social principle, rather than an economic one.
