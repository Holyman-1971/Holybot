Well, I figure that's what a lot of us spend our time discussing on this Forum, and other similarly-minded Forums.

We might put those discussions in the context of the issues or news of the day, but whether we are criticising or supporting a particular event, person or policy, we are (positively or negatively) expressing our ideas about the way we think our World could or should be.

So I figured what the hell, let's cut the crap, we're none of us doing much else besides just talk about this stuff, we may as well start with a clean sheet of paper and explicitly idealise the kind of World we might like to see.

On the issue of Optimism v. Pessimism/Idealism v. Realism:

Obviously when someone like me expresses a vision of the World where all Humanity is skipping about the place, singing songs of Love, holding hands, gazing at the awe-inspiring magnificence of the World, and of each other, without a thought about harming each other or our beautiful World...

...Then I and people like me just ever-so-possibly correctly, and maybe fairly, be accused of being a bit over-idealistic, and perhaps a shade over-optimistic.

It would be hard to dispute that.

However, I would contend that when others talk about the possibility of a fiercely independent, self-sufficient, Frontier-like World; where it's every man for himself, no quarter asked or given, damn your taxes and prise my weapon from my cold dead hands... That is no less idealistic or improbable a scenario.

The fact is that we are no closer to the kind of "Perfect World" that you AV, or Foota, or SomeGuy might imagine, than we are to the kind of World I talk about.

Whenever we criticise "The Way Things Are", we are implicitly expressing a desire for things to be different. If we don't clarify our criticisms with a proposed alternative, then we are at least implying that we would like things to be the *OPPOSITE* of "The Way Things Are" now.

It's still Idealism though: just a different shade of Idealism.

Yup, that's exactly how I envisage it too. I wrote a bit about that just above the passage you quoted:

[quote="Holyman"][color=#0000FF]So here then, it won't be the human beings who live in that Community who fall victim to Natural Selection, but the Community itself. And that's no bad thing, because a Society/Community that has failed to sort out the basic essentials for survival, shouldn't be artificially supported. It is Natural Selection doing what it does best: selecting naturally what works, and what doesn't.

Let's assume that pretty rapidly, the most successful formula for managing the basics (water, food, energy, shelter, health and safety) emerges somewhere on the Planet, and is clearly recognised as such (my bet is that it'll appear somewhere in Scandinavia, but that might just be me stereotyping...). The same thing would happen as happens with Best Business Practice: it would soon or immediately be adopted by Everyone Else, until something else provably better came along.

But those Communities/Societies/Regions that rejected or failed to successfully implement these Best Practices, would fail with the basic fundamentals. And unless they could find and/or convince another Community or Society to prop them up, that Community would fail.

And the human beings who formed that Community and participated in that particular experiment at Communal Living, would in all likelihood be absorbed in part or in whole into other Communities. Probably on the condition that they didn't keep harping on about their clearly unsuccessful ideas about Community living, just accept that they were wrong, and accept that the Community they have now turned to for salvation and safety, has clearly got it "less wrong" than they did..!

Yup, again, that's more or less how I see it.

I think the greatest problem we have with the way governments operate these days, is the size of the areas and number of people governed by a single political entity.

Democracy worked really well for the Greeks, because they existed in independent City States; and by the time you'd ignored women, slaves and children, the ratio between The Governing and The Governed was much closer than it is now.

Take the current President of the United States of America: one human being who technically has overall responsibility for more than 350 million others (and more than that, if he really is "The Most Powerful Man in the World).

That's nonsense. What size of bureaucracy is required to govern that many different people fairly and equitably? It's a bureaucracy that soon needs an additional bureaucracy to bureaucratise itself!

So Downsize. Become more agile, more flexible. A Head of State ought to know each and every person under his or her authority by name, be able to recognise them on sight.

But, I'm not mandating anything here: nothing but Peace is compulsory. If enough people can get their shit together and agree it, maybe there will still be Communities the size of Russia. But I'm basically talking about the fundamental right to secede being extended right down to it's logical end point: the Individual.

If you can master the basics, if you can manage your foreign policy and foreign exchange, if you can do it all without hurting anyone else: I must totally support your right to form the Democratic Independent Republic of Alaskan_Viking.

Everyone should try and Experience what it is like to be a Head of State at least once in their lifetime!
