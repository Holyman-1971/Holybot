Writing creatively and engagingly is a key part of my work. I have had a few articles and letters published, on-line and off-line, but most of my professional output is fairly dry, technical stuff.

Whilst there are obvious differences between writing novels and works of non-fiction, there are general rules that apply to the use of language in both.

Possibly the most important of those rules, as in so many areas of Life is, to keep it simple, stupid!

I occasionally glance back at some of my adolescent scribblings and shudder at my verbosity and circumlocution. Where I used complicated or uncommon words, not to communicate an idea, but to demonstrate how great my vocabulary is�

You are trying to communicate either a story or ideas, or both. You are not trying to demonstrate your mastery of language, but your ability to communicate. Begin with the story and/or ideas clearly and simply laid out, at least in your mind, if not in pictorial form. Then build your language around those ideas, describing those things you wish to communicate to the reader, and which as a reader you would most like to know.

Remember that writing is more of an art-form than it is a technical skill. Your writing will improve, the more you write. Don�t imagine that you need to create a landmark of literary prose in your first novel. You want to write a book that will get published first. You don�t need to worry about winning awards until you are at least on your fifth or sixth published work..! ;-)

If you feel you have a magnum opus in you (and who doesn�t?), like Tolkein�s �Lord of the Rings�, try not to feel like that is the novel you have to write first. Establish yourself as a published writer first, before you try to write the book that will change the World.

Of course, one of the best things to do is read other author�s works. Reading as a reader is very different to reading as a prospective writer. Try to pick a successful author, with an established canon of work, and pay attention to how their style evolves, what linguistic devices they use, and perhaps most importantly, how their confidence develops with each subsequent book published.

Terry Pratchett�s �Discworld� series is a great example. Read the first novel in the series, �The Colour of Magic�, and then read the latest (�Thud!� � I think). Compare and contrast the difference in language and syntactic structure.

Your aim is to hold a reader�s attention. You can do this with your subject matter, your ideas and your language. Ideally, you want to do it with all three.

I can get away with writing turgid and verbose posts on this forum, if my subject matter is another forum member, because I know that their ego will hold them through the post, no matter how obscure the ideas or foggy the language is. You won�t find it that easy to do that, writing fiction for a mass readership, but you can develop characters that readers can identify with.

Make sure your ideas are clear in your head before you begin to write. Again, on these forums, when I write, I�m often developing the ideas as I go. Indeed, often the idea isn�t fully-formed until I finish and hit the submit form: I�m probably my own, most attentive reader� Any prospective publisher you send your work to though, will want to read an exposition of fully-formed ideas in your work, rather than an aimless stream of consciousness, at least for your first few novels. There are successful writers of streams of consciousness, but they are few and far between.

Clear and entertaining language, can bring life and interest to even the dullest subjects and ideas. Verbal flourishes and lexical �signatures� are the literary equivalent of guitar riffs and drum syncopation. Keep them in rhythm, keep them in harmony, and you can write literary symphonies that people will enjoy reading purely for the reading�s sake.

There are countless tips, tricks and best practices for clear and entertaining language, and you shouldn�t rule out joining a writer�s group or guild to gain and share these methods.

One of these tricks/tips, is to pay attention to how �foggy� your prose is. It is possible to work out a �Fog Index� for any piece of prose, and indeed, most word processors (e.g. MS Word) have automated the task. The �Fog Index� of any piece of writing is based on how long the average sentence is, and how many of the words have three or more syllables.

Anything with a Fog Index above 40 is going to be virtually unreadable to most readers. Anything below 20 is going to be an easy read, and is most likely to communicate and convey your ideas most effectively.

The council also agreed that the programme be presented to the Joint Management Committees and senior representatives in the plants concerned to obtain their endorsement. Endorsement has been obtained from all plants except Clifton where meetings are being held today at shop floor level seeking a commitment satisfactory to everyone.

At meetings there earlier this week it was emphasised that commitment was required by 4pm Thursday 7 October as the NEB and the Company were meeting on Friday 8 October; without that commitment no submission could be made. Another useful tip is to use the �Active Voice� over the �Passive Voice�.

Unfortunately, but traditionally, education normally encourages the use of the passive voice since it sounds more formal. Take this example:

The requested working documents will be provided. However, recommendations cannot be given until further discussions have been held with our subcontractors.

In this form it is hard to read, formal and distant. In the active voice it is much more effective:

We will provide the working documents you have requested. However, we cannot give you our recommendations until we hold further discussions with our subcontractors.

The simplest way of describing the �Active Voice�, is to, uh, say it follows the �write as you speak� principle.

In the active voice, the subject of the sentence acts upon the object. In other words, the subject is the ACTOR. For example:

I (the subject) wrote (the verb) this post (the object).

In the passive voice, however, the subject is ACTED UPON by the object of the sentence. As in:

This post (subject) was written (verb) by me (object).

Here�s that above, turgid, impenetrable and foggy press release, written with a much lower fog index (around 20-ish I think), and using the active voice:

During his recent visits to all participation committees, David White outlined future spending plans: no submissions for any major capital expenditure will go for final approval, without commitment to improving work rates from employee representatives, involved in each project.

The ADO 99 project programme has been agreed by the National Cars Joint Management Council following discussions. The council also agreed to the presenting of the programme to the Joint Management Committee and Senior Plant Representatives for endorsement.

Endorsement has been obtained from all plants except Clifton. Meetings will be held there today to seek a commitment satisfactory to everyone.

Still boring information, but much easier to read and recall!

It is impossible to over-emphasise the importance of practice in writing. Whilst it is encouraging to practice writing for an appreciative audience, positive criticism or plaudits are not your aim.

The aim is to develop confidence and fluency in your writing, so that you can forget about the method and activity of writing, and get on with the creative expression of your ideas and stories.

You will always want as many people as possible to proof-read your work. Welcome corrections and adjustments from your readership. If you�re not making mistakes: you�re not trying hard enough.

A potential publisher will not care about grammar, spelling or �good� English: that�s what they employ editors for. They will however look for confidence in your prose style, and the fluidity of your ideas. They won�t be looking for the next J.K. Rowling or Stephen King (e.g. long-term revenue stream); they will be looking for a single book that will generate average sales.

As and when you write the breakthrough novel that garners massive sales, you can start to think about getting your magnum opus written and published. But until then, store it up until your writing ability has developed sufficiently to do it justice.
