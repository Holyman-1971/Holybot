���������������Well, strictly speaking, dogma can help to develop a *specific* understanding of a *specific* religion.

If the purpose of being religious is strict adherence to and observance of the established (by humans) precepts of an Earthly institution (insert religion of choice), then dogma is the way forward.

However, if the purpose of religion is to promote a positive, productive and purposeful relationship between Self and God, then one must necessarily discard dogma, in favour of a personal, responsible and dynamic exploration of that relationship.

There was a time when many of �The Flock� was illiterate, unsophisticated and largely terrified of what life held in store for them, and of course, what followed life. Simplistic, authoritative, generic dogma then served the purpose of �mass communication�. The �media� outlets were the pulpits of churches and cathedrals, and the �anchors� were priests and pastors.

But as I am quite certain God intended (and what God wants, God gets), we have, in the Western World at least, become more sophisticated, more intelligent, more capable of making our own minds up about our own lives.

It isn�t about the specific qualities of a particular dogma either. It may well be that a seeker of Spiritual Enlightenment through the Church of Christ may well form his or her own conclusions about God and life, which ultimately exactly match established dogma and doctrine. The point is, their conclusions will be their own, tailored by and for their self.

Ultimately life is about the journey, not the destination. When we consume our �Spiritual� beliefs, wholesale, from the nearest religious vendor, we are denying ourselves the possibility of experiencing our own, personal voyage of discovery.

Jesus never asked to be obeyed, he asked only that others might follow him; for their own benefit, not his. But Jesus also emphasised that it has to be a journey made through choice, through Love of him; not a journey of suffering, compelled by fear of God.

To listen to the ceaseless advertising jingles trumpeted forth from the outlets of organised religions, it is understandable why some have formed a different opinion.

Dogma then, *can* help to promote a greater understanding of religion; but surely the task in hand is to discover a higher awareness of Spirituality, of our Spiritual Nature.
