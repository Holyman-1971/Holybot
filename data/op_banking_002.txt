So we've dealt with "Consumer Banking", which is the type of banking most of us are familiar with. It is where we store our money, on the understanding that we can get it back whenever and (mostly) wherever we want it.

Investment Banks are financial institutions that act as intermediaries in complex corporate transactions, such as mergers and acquisitions. And that usually means providing funds (or "liquidity") to facilitate such transactions. Generally speaking, Investment Banks will also help do the paperwork (or pass that role off to a big consultancy firm, like KPMG or PWC for example).

The core activity of Investment Banking is lending money (usually very large amounts of money).

However, the money that is loaned by an Investment Bank, is itself borrowed.

Ultimately, that money is borrowed from one or more Central Banks, e.g. The Bank of England, European Central Bank, U.S. Federal Reserve &c. With the Central Bank creating the money out of thin air and crediting it to the Investment Bank. The bank then lends the money on, at a higher rate of interest than it cost to borrow the money from the Central Bank.

In the Olden Days, this used to be called the "3-6-3 Rule":

[i]"Borrow at 3%, lend at 6%, and be on the golf course by 3pm."[/i]

Contemporary interest rates mean it is currently closer to a "0.5-3-3" rule, but the principle is still the same.

That's kind of it for Investment Banking.

Investment Banks do control very, very large amounts of capital, but none of it belongs to them. If your business is making money out of money, then your money needs to be out working, rather than sitting in a vault somewhere. And thanks to the principle of "Fractional Reserve Banking", most Investment Banks typically owe around 10 times the amount of capital they own. Sometimes, much, much more.

Investment Banks are at the top of the Human Economic Pyramid. Politicians and Captains of Industry grovel at the feet of Investment Bankers (especially those involved in the Bond Market). They exist solely to make very large amounts of money out of handling others' financial transactions.

A hybrid of Loan-Sharking and Bookmaking for institutional gamblers.

Take a deep dive into the operations and processes of an Investment Bank, and they will look pretty much identical to any other Investment Bank.

The actual instruments used for trading money evolve continually, and since computers got involved, have become incomprehensibly complex. They have become what Warren Buffet has called, "Weapons of Mass Financial Destruction". He actually called them that in the late 1990's, warning about the explosive growth of Collateralised Debt Obligations (CDO's) for Investment Banking, and Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS's) for consumers.

And it was CDO's, MBS's and Credit Default Swaps (CDS's) that broke the Global Banking System in 2008; and put it on the Life Support it has been on ever since.
