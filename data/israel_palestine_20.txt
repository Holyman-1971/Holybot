It isn't a question of having to trust Hamas.

We're talking about diplomacy here: long-term conflict resolution and long-term Peace and stability.

In no Human Conflict does one side that has been battering and being battered by the other side ever resort to Trust or Faith.

Conflict resolution and Peace negotiations take place in stages, usually under the mediation of a (generally more powerful) Third Party.

What are Hamas' short-term demands of Israel?

• That Israel lift its blockade of the Gaza Strip

• That Israel cease its military incursions in the Gaza Strip

• That Israel open all border crossings between the Gaza Strip and the outside World

• That Israel completely withdraw its occupation of the Gaza Strip.

All reasonable and fair demands.

What are Israel's short-term demands of Hamas?

• That Hamas cease firing rockets at Southern Israel.

Again, perfectly reasonable and fair.

What are Hamas' long-term demands of Israel?

• That Israel formally recognise the independent state of Palestine, as it existed in 1967, with Jerusalem as its capital

• That Israel ends its occupation of the West Bank as well as the Gaza Strip

• That Israel dismantles all settlements on Occupied Palestinian Territory

• That Israel permit the one million or so Palestinian refugees the right to return to their Homeland.

These are not only reasonable and fair demands by Hamas; they are what is demanded of Israel by the International Community through United Nations Security Council Resolution 242, as well as several subsequent U.N. Resolutions.

Israel's long-term demand is that Hamas cease to exist.

Israel will not recognise Hamas, or the Palestinian State, let alone enter into Peace negotiations with Hamas - the democratically elected Government of the Palestinians.

And Israel justifies this position by saying that Hamas' [b]*ACTUAL*[/b] long-term goals are:

• The destruction of the Modern State of Israel

• The expulsion and/or eradication of all Jews from Israel/Palestine.

Now have a good think about those claims 'Slacks; and think about how the Peace Process might actually play out.

Do you seriously imagine that Hamas and/or the Palestinians could pull a bait-and-switch when it comes to destruction of the Modern State of Israel and the expulsion and/or eradication of all Jews from Israel/Palestine?

Can you seriously imagine a scenario whereby Hamas gets what it claims to want - a Palestinian State within the 1967 borders, with East Jerusalem as its Capital, and the return of all Palestinian refugees - and then suddenly go:

[color=green]"A-ha! Fooled you! We really [b]*DID*[/b] want to destroy Israel and kill all of the Jews! We've got you now! Ha-ha-ha!"[/color]

How exactly would that play out?

Let's imagine, for a moment, that somehow, Israel acquiesced to what Hamas, Fatah, the PLO and the International Community have been demanding, requesting and pleading for these last few decades, and permitted the establishment of a fully-functioning, independent Palestinian State in the Gaza Strip and the West Bank, free of settlements and the "Separation Barrier".

How long exactly do you imagine it would take the Hamas leadership to build this nascent Palestinian State into a fighting force capable of destroying the Modern State of Israel, and the expulsion and/or eradication of all Jews from Israel/Palestine?

The Egyptians, Jordanians and Syrians are Arab Nations that are well established - for a [b]*VERY*[/b] long time in Egypt's case; and yet they are not remotely capable of destroying Israel, or killing or expelling all Israelis. What risk is there that a newly independent Palestinian State would be capable of doing so?

And honestly, what do you think would happen if, during the Peace Process, Hamas or the Palestinians made one false move? If they even hinted at a resumption of hostility, or a genocidal intent?

It would bring an immediate end to the Peace Process; any gains the Palestinians might have made would instantly be reversed; and the situation would simply revert to what it currently is now. The only difference being that the Palestinians would have lost all credibility and sympathy from the International Community.

For a long time, the British Government refused to have anything to do with the Provisional IRA or Sinn Fein. And likewise, Sinn Fein and PIRA continued to hold a manifesto commitment to the complete expulsion of the British Occupation of the North of Ireland.

But eventually, and in very gradual stages, the Peace Process began, and with each step, the parties to the Conflict began to reconcile, and learn to Trust each other.

There were of course groups that attempted to disrupt the Peace Process, on both the Republican and Unionist sides of the Conflict. But there was sufficient dialogue between the strongest representative factions on both sides of the divide to endure and ignore these attempts to disrupt.

And now, after a fragile process of building trust up between the warring factions in the North of Ireland/Northern Ireland, there is what appears to be a lasting Peace to a Conflict that had been raging for the best part of 300 years - about six times longer, and at times just as bloody as the Israeli-Palestinian Conflict.

Trust is not something that is a prerequisite for Peace Negotiations; it is something that is meant to be a [b]*PRODUCT*[/b] of Peace Negotiations.
