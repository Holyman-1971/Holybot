It's an interesting facet of the political debates around here. If you actually read and absorb what many people around here write, it's hard to actually identify any classical Liberal thought.

However, there are, of course, several posters who are died-in-the-wool classical Conservatives, who religiously adhere to classical Conservative ideology. And part of the rubric of being a Conservative is to label and denounce anyone who doesn't also adhere to Conservative Dogma as "Liberal"/"Socialist"/"Communist"/Whatever...

The correct term that should be used is, of course, "Not a Conservative". Because that's all it really amounts to.

Just because someone doesn't completely and faithfully tow the Conservative Party Line, does not automatically make them a Liberal.

In Truth, I think the majority of the members of our Community - myself included - should be most accurately described as Apolitical - "Post-political"; "Post-Ideological" &c.

It isn't that they aren't politically engaged, or fully up-to-speed on the machinations of our "Leaders"; just that they recognise a Bad System for what it is: impractical; counter-productive; and unsustainable.

To adhere rigidly to any ideological line cast over the side of any Party Political vessel is to participate in that Broken System: it requires that one makes up one's mind about an issue or topic, *BEFORE* considering all the facts and implications.

There is a Full Spectrum of Colour between Black and White. We do ourselves and our Species no favour my trying to divide everything into mutually exclusive, binary alternatives.

[quote="ClayMoore"][color=#FF0000]I agree with you somewhat on the rest of your post that medicine ought not to be for-profit. Sure, that's a wonderful thought, and I think a lot of our basic medicine can be delivered in such a manner. However, when you remove the profit motive, you end up killing medical R&D very quickly. Profit drives the vast majority of cutting edge inventions we've seen in just about every arena of medicine.[/color][/quote]

I'm not sure that I agree with this.

With no exceptions that I can think of, every person I know or have ever met who is a medical professional (or research scientist in any field, come to that), is far more motivated by the prospect of winning the respect of their peers, and contributing positively to the welfare of Humanity as a whole.

I know I could ask any medical professional if they would rather invent or discover a medical breakthrough in order to enjoy the material riches that might follow, or because of the international renown (and a possible place in Human History) that would follow; and without a doubt, everyone would plump for the Nobel Prize over the 40-room mansion, Ferrari and a yacht in the Bahamas.

I'm pretty sure I'm not over-generalising.

If you look at the evolution of Medical Science over the centuries, it is really difficult to identify where the material profit motive fits in. It is only in the post-War period of the 20th Century that you first begin to see Finance Capitalism take an interest in the Pharmaceutical and Medical Technology "Industries".

But again, let's be clear here: medical research costs whatever medical research costs. We need to be clear on the distinction between costs and profit.

Frankly, in the Grand Scheme of Human Society, I would far rather see doctors, nurses and medical researchers paid the kind of money that our Society currently considers bankers and other finance capitalists to be worth. Would be quite happy to see doctors earning north of £/$1million p.a., and the Bankers having to scrimp by on merely $/£100k a year...

Point being that nobody is talking about starving the Medical establishment of funding. If the current total cost for medical research (and service delivery) = 'X', then 'X' is the amount of money that needs to be raised (through whatever facilities).

I'm just talking about eliminating the 'Y' from the equation; where 'Y' = Gross Profit.

Well, "confiscation" is a strong word, but I take your point.

I don't actually approve of the fact that a portion of my earnings are confiscated by the British Government, in order to pay for "Wars" of choice against people in poorly-developed parts of the World. I certainly don't approve when a portion of those confiscated funds are used to purchase ("renew") an entirely anachronistic Cold War-era nuclear missile system ("Trident").

And don't get me started on how "my" government uses the money it confiscates from me to bail out the bad gambles made by bankers...

Point is that under the present system, We, the People, don't get to decide how the money confiscated from us/contributed by us gets spent.

Personally, I believe we should.

If you don't want your contributions/confiscations spent of subsidising health care for those less able to afford it, then you shouldn't have to pay for it.

If I don't want my contributions/confiscations spent on blowing up brown-skinned people, then I shouldn't have to pay for that either.

I really don't see how, in this day and age of Information Technology, that managing and processing this kind of discretionary tax contribution dispersal would be a problem.

*OF COURSE* our politicians and civil servants will tell us that it would be a nightmare to implement - and doubtless if we ("The People") went ahead with the Project, those same politicians and civil servants would do their best to make it a nightmare - but this is because they have a vested interest in having our contributions/confiscations all going into a single pot, out of which they control the disbursement.

But I work with some fantastically complex financial trading systems, logistical databases, and actuarial and accounting applications. Instituting a System whereby tax-payers determine *EXACTLY* how their tax contributions are spent (and indeed, how much tax contribution they make), is entirely feasible - actually, fairly straightforward by the standards of today's Information Technology.

I personally believe that all taxation should be voluntary. I do also believe that the amount contributed in tax should also be transparent, publicly available information; as well as the choices made by individuals on how their contributions are allocated.

The only thing I'm not sure on is whether that makes me a Conservative or a Liberal..?!?
