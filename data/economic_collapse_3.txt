It's no longer about great or innovative ideas, or the ability to produce manufactured goods of better quality. You're in the Big World now, and manufacturing output moves to the countries where the wages are lowest, and the employment conditions most lax.

All that's left is the service industry, and particularly for the U.S. and U.K., that means Financial Services.

And Financial Services is all about making money out of money: out of thin-air basically.

Capital, such as it is, goes to the money markets where it can multiply itself with the minimum of regulatory oversight. Unfortunately, greed encourages great irresponsibility on the part of those with the greatest amounts of capital to multiply.

Unregulated market conditions are only of benefit, if those working in the markets can exercise self-restraint. Otherwise, they have a tendency to cook up all manner of arcane financial instruments, utterly incomprehensible to the outsider, and almost always, morally and socially reprehensible.

You can make money out of money, because money is just an abstract, notional system of exchange. What you can't make out of thin-air, is value.

Those at the top of the money-pile make so much money, that the precise number of zeroes becomes irrelevant; they quickly surpass the point where they can actually spend all the money they have, on anything that has any value to them. So they just reinvest it in more and more sophisticated and risky schemes, and create even more money for themselves.

Up to a point.

And now that the property bubble has burst, all those who took on larger and larger lines of credit, are now the indentured slaves of their creditors; doomed to spending the rest of their lives paying interest on a loan they will never be able to repay.
