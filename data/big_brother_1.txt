I had no idea that you guys were such strong supporters of child abuse.

See, I realise that you all live in idyllic, Utopian societies, where every single parent raises their children as physically and socially healthy model citizens. I realise that you have no problems with parents neglecting their children, and no problems with children below the age of criminal responsibility committing crimes. And I realise that you don't have any families where normal "parenting" is considered to be shouting, screaming, swearing at and hitting the kids.

But what you've got to try and realise is that there [b]IS[/b] some of that going on the UK. Thankfully it's not wide-spread, but there are sufficient cases to give Brits strong cause for concern. There have been a number of high-profile cases recently, including:

Also in the UK, and completely unlike the Rest of the World, we have problems with families that behave in a deeply anti-social manner towards their neighbours. Again, I realise that this is something completely unheard of in your own societies, but it is something that we still struggle with in the UK.

The problem with addressing the issue of these families is one of manpower and resources, which are always too limited to provide 24-hour monitoring of families known to be or suspected of being abusive and anti-social.

The main problem with just locking these people up, is that generally the Police cannot act until [b]AFTER[/b] a child has been physically abused or severely neglected; or [b]AFTER[/b] a family's anti-social behaviour has metastasized into far more serious crimes.

So under pressure from the Public and Media, the Government and Social Services have looked into ways of increasing the monitoring and surveillance of families that have been placed on the "Watch List".

One finding is that giving problem families increased monitoring in the shape of CCTV surveillance within their homes, is a more preferable alternative to splitting siblings up into foster-care and putting the parents behind bars for short prison terms.

Now I suspect that some of you may have inadvertently got the wrong end of the stick, and suspect that this may be the first iteration of a British Government programme to install CCTV monitoring equipment in the homes of all private citizens, [i]a la[/i] Orwell's "1984". And of course it is completely understandable and forgivable why you may have come to that erroneous conclusion, given the [i]prima facie[/i] evidence presented and framed by the Media.

But let me reassure you that far from this monitoring equipment being installed in the homes of private British citizens, it is only proposed that it be installed in the homes of those individuals known to be or strongly suspected of committing crimes of child neglect and abuse, and/or anti-social behaviour.

If CCTV monitoring were not an option, then these individuals would likely as not lose their liberty anyway, and therefore cease to be private citizens. Just as electronic bracelets are used on some convicts as an alternative to incarceration, so too the proposed CCTV monitoring systems.

Of course, the over-riding objective is to [b]PREVENT[/b] child abuse and neglect, before it is too late. There is little or no satisfaction in successfully prosecuting a child abuser or someone guilty of serial neglect. Far better to prevent the crime taking place in the first place, and to protect the innocent children.

But like I said, I can completely understand why many of you feel that this story is more about the arrival of some Orwellian Surveillance State, than it is about the prevention of child abuse and anti-social behaviour. Because obviously such horrendous crimes and behaviours are completely unknown in your own societies.

And even if such incidents were taking place in your own society, I completely understand why you would prefer child abuse and neglect to continue, if the only alternative was planting the seed of what could, in some media-fertilised hysterical fantasy, become a Real Life manifestation of a fictional construct.
