[quote=AlaskanViking][color=firebrick]3)	Laissez faire economics are superior to government interventionism on moral and utilitarian grounds.[/color][/quote]

[i]Laissez-faire[/i] economics [b]*ARE*[/b] superior to government interventionism, but only on utilitarian grounds - I don't really have time for morality, which is only ever a subjective matter of perspective.

Government can't organise a piss-up in a brewery. Government is dominated by individuals obsessed with the pursuit of power (for good reasons and bad). Government is also, currently, a very expensive affair for those who desire to govern.

Put all those qualities anywhere near vast quantities of money, and you have a recipe for massively destructive corruption.

So a genuinely free economic system is definitely preferable to a government-managed economic system of any kind.

However, on the question of practicality:

Industrial Capitalism, Finance Capitalism, and the current Industrial-Grade Finance Capitalism that dominated the Human Economy has as much potential to cause suffering and destruction, as it has potential to encourage, deliver and reward Human ingenuity and creativity.

How would you feel if a nuclear power station was built within, say, three miles of your home?

You might have mixed emotions, because you can recognise both the potential advantages and the potential dangers.

Alright then. How would you feel if a nuclear power station was built without any safety measures such as safeguards and regular inspections?

I mean, it [i]should[/i] be okay... Nuclear power stations don't go wrong all that often, so you'll probably be okay. And hey, without the added expense of a Health & Safety regime, you'll be able to get your energy at a much cheaper rate, right?

That's the only view I have of regulations for Finance Capitalism AV: that there needs to be Health & Safety measures in place. Because the potential for damage when something goes seriously wrong with the Economic System, is arguably wider-reaching and longer-lasting than that which can be caused by a nuclear power station going into meltdown.

What the precise nature of those Financial "Health & Safety" regulations should be is open to discussion. But I would have thought that at the very least, a system of compartmentalisation to prevent an economic "fire" or contagion spreading throughout the whole system should be a basic minimum.

Leaving it up to pyromaniacs and virus-creators to manage Health & Safety for the Human Economy just strikes me as asking for trouble.

The only thing I could think of that would be worse, would be letting [i]Government[/i] do it.

[quote=AlaskanViking][color=firebrick]4) The only proper role of the state is to protect its citizens from foreign attack, and to protect their natural rights from violence and fraud. [/color][/quote]

Well that's the Statist view, isn't it?

I don't personally believe that "The State" should exist at all - I'm not a Statist.

I think that "protection from foreign attack" has always been, pretty much, the standard justification for the creation of "The State". I understand the historic need for collective defence, and safety in numbers and all that, but I have grown up in a post-United Nations World, where actually, "foreign attacks" are a thing of the past. And on the odd occasion where a "foreign attack" has taken place, it has been met by a combined, collective response of the community of nations as a whole.

I think that [i]Citizens[/i] should organise their own protection from any and every form of violence, foreign or domestic. How they do that, and with who and how many other citizens they choose to do that with is up to them.

I mean, while it can fairly be said that "The State" [i]has[/i] protected many of "The State's Citizens [sic]" in the past; it can equally fairly be said that States have imperilled those same Citizens on at least as many occasions.

So, you know, in summary: I'm all for Health and Safety controls; and I'm all for practical measures that can ensure the sustainability of a Society.

But as with the Internet, I'm fairly sure that those measures and controls would be more effectively realised, if carried out on a peer-to-peer model, rather than a client-server one.
