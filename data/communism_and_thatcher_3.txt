Take a look at the relationship between Truman and [url=http://www.doug-long.com/byrnes.htm]James F. Byrnes, Truman's Secretary of State[/url].

Byrnes was to Truman what Dick Cheney was to George W. Bush.

Don't know if you caught the recent "House of Cards" drama with Kevin Spacey; but the character that Spacey played, Frank Underwood, was essentially modelled on Byrne.

Franklin Roosevelt's anointed successor was the guy who had been Roosevelt's Vice President from 1940, [url=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_A._Wallace]Henry A. Wallace[/url]. But despite his overwhelming popularity with the American Public and the DNC's grassroots, he was considered to be too Left Wing for the tastes of the DNC Leadership, and by extension, America's Power Elite as it looked at the post-War World.

What followed were some very shady shenanigans at the [url=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_Democratic_National_Convention]1944 Democratic National Convention[/url], that resulted in a virtually unknown and entirely lightweight Missouri Senator being parachuted in as the "Anyone-but-Wallace" candidate.

The stakes were understood by all involved to be the highest possible, since it was obvious that Roosevelt wasn't going to see out his (unprecedented) Fourth Term. Therefore the nomination for Roosevelt's running mate in the 1944 Election was no less than the nomination for the next President of the United States (without the messy business of allowing all American citizens to choose their next President).

Truman's personal attributes as President were characterised by a cluelessness that wouldn't be matched again until Reagan, and of course, George W. Bush. James Byrnes ran Foreign Policy, and [url=http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Forrestal]James Forrestal[/url] created the post of first U.S. Secretary of Defense (a position that Roosevelt never felt necessary to create during World War II...).

Domestically, Edgar J. Hoover successfully ran an effective Police State; and it was under the Truman Administration that the House Un-American Activities Committee became a Standing Committee that oversaw one of the most shameful episodes ever witnessed in "The Land of the Free".

Truman was presented as a no-nonsense, bluff and forthright President. But the reality was that as with George W. Bush, he was a small-minded man of limited intellectual capacity who demonstrated no understanding of the forces that were nominally under his control.
