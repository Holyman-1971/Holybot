Okay, so, the kind of fighting I most enjoy is usually conducted from about 12 miles behind enemy lines. Whilst I sip cool glasses of Sancerre, I like to know that I�ve got plenty of ideological crusaders doing the actual killing and dying, as far away from my mess tent as possible.

Anyway, since I�m tending to play Commander a lot more these days, particularly as my rank is giving me increasing precedence over other pretenders, I thought it might be a good idea to set the record straight about one or two things. Well, several things actually, so I best get started.

A BF2 Field Commander has at their disposal, four support mechanisms: satellite scanner; UAV; Artillery; and supplies.

The scanner runs from the whirly-radar looking thing, and allows the commander to �wipe� the whole map, and see the positions of every enemy soldier. (On a side note, I personally feel that it shouldn�t display Special Ops or Snipers�)

The UAV runs from the caravan-trailer thing, and allows the commander to position it wherever he likes, to show his own troops the position of other troops in their immediate area.

The artillery is obviously the most ubiquitous evidence of a commander�s presence, and can be directed at any point, anywhere on the map.

And supplies can be dropped at 2-3 minute intervals, also anywhere on the map.

And that is pretty much all a BF2 Commander can do, independently.

Anything else he or she may or may not be able to do, is dictated by how many players on their side, form into squads. A Commander has no way of issuing instructions to Lone Wolves, nor even issuing a warning (beyond the very obvious red-flashing artillery gun) about artillery in their area. Supplies and UAV�s can occasionally be provided, if the Commander is quick enough to identify the flashing blue/yellow of the Lone Wolf�s position, and relating it back to a recent request for supplies or support.

Because each Squad Leader has an alternative �Squad Leader�s Commo Rose� button, which allows them to request artillery, UAV or supplies; requests which the Commander can respond to, with a simple click of their mouse on �Accept� or �Refuse�.

But here�s the thing, there is nothing to stop a Lone Wolf forming their own, single-unit squad, and locking it to prevent anyone else joining it � if they�re not team players, or are frightened of being shouted at. The advantage provided by a single-unit squad, over a non-squadded Lone Wolf, is the availability of the �Squad Leader�s Commo Rose�.

Obviously it makes *far more* sense to form larger squads, but if a player isn�t comfortable with doing that, they really should consider forming a one-man squad. It becomes then a *lot* easier for the Commander to assist them.

Unless of course they want to even it out and give the Commander the opportunity to either boot insubordinate players from their side, shoot them for desertion, or press-gang them into squad-service.

Legitimate mutinies are those initiated by fully-formed squads, doing as they are told by their Commander, but suffering badly as a consequence.

An illegitimate, or �Stupid Bastard� mutiny, is one initiated by a Lone Wolf, or �n00b�, who doesn�t understand the game mechanics, the Commander�s ability, and/or personal responsibility and respect for others.
