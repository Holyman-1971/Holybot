The rather large and quite fundamental problem with Paulson's "Bail-Out" plan, is that it isn't a bail-out plan at all.

Rather, it is a way of giving U.S. tax-payers' (actually, [b]*FUTURE*[/b] tax-payers', since all the money has to be borrowed) money to Wall Street insiders, to sell out all their bad bets.

The situation that currently exists between the Bush Administration, Congress and Wall Street, is not wholly dissimilar to the situation that played out in 1996, in Russia, between Boris Yeltsin, the "Seven Bankers" and the "Oligarchs".

Paulson argues that the problem to be addressed by his "Troubled Asset Relief Program" (TARP), is that of "illiquidity". He argues that because the mortgage "assets" held by the banks have lost (so much) value, they are "...choking off the flow of credit that is so vitally important to our economy.".

But if the problem were merely that these bad loans were "illiquid", then that problem would only be temporary. And the Federal Reserve exists to provide liquidity to gummed up financial markets - for good collateral, of course...

The actual problem is that the face value of all the mortgage and other loans, [b]*FAR*[/b] exceeds current or likely future market prices. It is for this reason that the CDO packages are rightly described as "Toxic".

Paulson's plan can only deal with the banks' insolvency by buying (for the U.S. taxpayer) bad assets at far above their true value. His plan then [b]*GUARANTEES*[/b] huge losses for U.S. taxpayers, at the same time as it provides an open-ended bail-out only to the most irresponsible investors.

The key here is simple: if debts cannot be paid, then creditors [b]*MUST*[/b] take losses.

The idea that U.S. taxpayers should underwrite Wall Street's bad gambles is not only morally repugnant, but it breaks a fundamental tenet of Economics.

The general attitude amongst both lenders and - bizarrely - borrowers, has always been that lenders are always doing borrowers a favour, when they deign to lend the borrowers money. The social and moral "favour" that lenders provide, when they are kind enough to lend money to those who desire to borrow it, has created the mood that lenders should always be protected from the irresponsibility of borrowers.

But as any Mafiosi loan-shark will tell you: if you lend money to someone, you better be ready to deal with the real possibility that you may not get your money back.

How you choose to deal with a default, depends on your business, and uh, legal model...

But the idea that lenders can or should turn to Society's tax-payers to compensate them for the fecklessness and irresponsibility of borrowers, is as morally misplaced as it is deleterious to the whole concept of Economics.

Pity the poor lenders...

The real problem that the U.S. has right now, is that foreign investors no longer trust the U.S. investment bankers or money managers to be honest. Many of the loans that have been packaged into CDO's, are outright fraudulent, and they are being sold by crooks who work for banks.

The idea that U.S. taxpayers should permit their government to borrow money in their name, to give to these crooks, is an idea that Paulson & Co. would really rather have hoped would not come out. But it would have taken monumental and crass stupidity for Americans not to have figured this out, and this is the reason why Bush and Paulson are - or were - hoping to get the legislation required passed through, before too many people noticed.

Fact of the matter is that the lenders - the bankers - [b]*MUST*[/b] take their hits: take their losses.

A "bail-out" is designed to keep the boat afloat, but the existing Wall Street "boat" [b]*MUST*[/b] sink. It is only a question of who sinks with it, and who swims.

Writing off the debts that have accrued in such a toxic financial tsunami is an absolute necessity to drive [b]*DOWN*[/b] the volume of debt within the U.S. Economy. Paulson's plan is to drive debt levels [b]*UP*[/b], by keeping CDO's alive, and of course, increasing the U.S. National debt.

But it is the level of debt that is punishing and threatening to kill the U.S. Economy. The debt must be reduced, not expanded and spread around.

The worst part of the plan - and this is where it carries shades of Yeltsin and Russia - is that those investors who receive U.S. Government money in exchange for their bad loans and gambles, will take that money and move it abroad, to a "hard" currency country.

Well, these are smart and greedy guys: they are hardly likely to invest in an Economy as weak and faltering as the United States, now are they?

On top of the expansion of the U.S. National Debt, this will guarantee the collapse of the U.S. Dollar, and immediately create a Russian-style post-Soviet economy in America.

The U.S. Economy [b]*MUST*[/b] take the hit from all this toxic debt; and it must take it now. The only "plan" or "solution" that can work, is one that drives [b]*DOWN*[/b] the level of debt within the American Economy; not one that maintains the current level of debt, and certainly not one that increases the level of indebtedness.

It's going to be hard to take that hit - and it will be hard for [b]*ALL*[/b] of us in this entirely Globalised World we are already living in (you know: the one everyone keeps telling me will never happen... :)). But putting off that hit, as the Bush Administration has been doing for too long now, will only make it all the harder when those losses are finally and fully accounted for.

It's time to pay the Piper.
