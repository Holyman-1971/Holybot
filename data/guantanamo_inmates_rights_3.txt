Surprised you don't know Foota.

The function of a spy is to steal information from an enemy source and report that information back to base.

Stealing information regarding an enemy's forces, capabilities, plans etc., and reporting that back to the friendly side, weakens the enemy's chances in battle and war.

Once a spy has been identified, it is impossible to know what information he may have already stolen. Returning him to his own side, after no matter how long in captivity, runs the risk of allowing the information he has or may have stolen, to fall into enemy hands.

Therefore, in time of War, spies are executed as soon as their identity as spies has been established. (Spies are treated less harshly in peace-time...)

A soldier on the other hand, exists to fight; keeping a soldier as a prisoner of war, means that he is not able to fight against you. However, he has committed no crime, and as soon as hostilities have ceased, he will be returned home; or possibly earlier, in a prisoner of war exchange.

And that is why spies are classified differently to soldiers, by the Geneva Conventions Foota.
