It depends what you're after in London, we have plenty of things to cater for all tastes. :)

You'll be wanting to keep well clear of Piccadilly Circus and Leicester Square - Bright Lights, Stupid Idiots - though Covent Garden is worth hanging around, particularly during the day.

Covent Garden is in the heart of London's Theatreland, so you can expect to see a lot of stage-wannabe's doing street-theatre (and yes, you are legally allowed to beat the crap out of mime-artists in the UK).

For other day-time hangouts in London, your best bet is to head up to the Angel, Islington. It is full of hep-cats and all, but there's some good pubs and bars up there where you can find out what's going down in Groove Town.

Camden and the Market, obviously, is also another must visit, particularly if you're going on to Amsterdam - Camden will act like a sort of halfway house, so you'll want to leave Camden 'til just before you leave London for Holland.

A lot also depends on what days of the week you're going to be visiting, 'cos obviously Mon-Wed isn't exactly hot-banana time in London.

For night-time fun, the Capital is your oyster really, and it's really a question of your tastes. If you're into chunky house music, then somewhere like Pasha, right next to Victoria Station is cool enough. Because of its proximity to the station, it's mostly non-locals that go to Pasha; but, on the other hand, it's mostly non-locals that go to Pasha... So you'll probably make friends more quickly there.

If you're into more committed dance music, then head up to Clerkenwell (for Turnmills Club) and King's Cross (for The Cross Club); that's where most of the groovy chicks tend to flash their navels.

If you prefer something less chemical, more alcohol based, then visit Earl's Court to the west of the City. Earl's Court is populated almost entirely my Australians, New Zealanders and South Africans, and those boys know how to let their hair down. :)

Antipodeans in London are a lot more friendly and approachable than the locals, as are many other of the immigrant minority groups in London, but Aussies & Kiwis speak the same language as you, and won't regard you as an annoying tourist.

There are plenty of places where you can drink around the clock now, particularly in the ANZAC ghettoes around Earl's Court and Shepherd's Bush. Plus, Aussie and Kiwi birds are a doddle to pull, so if you're looking for a friendly face to sit on while you're here, that's where you should head.

When you get to Amsterdam, the absolute, very most important thing that you will absolutely, mostly do as absolutely soon as you get there is... Dump all of your baggage and travelling stuff off wherever it is you're staying.

Then, all you will take out with you into Amsterdam will be as many folded Euro notes as you think you are going to need until you return to where you're staying; and whatever key or hotel identification you need to get back into where you are staying.

Then, you go out and hit the 'Dam!

{cue audio: "All Along the Watchtower" by the Jimi Hendrix Experience)

The first thing to remember about Amsterdam is that it is full of Dutch people. And Dutch people are just about the loveliest people in the Whole World (except Scandinavians, of course).

Every single Dutch girl is six foot 3 inches tall, is blonde, has really great tits, legs that go on for ever, and they all cycle lazily around Amsterdam giving wistful smiles to stoned English tourists.

Well, in my experience anyway...

Every sinle Dutch bloke on the other hand, is seven foot 9 inches tall, is built like a brick bijgebouw, holds 97th degree blackbelts in kickboxing, jiu-jitsu and tae kwan do, and is the most placid and gentle human fighting-machine you could ever wish to meet.

All of which means that if anything untoward should happen to you in Amsterdam, you are in very safe hands.

And that's kind of important, because pretty much the first thing that any decent tourist does (when they've dropped off their luggage..!) is go and get shit-faced on spliffs and/or cakes. Thing is, marijuana never killed anyone, but it can make you feel a bit out-of-sorts if you're not used to the strength of weed you can get in Amsterdam. But the key thing is always not to panic, the Dutch are as used to people being floored by dope as just about everyone else is used to dealing with drunken assholes.

The best coffee-shop for variety and quality of marijuana is the Dampkring, which is to the South of the city, behind the road called Rokin. It also happens to be the coffee shop where they filmed a scene or two in "Ocean's Twelve" - but I saw the shop before George and Brad.

Other coffee shops are easier to find, particularly in the Red Light district. And I can recommend Club-36 if you can find it, just behind the main Grasshopper opposite Central Station.

And you'll be going to the Red Light district at the height of Holiday Season, so all the *really* fit girls from Eastern Europe will be working - and they're a must see!
