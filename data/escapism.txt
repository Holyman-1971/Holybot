�	�	�	[�	�	�	�	��������OK, so, here's the deal:

On the one hand, we have what is laughingly called and generally agreed to be "Real Life" "!. And, on the other, we have Escapism - that is, the avenues by which we *all* try to escape "Real Life" "!.

Now different people use different methods to escape from their different lives. And, anyone reading this, right here and right now, uses or has used playing the BF2 Game to help them escape from their  Real Life  "!.

*No-one* who plugs in their headset/joystick/Z-board and fires up this Game does so in order to enter just another mundane extension of their "Real Life" "!. The enthusiasm, the passion, the emotional commitment of players in-Game, and forum users, is the clearest possible indication that we are all involved in some form of escapism.

Because, BF2 is *not* "Real Life" "!.

OK then, having established that, we can now all agree that whatever or whoever it is we try and/or succeed in being, in this escapist framework we call BF2, is hopefully something or someone that doesn t bear any resemblance to what or who we are in "Real Life" "!. To insist that we are or should be as we are in  Real Life  "!, is not only to miss the point of online Gaming, but drags into our escapist World an unwelcome dose of reality.

More than that, anyone trying to negatively contrast a person's in-Game/in-Forum persona with speculation on that individual's "Real Life" "! personality or circumstance, is only actually displaying frustration at their own inability to escape from the drudgery and monotony of their own life.

Now BF2 doesn't say "MMORPG" on the tin. In fact, a very different kind of person is attracted to BF2 than those that are willing to spend hours upon hours searching for the "Lost Ring of Ragnarok", just so that they can participate in some endlessly repeated (and therefore non-epic) Epic. *But!* That doesn't mean that in the larger, supra-game-round culture that surrounds individual games of BF2, there isn't room for people to create their own escapist personalities.

And this is where dear PlaneWhore comes hurtling from the skies, into the picture.

I think it was a few, phat PlaneWhore threads ago that I had a moment of epiphany regarding the boy-Plane-Whore. It was when I realised that if you took BF2 as a much grander, on-going (virtual) War, rather than just a sequence of endlessly (and rather pointlessly) repeated battles and skirmishes, then you can begin to see the emergence of the same kind of on-going "War" culture that existed in our "Real Life" "! World during World War Two.

Now anybody who knows anything about the History of World War Two understands that there were different kinds of fighters, fighting in different kinds of ways. And between those different kinds of fighters, there was both camaraderie and rivalry. Indeed, it was (and is) very likely in time of War that soldiers will feel more affinity with enemy soldiers than they will with say, friendly sailors.

And of course, the one group of fighters whom both soldiers and sailors learned to love to hate in 20th Century warfare, were of course Airmen.
