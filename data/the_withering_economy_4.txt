Umm, not really Spoot.

Economics starts with whoever controls the supply of money; and whoever is tasked with restraining the excesses of Capitalism.

If the Federal Reserve had not created a free money supply (to off-set the economic pain of the Dot.Com crash and 9/11), then institutional banks and brokerages would not have been in a position to make the loans that they did.

And if the Bush Administration had not relaxed regulatory oversight to the degree that it did, then the financial institutions would not have been able to leverage their loan facilities to the degree that they did (see my post above).

The consumer is at the bottom of the Economic Food Chain, and always has been. They are end users. They are not in a position to create the complex, arcane and opaque financial instruments that have created this economic cataclysm.

If the Federal Reserve had raised interest rates to reduce house price inflation, then the defaults on mortgages would not have impacted the banking system as hard as they have. This because the lenders would have simply repossessed the property on which the debt was secured, knowing that it represented the same degree of value as the defaulted loan.

Instead, a combination of interest rate mismanagement on the part of the Federal Reserve, and sheer greed on the part of the lenders, created a catastrophically unstable housing bubble that [b]*HAD*[/b] to burst.

There was nothing that consumer-borrowers - no matter how reckless - could have done to influence this.

This is not "After the Fact" finger-pointing I'm engaged in Spoot. I have been reading (and writing) all about the inevitability of this situation for the last five years. [b]*PLENTY*[/b] of people were shouting at the top of their voices that the current situation was inevitable and unavoidable, if the Bush Administration did not change its policies in regard to interest rates and financial market regulation.

But the Bush Administration did nothing.

This isn't a partisan issue by the way - I'm [b]*NOT*[/b] partisan. I very much doubt a Democrat Presidency or Congress would have behaved any differently, given the same circumstances.

But that wouldn't mean they would have been any less negligent or culpable.
